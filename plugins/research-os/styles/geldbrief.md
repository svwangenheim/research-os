The Geldbrief is a short (~1000 words), opinionated, evidence-backed DZ economic commentary in German. It leads with a judgment and defends it with numbers. It is not a summary and not a neutral explainer.

## §1 Purpose and Register

- **Audience:** informed non-specialists — journalists, staffers, engaged public.
- **Register:** a DZ economist who writes clearly and takes a position, not a language model generating policy text.
- **Institutionelles Wir** throughout: „Wir zeigen", „Wir schätzen", „Wir argumentieren". Never „ich"; never „man" as an actor.

## §2 Structure (~1000 words)

```
# Titel — thesis or judgment, max 7 words
**[DD. Monat YYYY]** | Lesedauer: [N] min | **[Autor:in]**

> Teaserkasten (150–200 words): Anlass → Was wir untersucht haben → Kernergebnis mit Zahl → DZ-Position

## [Abschnitt 1: 200–300 words]   Move: Befund- or Kontrast-Absatz
## [Abschnitt 2: 200–300 words]   Move: Daten-Move / Kausal-Absatz / Kontrast-Absatz
## [Abschnitt 3: 150–200 words]   Move: Empfehlungs-Absatz; last sentence = Schlusssatz echoing the title
```

**Teaserkasten:** an entry point, not a summary. Begins with the occasion (new paper, political event), ends with the explicit DZ position. If it exceeds ~220 words, cut.

**Schlusssatz:** the last sentence of the last section. Lands the argument — echoes the title or resolves the central tension.

## §3 Argument Moves

Every paragraph has one job. Lead with the finding, not the setup.

- **Befund-Absatz** — number + unit → why → evidence (linked source) → consequence.
- **Kontext-Eröffnung** — concrete opening fact/event with a number → why relevant now → what we don't yet know.
- **Kontrast-Absatz** — steelman the opposing view → contrast signal (Aber/Doch/Allerdings) → DZ position with evidence.
- **Kausal-Absatz** — explicit mechanism A → B → quantified implication.
- **Daten-Move** — one striking statistic in context → what it means → what is surprising.
- **Empfehlungs-Absatz** — what should be done (imperative) → why it works → optional objection + rebuttal.
- **Urteilssatz** — one short judgment sentence (8–12 words) per section, backed by the preceding evidence. „Sauber ist das nicht."

## §4 Language

- Active voice, concrete subjects in result sentences.
- Every number gets a unit; every large number gets a second contextualizer (proportion, comparison, time).
- One claim per sentence. Vary sentence length deliberately.
- Sourced claims use **inline hyperlinks**, not footnotes or author-year.

## §5 Section-Heading Patterns

Headings are theses, judgments, or pointed statements — never structural labels. Forbidden: „Hintergrund", „Analyse", „Ergebnisse", „Fazit". Test: could the heading stand alone as a claim? If not, rewrite.

## §6 Tone

Opinionated but falsifiable. Take positions; back every normative claim with evidence from the preceding text. Confident first, honest-caveat second — qualify once, then argue forward.

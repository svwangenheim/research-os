# Policy Brief — DZ House Style

> standard think-tank policy-brief shape to DZ argument discipline. Calibrate against real

The policy brief is a short (~1200–2000 words), decision-oriented DZ document. It exists to move a specific decision: it states a problem, gives the evidence, and makes concrete recommendations a policymaker can act on. Can be German or English depending on audience.

## §1 Purpose and Register

- **Audience:** policymakers and their advisors who will act on the recommendation.
- **Register:** direct, concrete, recommendation-first. Institutionelles Wir throughout.
- Every paragraph earns its place by advancing the decision — no background for its own sake.

## §2 Structure (~1200–2000 words)

```
# Titel — the recommendation or the stakes, stated plainly
**[Autor:in]** | [DD.MM.YYYY] | Kontakt: [email@dezernatzukunft.org]

## Kernbotschaften / Key Messages   3–5 bullets: the problem, the evidence, the recommendation (with numbers)
## 1. Das Problem        what is at stake, quantified; why a decision is needed now
## 2. Die Evidenz        what the analysis shows — findings with numbers, briefly sourced
## 3. Handlungsoptionen / Empfehlungen   numbered, concrete, each with expected effect and trade-off
## Quellen / Sources
```

**Kernbotschaften:** self-contained. A reader who reads only these bullets knows the problem, the key number, and what DZ recommends.

## §3 Argument Moves

Recommendation-led. Lead with the **Befund-Absatz** (the number that motivates action) and close each option with the **Empfehlungs-Absatz** (imperative → why it works → objection → rebuttal). Use the **Kontrast-Absatz** to pre-empt the strongest objection to the recommendation. Keep the **Kausal-Absatz** short: how the measure produces the intended effect.

## §4 Language

- Active voice, imperative in recommendations.
- Numbers with units and context; the headline number appears in the Kernbotschaften and again in the evidence section, matching exactly.
- One claim per sentence. Short sentences for the recommendation; longer for the mechanism.
- Sourced claims: inline hyperlinks or a short Quellen list — keep sourcing light but real.

### §4.1 Forbidden words and phrases

> Calibrate against the real DZ style guide. Inherit the shared forbidden lists: significance
> inflation, promotional language, throat-clearing openers, and evasive closers
> („bleibt abzuwarten", „die Zeit wird zeigen").

## §5 Section-Heading Patterns

Plain and decision-oriented ("Das Problem", "Handlungsoptionen"), or stated as the claim itself. Never bury the recommendation behind a neutral label.

## §6 Tone

Confident and actionable. Take a clear position, quantify the stakes, and make the recommendation concrete enough to implement. Acknowledge the main trade-off honestly rather than pretending the choice is costless.

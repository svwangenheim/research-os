# European Innovation Scoreboard

## What it covers

The European Innovation Scoreboard measures innovation performance across EU member states and comparator countries using a multi-indicator framework. The 2025 report uses 32 indicators and groups countries into Innovation Leaders, Strong Innovators, Moderate Innovators, and Emerging Innovators.

## Unit of observation

- Country-year for most indicators.
- Indicator-level country performance.
- Composite index values and performance groups.

## Key variables

- Summary Innovation Index.
- Human resources.
- Attractive research systems.
- Digitalisation.
- Finance and support.
- Firm investments.
- Innovators.
- Linkages.
- Intellectual assets.
- Sales and trade impacts.
- Resource productivity and resilience/dependency indicators.

## What it is used to evaluate or demonstrate

In this vault, the EIS is used to locate Germany relative to European innovation leaders. It supports the claim that Germany is a Strong Innovator but not an Innovation Leader, motivating explanation through allocation, commercialization, finance, and sectoral lock-in.

The EIS is also used as a retrieval bridge between national benchmarking and the mechanisms developed elsewhere in the wiki. It can show that Germany underperforms relative to Innovation Leaders, but it cannot by itself explain whether the gap comes from weak commercialization, low start-up dynamism, green-transition bottlenecks, data access, incumbent allocation, or insufficient radical innovation. Those mechanisms are supplied by concept and synthesis notes such as [[30_concepts/innovation-paradox.md]], [[30_concepts/commercialization-gap.md]], [[30_concepts/equity-gap-sme-financing.md]], [[30_concepts/green-innovation-and-transition-policy.md]], and [[90_synthesis/germany-innovation-policy-evidence-map.md]].

## Strengths

- Broad, repeated cross-country benchmark.
- Multi-dimensional rather than purely R&D-based.
- Useful for project framing and international comparison.

## Limitations

- Composite scores depend on indicator choice and weighting.
- Does not explain causal mechanisms.
- Country-level aggregation hides sector and firm-size heterogeneity.
- Does not directly measure federal funding allocation or TRL distribution.

## Known biases

Composite indicators can overweight available and comparable data. Some innovation outcomes, especially deep-tech commercialization and radical firm creation, may be imperfectly captured.

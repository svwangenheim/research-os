# Mannheimer Innovationspanel

## What it covers

The Mannheimer Innovationspanel is a recurring German firm-level innovation survey used in research and policy monitoring. It covers innovation activity, R&D, innovation expenditure, barriers to innovation, public funding, firm characteristics, and related indicators. In this vault it is the canonical dataset note for references to MIP, the German innovation survey, and ZEW-linked firm-level innovation evidence.

## Unit of observation

The usual unit is the firm or enterprise. Specific studies may aggregate by sector, size class, region, innovation status, or time period.

## Key variables

Common variables include:

- innovation activity and innovator status;
- R&D activity and innovation expenditure;
- firm size, sector, age, and other characteristics;
- use of public support or policy instruments;
- barriers to innovation;
- sales from new products or other innovation outputs;
- sometimes information relevant to cooperation, finance, and adoption.

The exact variable set depends on the wave and study. Citation-level use should check the relevant paper or data documentation.

## What it is used to evaluate or demonstrate

MIP is used to demonstrate trends in German firm innovation, SME participation, R&D behavior, barriers, funding use, and sometimes the relationship between policy instruments and firm outcomes. It is also a potential input to causal evaluation when linked with treatment, eligibility, or funding data.

## Strengths

- Central German firm-level innovation evidence source.
- Repeated survey structure supports time comparisons.
- Relevant for SME innovation, R&D, funding, barriers, and policy evaluation.
- Often used by ZEW and policy-facing German innovation studies.

## Limitations

- Survey data depend on response quality, sample design, and variable definitions.
- Some policy effects require linkage to administrative treatment and outcome data.
- Firm-level surveys may miss project-level maturity, TRL, or detailed technology content.
- Citation-level use should verify sample restrictions and wave coverage in the specific study.

## Known biases

Potential biases include non-response, self-reporting, survival bias, and limited observability of informal or non-R&D innovation. Studies must handle firm heterogeneity and selection into public funding carefully.

Based  on  the  knowledge-based  view,  we  conceptualize  the  technology  search  of  high-tech
manufacturing firms into three dimensions, namely search breadth, search depth, and balanced
search, and construct the functional paths of the relationships between the different technology
search  dimensions  and  innovation  quality  of  high-tech  manufacturing  firms.  Grounded  in
absorptive capacity theory, we analyze how such relationships will change with the absorptive
capacity level. Using data from China’s high-tech manufacturing industry from 2009 to 2017, we
construct  a  panel  data  model  to  test  the  moderating  effect  of  absorptive  capacity  on  such  re-
lationships. Our research findings suggest: (i) Technology search breadth and depth each have a
significant,  inverted  U-shaped  relationship  with  innovation  quality,  i.e.,  technology  search
breadth and depth have an optimal point, and innovation quality exhibits an initial increasing and
then decreasing trend. (ii) Balanced search has a significant positive relationship with innovation
quality,  i.e.,  there  is  a  balanced  point  between  technology  search  breadth  and  depth,  and  the
higher  the  balance  point  is,  the  higher  is  innovation  quality.  (iii)Absorptive  capacity  does  not
merely positively moderate the relationship between technology search and innovation quality,
but has different moderating effects under different search levels.

1. Introduction

In the high-tech industry, where technology is rapidly developing, high-tech manufacturing firms can only meet the ever-changing
market demand by continuously innovating and constantly improving the quality of innovation and by enhancing market competi-
tiveness  to  be  formidable  in  competition.  Currently,  facing  a  dynamic  external  competitive  environment,  changing  technological
knowledge of firms, and problems such as the “capability trap”  and “innovator dilemma”  (Duan et al., 2021; Nerkar, 2003; Swift,
2016), many high-tech firms have embarked on the journey of open innovation. In the context of open innovation and rapidly changing
business environments, “closed-door” innovations cannot cope with the era of accelerating product renewal, and firms have to conduct
technology search at various stages of innovation (Dong and Netten, 2017; Escribano et al., 2009; Hameed et al., 2012). Through

their knowledge bases, overcome the limitations of internal technical knowledge, track advanced technologies, and better deal with
problems such as high cost and complexity in the process of technological development (Enkel et al., 2009; Huizingh, 2011; Tan et al.,
2020). Spithoven et al. (2010) proposed from the perspective of innovation input that in open innovation, firms do not rely entirely on
internal R&D but scan the environment to search for technology and knowledge. In the increasingly open and rapidly changing in-
dustrial environment, manufacturing firms need cross-integration of heterogeneous knowledge from different fields and industries to
generate new ideas and technologies through “creative collision”, so external technology search has become an important source and
channel for firm innovation resources (Laursen and Salter, 2014).

In recent years, there is the phenomenon of “quantity over quality” in Chinese firms’ pursuit of innovation. According to national
statistics, China has the world’s second largest expenditures on R&D, behind the US in total expenditures and exceeding the average
level of the 15 countries in the EU. Specifically, China ranks first in the world in total R&D personnel, and in invention patent ap-
plications and authorizations. The number of scientific and technological papers published in China accounted for 26.9 % of the global
total published in 2015–2017, followed by the US with 16.8 %.1 Although the number of innovative achievements of China is in the
world’s leading position, the quality and effectiveness of technological innovations are still far from those of the major developed
countries. The fundamental reason is that the state and firms have been focusing less on innovation quality and more on innovation
quantity.  Since  the  financial  crisis  in  2008,  and  with  the  experience  of  the  recent  Sino-US  trade  war  and  ZTE  incident,  Chinese
manufacturing firms, especially high-tech manufacturing firms, have undergone major transformations in both material and spiritual
aspects.  Innovation  quality  is  the  sum  of  innovation  performance  in  processes,  outputs,  and  economic  benefits  (Haner,  2002).
Currently, most of China’s manufacturing industries are largely at the bottom of the industrial value chain due to the lack of core
technologies (Yuan and Li, 2021), e.g. semiconductor, pharmaceutical, electronics, frontier equipment and other manufacturing in-
dustries.  Low-quality  innovation  leads  to  low  economic  performance.  Only  high-quality  innovation  with  heterogeneity  can  yield
competitive advantage for firms and improve their performance (Hirshleifer et al., 2013; Tan et al., 2020). Existing empirical studies
have confirmed the important role of innovation quality in firm development.

A firm’s innovation quality lies in the industrialization of innovative achievements, e.g., high level patents, scientific and tech-
nological breakthroughs, and R&D of new products, which generates profits and lead to continuous improvement of core competency,
not just the number of patent authorizations, number of scientific and technological papers published, or number of new-product
developments. According to the 2018 China Patent Investigation Report released by the State Intellectual Property Office in January
2019, the industrialization rate of effective invention patents in 2018 was only 32.3 %, down from 36.7 % in 2016 and 36.2 % in 2017.
Therefore, firms in the development process, especially high-tech manufacturing firms, urgently need to improve the efficiency and
quality of their innovation efforts after investing large amounts of R&D resources. However, the quality of innovation is inevitably
influenced by the new technical knowledge that R&D personnel have searched before proposing ideas. If the technology used to
develop  a  product  is  outdated  or  is  not  the  future  development  direction  of  the  market,  it  is  difficult  to  realize  the  profit  and
commercialization of the new product in the market, even if there are many patent applications. Through conscientious searches for
heterogeneous knowledge elements, high-tech firms realize the rapid transfer of high-quality information and tacit knowledge (Duan
et al., 2021; Gay, 2014), integrate advanced technical knowledge into their R&D processes, create new combinations of technology and
knowledge, and achieve integrated innovation. Then, the new products developed tend to be more easily recognized by the market and
customers, and the innovation efficiency and core competency of the firms will also be improved (Bolívar-Ramos et al., 2012; Leiponen
and Helfat, 2010; Tan et al., 2020), thereby improving their innovation quality.

The improvement of innovation quality of high-tech manufacturing firms cannot be separable from their searches for technical
knowledge, whether the goal of high-quality innovation can be achieved after acquiring the necessary technical knowledge is closely
related to their absorptive capacity (Tamayo-Torres et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2020). The new technological knowledge acquired by
firms through technology search can produce innovative output through a series of internal processes in which absorptive capacity
plays an important role in helping them to generate high-quality innovation output (Fosfuri and Trib´o, 2008; Petti et al., 2019).
Absorptive capacity is the dynamic ability of the innovation subject to identify, acquire, digest and utilize valuable external knowledge
and information through a series of learning processes (Lane et al., 2006; Martinkenaite and Breunig, 2016; Zahra and George, 2002).
The reason is that absorptive capacity can reduce the potential threats and risks in the process of technology search, and the firm that
has high absorptive capacity can identify high-quality knowledge elements beyond the technological boundary, bringing high-quality
innovative output to the firm (Huang and Rice, 2009; Mir et al., 2016). Researchers have criticized that existing research places too
great an emphasis on the impact of external contact on firm innovation, ignoring the series of processes through which firms digest and
transform knowledge. Differences in firms’  ability to absorb external technical knowledge lead to uneven innovation performance
(Cohen and Caner, 2016; Giuliani and Bell, 2005), with firms having low absorptive capacity stagnating at low levels of innovation
quality.

In view of the globalization of competition, and an increasingly complex and dynamic market environment, how to improve the
innovation quality of China’s high-tech manufacturing firms and maintain their core competency are critical management issues to
address. In this study, seeking to deepen and enrich search and innovation theories, we use data from China’s high-tech manufacturing
industry as a sample to explore the relationship between the technology search behaviour of high-tech manufacturing firms and their
quality of innovation. Grounded in absorptive capacity theory, we explore the moderating effect of absorptive capacity to expand the

2. Literature review and research hypotheses

2.1. Related concepts and dimensions

2.1.1. Technology search

The concept of technology search stems from the organization search theory proposed by Winter and Nelson (1982). Researchers
hold different views of technical search based on different perspectives. However, there is agreement among the different views that
technology search is a behavioral process based on a technical perspective to solve technical problems or find new opportunities for an
uncertain future by sourcing innovative knowledge inside and outside an organization to achieve innovation goals. Examples include
Cruz-Gonz´alez et al. (2015); Lichtenthaler (2011); Rohrbeck (2010); Tan et al. (2020) etc.

Existing research on the dimensions of technology search is based on different perspectives, including such single dimensions as the
time  dimension  (Nerkar,  2003),  spatial  dimension  (Ahuja  and  Katila,  2004),  organizational  dimension  (Miller  et  al.,  2007),  and
cognitive  dimension  (Rosenkopf  and  Almeida,  2003).  Researchers  have  also  discussed  the  synergistic  effect  of  two-dimensional
technology search, e.g., Katila (2002) and Phene et al. (2006). In addition, based on the extent to which firms use existing tech-
nical knowledge and the scope of technical knowledge sources, Guan and Liu (2016); Li et al. (2008) and Katila and Ahuja (2002)
classified technology search into search depth and search breath, which have been widely recognized in academia.

On the basis of existing research, combined with the current innovation status of high-tech manufacturing firms in China and with
reference  to  Ahuja  and  Katila  (2004);  Cao  et  al.  (2009);  Laursen  and  Salter  (2006)  and  Guan  and  Liu  (2016),  we  conceptualize
technology search in the context of high-tech manufacturing into three dimensions, namely search breadth, search depth, and balanced
search (see Fig. 1). Technology search width is the scope of searching for new technology knowledge, technology search depth is the
extent to which firms reuse existing technology knowledge, and balanced technology search means that firms maintain a relative
balance between search depth and search width in the process of technology search (Ahuja and Katila, 2001; Cao et al., 2009; Guan and
Liu, 2016; Laursen and Salter, 2006). The reasons for such a classification system are as follows: First, the breadth and depth di-
mensions of technology search are helpful for high-tech manufacturing firms to formulate an effective search strategy for technical
knowledge  under  the  constraint  of  limited  organizational  resources  such  that  they  have  a  definite  target,  and  can  improve  their
innovation efficiency and quality. Second, under the constraint of limited resources, expanding the breadth of technology search is
conducive to increasing firms’ heterogeneous knowledge and enriching their knowledge bases. In addition, absorbing and digesting
the heterogeneous knowledge is conducive to eliminating firms’ knowledge blind spots, enabling them to better cope with environ-
mental turbulence (Laursen and Salter, 2006). Third, increasing the depth of technology search can help firms gain a deeper under-
standing of some knowledge so that they can leverage the heterogeneous knowledge to improve their innovation ability (Laursen and
Salter, 2006). Fourth, in practice, a single search strategy will not be able to adapt to the rapidly changing market environment and
shifting strategic focus, while multiple search strategies will inevitably give rise to resource competition under the constraint of limited
firm resources, thus affecting the efficiency and quality of innovation (Gatti et al., 2015). There is a tension between search breadth
and depth under the constraint of organizational resources and self-reinforcing mechanisms, which drives the search to an unbalanced
state (Gupta et al., 2006), while firms’ innovation focusing on search balance is more robust (Lee, 2015).

digest, and use the value of external new information for commercial purposes. Later, researchers, based on dynamic capability theory
and the process perspective, supplemented and complemented the discussion (e.g. Lane et al., 2006; Zahra and George, 2002; Gkypali

Although there are different classifications of absorptive capacity in the literature, they are mainly based on the three-dimensional

external knowledge proposed by Zahra and George (2002). In most of the existing empirical studies on absorptive capacity, researchers
generally agree with the four-dimensional model of Zahra and George (2002).

At present, research on absorptive capacity is well developed, and researchers have come to a consensus that absorptive capacity
has process attributes and dynamic ability attributes. In general, the absorptive capacity of a firm is the process of organizational
learning and knowledge management, including identification and acquisition of technical knowledge, application and integration of
knowledge elements, and commercialization of new technical knowledge.

2.1.3. Innovation quality

Prior studies on innovation quality are mainly focus on the innovation and quality perspectives (Haner, 2002; Lahiri, 2010; Mu
et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2018; Duan et al., 2021). The concept is developed on the basis of innovation theory and quality management
theory. Based on innovation theory, research from the perspective of innovation holds that innovation quality is the degree of influence
of innovation output, embracing innovation ability and innovation performance. Haner (2002) first proposed the conceptual frame-
work of innovation quality, arguing that innovation quality is the sum of innovation performance in various fields and has special
dynamics that can be defined by variables such as quantity, performance, efficiency, reliability, time, cost, and value to customers.
Lahiri (2010) argued that innovation quality is the impact of innovation output and an important embodiment of innovation ability.
Researchers have advocated that innovation quality be measured by using the authorization rate, number of times cited, and length of
a patent. Based on quality management theory, innovation quality is regarded as the embodiment of the innovation process quality and
the degree to which innovation results meet the market demand, which is different from quality innovation. Haner (2002) proposed
that innovation quality is the quality of all the innovation results, including product or service quality, process quality, and firm
quality.

High-tech manufacturing  firms produce high-tech  products  (Raab and  Kotamraju, 2006), of  which  knowledge innovation and
product innovation are their most prominent features. Based on the studies of Haner (2002), Mu et al., 2017; Palm et al., 2016; Wang
et al., 2018; Tseng and Wu, 2007) etc., We regard innovation quality as the adaptability of the market and the satisfaction of the
interests of all parties to the results of innovative products such as new products or services, new processes or technologies, and new
management models created by firms using various innovative resources, as a comprehensive manifestation of the ability, perfor-
mance, efficiency, and value of corporate innovation activities in terms of process and results, as a standard for measuring and judging
the level and quality of firm innovation output.

2.2. Technology search and innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing firms

Current academic research on technology search and innovation mainly discusses the relationship between technology search, and
innovation capabilities and innovation performance. Some scholars, such as Leiponen and Helfat (2010), and Phelps (2010), have
found  that  external  technology  search  has  positive  effects  on  firms’  innovation  capabilities  and  innovation  performance.  The
continuous enhancement of search contributes to the rapid transfer of high-quality information and tacit knowledge, and improves the
efficiency of firm innovation (Gay, 2014). Some scholars have also found that technology search has a negative effect on innovation
performance, e.g., Knudsen and Mortensen (2011) proposed that increasing the openness of external knowledge sources at the project
level would have a negative impact on new product innovation performance. Although firms can search for technical knowledge in
depth, they can easily fall into “core rigidity” and a “capability trap”, hindering and inhibiting firm innovation (Swift, 2016). With the
deepening of research, scholars have found that the relationship between technology search and innovation performance is not a
simple linear relationship. For example, (Laursen and Salter, 2006), and Wu (2013) took British and Taiwanese firms in China as
samples, respectively, and detected an inverted U-shaped relationship between technology search and innovation performance.

To summarize, there are many research achievements relating to firm technology search in the field of innovation, but the research
still needs to be further deepened. First, existing studies focus on the relationship between technology search, and innovation per-
formance and innovation capabilities. Few studies take innovation quality as a dependent variable to explore the relationship between
technology search and innovation quality. Second, there are still differences in existing findings, mainly with regard to the linear or
nonlinear relationship between technology search, and innovation performance and innovation capabilities. Third, in terms of the
impact of different search dimensions on innovation performance, although scholars have abandoned the single dimension in recent
years  and  studied  the  impact  of  multi-dimensional  search  on  innovation,  few  have  considered  the  impact  of  multi-dimensional
balanced search on innovation. In addition, most existing studies only focus on the direct impact of technology search on innova-
tion, paying scant attention to the contingency factors that affect the relationship between technology search and innovation.

At present, in the context of China’s economic and social transition, searching for and utilizing external technical knowledge to
improve the efficiency and quality of technological innovation is an inevitable choice for Chinese firms to integrate into an open
innovation system (Chen et al., 2011; Duan et al., 2021). Innovation quality is an important index to measure the level and height of
innovation,  and  the  integration  of  innovation  performance  in  each  field.  Innovation  quality  and  innovation  performance  are

the relationship between technology search and innovation quality. In view of past studies, we divide the technology search dimension

quality.

2.2.1. Search breadth and innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing firms

Knowledge sources outside the firm play an important role in acquiring new knowledge, inspiring new thinking, and generating
new ideas; technology search describes the scope to which a firm searches for new technology knowledge. Different types of knowledge
provide firms with a more diversified knowledge base, which is beneficial for firms to carry out exploratory innovation activities
through a unique combination of knowledge (Choi et al., 2018; Subramaniam and Youndt, 2005). First, increasing the breadth of
search is conducive to expanding the sources and scope of external innovation resources acquired by firms and will increase the
heterogeneous knowledge acquired by firms from external search. It is helpful for firms to increase opportunities for the reorganization
and application of existing technologies, and generate diversified selection effects (Ahuja and Katila, 2004). Firms can effectively
reduce  the  risk  and  uncertainty  of  innovation,  improve  the  probability  of  innovation  success  and  promote  the  improvement  of
innovation quality by reorganizing, absorbing and utilizing the heterogeneous knowledge obtained from search (Cohen and Levinthal,
1990; Fleming and Sorenson, 2004). Second, the firm’s extensive technology search can select valuable high-quality innovation el-
ements from many heterogeneous resources, effectively breaking the resource bottleneck of high-tech manufacturing firms (Sampson,
2007). The combination of these key heterogeneous resources will also bring new ideas to firms, increase the potential for firms to
explore new knowledge and novel solutions (Chiang and Hung, 2010; Choi et al., 2018), and generate more high-quality innovative
outcomes. Finally, abundant heterogeneous resources can not only meet the demand of innovation but also pose threats and challenges
to the firms’ current cognitive paradigm, thereby increasing the possibility of cooperation between firms and external heterogeneous
knowledge  sources,  promoting  firms  to  search,  absorb  and  utilize  knowledge,  improving  organizational  learning  efficiency  and
stimulating the improvement of the quality of innovation (Fleming and Sorenson, 2004).

However, the breadth of firm technology search is not as wide as possible. With the increase of search breadth, the financial and
cognitive costs of search will also increase, thus decreasing the marginal benefit to firms from integrating and utilizing knowledge
(Fleming and Sorenson, 2004). Firms have limited ability to learn and utilize the new technical knowledge they have found, which will
increase the cost of knowledge reconstruction and may lead to knowledge redundancy (Gonz´alez-Moreno et al., 2019; Wu, 2013). In
particular, the increasing complexity of SMEs’ extensive search for diverse knowledge will bring significant challenge to the innovation
activities of firms (Leiponen and Helfat, 2010). Second, the advanced technical knowledge found only represents the potential for
future innovation; its implementation requires much investment (Kotha et al., 2013). In a high-tech industry with rapid technological
upgrading, innovation risks are relatively high. Due to the limitations of scale and financial strength, excessive search may cause firms
to take higher risks and squeeze innovation resources, which is not conducive to firm innovation.

H1. Technology search breadth has an inverse U-shaped relationship with innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing firms. In
other words, with an increase in technology search breadth, the quality of firm innovation is on the rise; when technology search
breadth exceeds the inflection point level, the quality of firm innovation begins to decline.

2.2.2. Search depth and innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing firms

In the process of technology search, firms, especially SMEs, are more inclined to increase their search depth due to limitations on
their own resources (Guo et al., 2015). Search depth reflects the extent to which a firm reuses existing knowledge elements to identify
new opportunities. First, the enhancement of search depth helps maintain the relevance of external sources of knowledge to the firm
(Lavie, 2007). Searching in relatively familiar fields is convenient to form and accumulate valuable tacit knowledge, summarize its
utilization rules and improve its potential defects, produce newly technical knowledge that is difficult to imitate, form the technical
barriers of the firm itself, and promote innovation quality. With the continuous deepening of search, firms’ knowledge discrimination
ability is enhanced, utilization of erroneous knowledge resources is avoided, the reliability of external knowledge search is improved,
and the innovation efficiency and success rate of firms are improved (Gay, 2014). Second, in-depth search in familiar fields reduces the
diversity of knowledge, reduces the complexity of management, and helps firms to quickly match the external knowledge they find
with their own knowledge and reduce the risk and cost of innovation (Kaplan and Vakili, 2015). In-depth search of existing knowledge
can effectively promote communication and interaction between firms and the outside world and knowledge sharing, optimize the
allocation of innovation resources among subjects to produce a “synergy effect”, and gradually establish cooperative practices and tacit
understanding, thus leading to higher quality innovation outcomes (Dhanaraj et al., 2004).

Despite  the  reliability  and  cost  advantages  of  deep  search,  search  depth  may  not  be  as  deep  as  possible  based  on  the  entire
innovation process. In a familiar field, the knowledge obtained by deep search lacks inspiration and diversity, and a single knowledge
situation will generally reduce the sensitivity of firms to changes in the external environment (Duan et al., 2021; Laursen and Salter,
2014). It is not possible to quickly perceive customer preferences, and it is difficult to track the frontier of technology in time; firms are
thus unable to effectively cope with dynamic competition, form a mindset and overcome organizational inertia, which causes firms to
experience a “lock-in” effect, fall into the capability trap, and transform core competitiveness into core rigidity (Swift, 2016), thus
hindering the improvement of firm innovation quality. Second, from the law of diminishing marginal utility, it can be seen that firms
continue to strengthen the depth of utilizing the same knowledge, and their incremental innovation revenue is decreasing (Choi et al.,
2018; Fu, 2012). Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes the following research hypothesis:

H2. Technology search depth has an inverse U-shaped relationship with innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing firms. In

2.2.3. Balanced search and innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing firms

Due to the influence of organizational resources and organizational culture, there is a certain tension and contradiction between the
depth and breadth of technology search (March, 1991). The breadth and depth of technology search make competitive demands on
organizational resources, and firms are faced with the problem of resource allocation and balance when choosing a search strategy. In a
dynamic and complex market environment, firms not only need to expand their search breadth to acquire diversified and heteroge-
neous technical knowledge to adapt to the changing environment but also need to deepen the search depth of existing technical
knowledge to maintain competitive advantage and market share. Due to uncertainty of the environment and limited organizational
resources, excessive emphasis on search breadth, although it can update the knowledge base of the firm, will lead to the results of
“suboptimal balance”  and “path dependence”; excessive emphasis on search depth can dig deep into existing technologies, but it
cannot quickly adapt to changes in the environment and is prone to fall into “core rigidity” and “capability trap”, thus hindering and
inhibiting firm innovation (Swift, 2016). Therefore, how to coordinate the tension and contradiction between the breadth and depth of
technology search is a problem that cannot be ignored when high-tech manufacturing firms implement a search strategy.

This paper refers to the studies of Cao et al. (2009) and Gupta et al. (2006) to introduce the balanced search dimension. Balanced
search is a relatively balanced state between search breadth and depth intended to reduce search risks and maximize search benefits
when a firm conducts the strategic behavior of technology search. On the one hand, in-depth search is carried out in familiar fields,
which allows firms to explore some specialized knowledge features and knowledge correlation rules, facilitate a deep understanding of
relevant knowledge, and develop combination products with novel technologies and functions to meet market demands to improve the
efficiency of innovation. However, by giving too much attention to search depth and ignoring its breadth, it is easy to form a certain
cognitive inertia and let firms tilt toward the familiar environment (Beckman et al., 2004), thereby limiting firms’ ability to absorb
external  knowledge  and  hindering  their  improvement  of  innovation  quality.  It  is  also  difficult  to  adapt  to  the  presently  popular
“crossover” thinking, which will expose firms to the risk of knowledge obsolescence. On the other hand, if a firm focuses too much
attention on search breadth but ignores search depth, it will bear a higher cost and risk. After all, not all the knowledge found can be
immediately applied to current innovation activities, so it is easy to experience difficulty in obtaining timely returns. Therefore, to
avoid the above extreme situation, firms need to focus on the duality of technology search, especially high-tech manufacturing firms
with high investment in research and development and high risk, and they need more balanced support for both to reduce search risk
and maximize search benefit. Based on these needs, this paper proposes the following research hypothesis:

H3. Balanced search positively impacts on innovation quality of high-tech manufacturing firms. In other words, the more the breadth
and depth of technology search tend to be balanced, the better the firm innovation quality is.

2.3. The moderating effect of absorptive capacity

Absorptive capacity is the ability to internalize and apply external knowledge; it is an indispensable contingency factor in studying
the  relationship  between  external  technology  search  and  innovation.  Current  considerations  for  innovation  contingency  factors
include existing research mostly on external factors, such as environmental turbulence and external networks, and also include the
internal factors of the firm, such as internal resource constraints and the knowledge base of the firm itself. However, the firm’s ability
to absorb the acquired technical knowledge after the search is not enough. In fact, for high-tech manufacturing firms, the goal of
improving innovation ability and innovation quality can only be achieved by fully integrating and applying diversified new knowledge
elements acquired through technology search into internal knowledge under the role of their own absorptive capacity.

The existing research results show that there are three main paths for absorptive capacity to influence firm innovation. First, it
directly influences the firm innovation (Kostopoulos et al., 2011). Second, it has a mediating effect on firm innovation (Gkypali et al.,
2018; Lavie et al., 2010). Third, it has a moderating effect on firm innovation (Patel et al., 2015; Zobel, 2017). Based on the path
constructed in the previous section for relationships between different technology search dimensions and the innovation quality of
high-tech manufacturing firms, the absorptive capacity theory is introduced to analyze how the relationship between the technology
search and innovation quality of high-tech manufacturing firms will change under different absorptive capacity levels. The reasons lie
in the following two aspects:

First, existing studies on contingency factors of firm innovation mainly focus on external factors of firms, and there are few studies
on the relationship between firms’ own absorptive capacity and innovation. However, the innovation quality of firms have a great
relationship with their own absorptive capacity.

Second, current research on the moderating effect of absorptive capacity, although there are many research results, mainly dis-
cusses its moderating effect on firm innovation performance (Wang and Han, 2011) and new product innovation (Zobel, 2017); few
studies have involved the moderating effect on firm innovation quality. Firms conduct technology search behaviors for innovation and
on whether the quality of their innovation results will be moderated by the firm’s own absorptive capacity (Petti et al., 2019). There are
no studies on this topic at present. Therefore, for this paper, after analyzing the relationship between different dimensions of tech-
nology search and innovation quality of high-tech manufacturing firms, it is necessary to introduce absorptive capacity to analyze its
moderating effect.

The  key  to  achieving  improvement  in  high-tech  manufacturing  firms  innovation  quality  is  not  only  to  acquire  heterogeneous
knowledge from external  sources  through technology  search  but also to have  the  ability to integrate and  apply such  knowledge.

Technology search provides potential possibilities to create new knowledge and products for a firm. How to internalize the searched
external knowledge into their own technology, innovate and form a core competitive advantage, and develop absorptive capacity is a
key factor (Enkel and Gassmann, 2010; Lu et al., 2016; Petti et al., 2019). Rothaermel and Alexandre (2009) found that the differences
in firm’s absorptive capacity lead to significant differences in the breadth and depth of external technology search.

The strategic behavior of increasing firm search breadth is to expand the scope of technology search and search for heterogeneous
knowledge elements from suppliers, customers, competitors, partners, intermediaries, universities and scientific research institutions.
However, what is searched with a wider scope is a diversified new knowledge element that has relevance differing from that of the
firm’s previous knowledge. How to transform diverse new knowledge elements into ones that can be used by the firm and applied to
the development of products, services or technologies is the key to the success and efficiency of firm innovation (Leal-Rodríguez et al.,
2014; Wang et al., 2020). Firms with high absorptive capacity  can effectively manage diversified and heterogeneous knowledge,
identify the “blank space”  between low-correlation knowledge, explore innovation opportunities, and improve level of innovation
quality. Existing studies have shown that the innovative output of firms that continue to invest in their absorptive capacity is more
consistent with the demands of different markets (Gkypali et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2020). Thus, the stronger the absorptive capacity of
firms, the greater is the degree of diversified heterogeneity knowledge marketization and the greater the quality of their innovation
output is. Therefore, this paper proposes the following research hypothesis:

H4a. Absorptive capacity positively moderates the relationship between search breadth and innovation quality. In other words, the
inflection point of the inverted U-shaped curve relating technology search breadth with innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing
firms with high absorptive capacity moves to the right compared with that of firms with low absorptive capacity.

The strategic behavior of firms to strengthen their search depth is to conduct a deeper search for existing technical knowledge to
achieve technical renewal and to upgrade products and services through knowledge reorganization (Enkel and Heil, 2014; Petti et al.,
2019; Wang et al., 2020). In-depth technology search strengthens firm’s understanding of existing knowledge, improves the utilization
efficiency of similar knowledge elements, and reduces the cost and risk of searching. A firm with low absorptive capacity has a low
degree of in-depth integration and effective transformation of the searched knowledge elements, and the efficiency and quality of
innovation are low. A firm with high absorptive capacity can find opportunities for the integration of heterogeneous knowledge el-
ements and realize synergies of knowledge to carry out product improvement and new product development in a more targeted way. At
this time, strengthening search depth can promote the transformation of high value and high-quality knowledge and promote the
integration of new knowledge and innovation activities to improve the innovation quality. Thus, firms with strong absorptive capacity
are better at reorganizing and changing old knowledge, which can better promote the relationship between search depth and inno-
vation quality. Therefore, this paper proposes the following research hypothesis:

H4b. Absorptive capacity positively moderates the relationship between search depth and innovation quality. In other words, the
inflection point of the inverted U-shaped curve relating technology search depth to innovation quality in high-tech manufacturing
firms with high absorptive capacity moves to the right compared with that of firms with low absorptive capacity.

Balanced search is also a strategic behavior of technology search, which aims to maintain a relative balance between search depth
and breadth. In the process of firm technology search, increasing the search distance along any dimension may bring both positive and
negative effects. The moderating effect of absorptive capacity is as follows: First, the identification and acquisition ability of absorptive
capacity can not only increase the breadth and depth of technology search and strengthen the potential connection among knowledge
but can also shorten the development cycle of new products and accelerate the speed of new products entering the market, thus
improving the quality of innovation (Petti et al., 2019; Vasudeva and Anand, 2011). Second, the digestion ability of absorptive capacity
can connect new technical knowledge with owned technical knowledge and accelerate the speed of high-quality innovation outcomes.
Moreover, digesting external knowledge can avoid repeated searching. Third, the transformation ability of absorptive capacity can

3. Methods

3.1. Data

The source of data for this study is from the China Statistical Yearbook on China’s High Technology Industry, which has been
collected and published by the National Bureau of Statistics. The Yearbook contains the production and operation status, research and
development activities, new product development and sales, and patents of all high-tech manufacturing firms in China. The Statistical
data of all high-tech firms are classified by industry sector, region (province), ownership, and scale of firms. This paper selects industry
data as a research sample. The reasons are as follows: First, The Yearbook includes all high-tech firms in China. This paper selects the
data of high-tech manufacturing firms summarized by industry sector in the Yearbook to explore the relationship between technology
search behavior and innovation quality of firms of the same type-all firms belonging to the same industry (Rosenkopf and Nerkar,
2001; Tan et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2019). Second, it is the only way we can obtain comprehensive and complete high-tech firm data, and
the data is true and reliable due to the legal obligation of true reporting.

According to the current industrial classification of China’s national economy, high-tech manufacturing mainly includes six major
categories, namely pharmaceutical manufacturing, aerospace and equipment manufacturing, electronic and communication equip-
ment manufacturing, computer  and office equipment manufacturing, medical equipment and instrumentation manufacturing and
information chemical manufacturing. There is a lack of data on R&D activities in China Statistical Yearbook on China’s High Tech-
nology Industry before 2009, and the information chemicals manufacturing industry only has data for 2015 and 2016. To fit the data
availability to the balanced panel, this paper selects the panel data of five high-tech manufacturing industries from 2009 to 2017 for
empirical analysis. (Considering the hysteresis of the influence of variables, the dependent variable innovation quality is the data from
2010 to 2017, while the other variables are the data from 2009 to 2016.) We use STATA 15.1 to process and analyze the sample data.

3.2. Measures

3.2.1. Dependent variable

Existing studies mainly measure the quality of innovation by adopting the first four places of patent IPC classification number,
patent citation rate and citation times, patent authorization rate and patent length, etc. (Bloom and Van, 2002; Lahiri, 2010; Lerner,
1994). Comino and Graziano (2015) holds that innovation quality is an important index to measure innovation level and height, and
the number of patents will affect innovation quality. The types of patents in China are divided into invention patents, utility model
patents and design patents. In particular, invention patents refer to new technical solutions for products, methods or improvements,
which are regarded as representative of high-quality innovation output in China (Duan et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2019). Based on the
availability of data and the reliability of research results, this paper refers to the study of Duan et al. (2021) and Yu et al. (2019), and
the innovation quality is measured by the ratio of the granted number of invention patents to the number of patent applications.

3.2.2. Independent variables

Current studies mostly use patent citation data and questionnaires to measure technical search variables, but patent citation in
developing countries does not objectively reflect the level and direction of search (Laursen and Salter, 2006). Based on the availability
of  data  and  the  reliability  of  research  results,  this  paper  selects  high-tech  manufacturing  data  from  official  statistics  to  measure
technology search. There is an indicator of technology expenditure in China Statistical Yearbook on China’s High Technology Industry.
Technology expenditure includes four sub-indicators, namely technology introduction expenditure (TIE), purchase domestic tech-
nology expenditure (PDTE), technology digestion expense (TDE), and technological transformation expenditure (TTE). Total tech-
nology expenditure is the sum of these four sub-indicators.

The technology search breadth(Sb) reflects the extent of searching new knowledge or technology and the channels of its source. The
breadth of search can be replaced by “search scope” (Choi et al., 2018; Katila and Ahuja, 2002). In terms of geospatial dimension, the
scope of new knowledge and new technology sources is divided into local (domestic) and non-local (foreign) (Laursen, 2012)—that is,
the purchase of new  technical knowledge from within the country or the introduction of new  technical knowledge from abroad.
Therefore, this paper takes the proportion of the sum of purchasing domestic technology and importing technology from abroad costs
accounting for total technical expenditure as the proxy variable to measure the technology search breadth of high-tech manufacturing
firms. Its calculation formula is Sb = (TIE + PDTE)/ Total technology expenditure.

JournalofEngineeringandTechnologyManagement62(2021)1016568Y. Duan et al.

The technology search depth(Sd) reflects the degree of integrating and utilizing the existing knowledge. Laursen and Salter (2006)
believe that after the absorption and digestion of searched heterogeneous technical knowledge, a deeper understanding of information
correlations will result: the greater the degrees of digestion, absorption and transformation are, the more flexible their application will
be, and the higher the efficiency of problem solving will be (Cruz-Gonz´alez et al., 2015). Therefore, this paper takes the proportion of
the sum of digesting and absorbing technical knowledge costs accounting for total technical expenditure as the proxy variable to
measure the technology search breadth of high-tech manufacturing firm. Its calculation formula is Sd = (TDE + TTE)/ Total tech-
nology expenditure.

For balanced search(BS), this paper refers to the studies of Cao et al. (2009) and Wong et al. (2013) to calculate the balance
dimension; using the absolute value of search breadth and depth, the smaller the result, the more balanced it is. Its calculation formula
is BS=|Sb-Sd|.

3.2.3. Moderating variables

There are two methods to measure absorptive capacity in existing studies, which mainly design the scale for questionnaire survey
and use proxy variables. In this paper, based on the availability and reliability of data and following Cohen and Levinthal (1990); Xie
and Li (2018) and Volberda et al. (2010) absorptive capacity was measured by the ratio of R&D expenditure to sales revenue in the
year.

3.2.4. Control variables

Based on the existing research results, this paper takes variables such as the average size of industry, R&D expenditure intensity,
R&D manpower input intensity, market competition degree and export intensity that may affect the innovation quality results of high-
tech manufacturing firms as control variables.

(1)  Average size of industry. In existing studies, scale has always been considered to have an impact on innovation, but the average
size of the industry is different from that of a firm. Firms with a large industry average size have more advantages in terms of
manpower, capital and risk resistance and thus have more convenient conditions for R&D activities (Laursen and Salter, 2006).
Segarra-O˜na et al. (2011) found a positive relationship between average size of industry and innovation performance of a firm
through empirical analysis. Therefore, this paper takes the average size of industry as the control variable and measures the
ratio of the total number of employees in different industries to the number of firms in high-tech manufacturing.

(2)  Intensity of R&D expenditure investment. R&D expenditure is the main source of innovation funds, which provide the basis and
possibility for technological innovation and directly affect the capability and performance of technological innovation. Existing
studies show that investment in R&D funds is conducive to the creation of high value-added output, which is significantly
positively with innovation performance (Wang et al., 2010). Therefore, according to existing studies, this paper takes the in-
tensity of R&D expenditure investment as the control variable and measures the proportion of R&D expenditure in the main
business income of the year.

(3)  Intensity of R&D human capital investment. In addition to R&D expenditure investment, R&D investment also includes R&D
human capital investment. R&D human capital effect will directly affect innovation performance. Existing studies show that
R&D personnel investment have significantly positively impact on innovation performance (Artz et al., 2010). Therefore, based
on existing studies, this paper takes the intensity of R&D human capital investment as the control variable and chooses the
proportion of total time equivalent of R&D personnel and total number of employees to represent that intensity.

(4)  Degree of market competition. The degree of cooperation and competition within the industry is closely related to the number of
firms. The increase in the number of firms within the industry and the improvement of market competition will strengthen the
awareness of crisis, promote the diffusion of knowledge and technology spillover and have an important impact on innovation
performance (Guan and Chen, 2010). Therefore, this paper takes the degree of market competition within the industry as a
control variable and uses the number of high-tech manufacturing firms to reflect the degree of market competition.

4.2. Unit root test and stationarity analysis

Since panel data is composed of time series data and cross-section data, a unit root test should be conducted on the stationarity of
the variable data before regression to prevent model pseudo regression. The common unit root test methods mainly include LLC, ADF,
PP, HT, IPS, HADRI etc. Based on the applicability of different methods, we applied LLC, HT and IPS to test the data. Considering that
the test results of different methods are not the same, we consider that as long as two or more methods are considered stable among the
three methods, the data are considered stable. Table 5 reports the results.

4.3. Analysis of regression results

The sample data selected in this paper are panel data. Before conducting the regression analysis, it is necessary to determine
whether the regression equation is a traditional OLS model, fixed-effect model or random-effect model. To this end, we performed the
Hausman test and Breusch-Pagan test. The Hausman test results accept the null hypothesis (p = 0.838), while the Breusch-Pagan test
results reject the null hypothesis (p = 0.000). Therefore, it is more appropriate to use the random-effect model.

5. Discussion and conclusion

5.1. Conclusion

Concerning the influence of technology search behavior on firm innovation, some scholars believe that there are positive effects
(Leiponen and Helfat, 2010; Phelps, 2010), and some results show negative effects (Knudsen and Mortensen, 2011; Swift, 2016). Some
scholars also propose that there is a nonlinear relationship between technology search and innovation (Laursen and Salter, 2006; Wu,
2013). This paper divides technology search into three dimensions: search breadth, depth and balanced search. Combined with the
above  viewpoints  and  exploring  the  benefits  and  costs  of  different  search  dimensions,  according  to  the  dynamic  change  of  the

JournalofEngineeringandTechnologyManagement62(2021)10165616Y. Duan et al.

relationship, we propose the hypotheses of an inverted U-shaped relationship between the technology search breadth, depth and
innovation quality of high-tech manufacturing firms as well as the hypothesis of a linear relationship between balanced search and
innovation quality of high-tech manufacturing firms.

This paper takes the data of China’s high-tech manufacturing industry from 2009 to 2017 as the sample, and the research con-
clusions are as follows: First, The relationship between technology search width and innovation quality, technology search depth and
innovation quality is inverted U-shape. After introducing absorptive capacity as a moderating variable, we find that the inflection point
shifts to the upper right, which confirms that the higher the absorptive capacity of high-tech manufacturing firms, the more technical
search breadth or technical search breadth is conducive to the improvement of firm innovation quality. The moderating effect of
absorptive capacity is closely related to the level of breadth or depth of technology search in high-tech manufacturing firms. At a lower
level of technology search breadth or technology search depth, absorptive capacity negatively moderates the relationship between
them. When the extent of technology search breadth or technology search depth is high, absorptive capacity positively moderates the
relationship between them. Second, the balance search is positively with the innovation quality of high-tech manufacturing firms, and
high absorptive capacity increases the slope of the innovation quality curve and makes it steeper. In other words, the higher a firm’s
absorptive capacity level is, the faster the balanced search will make the innovation quality increase

5.2. Theoretical contributions and management implications

We make three theoretical contributions in this study as follows: First, it further enriches the research of innovation management.
Previous researches mainly focus on firm innovation activities from the perspective of quantity (Fleming and Sorenson, 2004), while
few focus on innovation quality (Duan et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2019). Based on the knowledge base view, we construct the relationship
model of technology search width, technology search depth, balance search and innovation quality, and verify the inverted U-shaped
relationship between width and depth of technology search and innovation quality, and the linear relationship between balance search
and innovation quality. Our conclusion is consistent with previous research (Laursen and Salter, 2006; Wu, 2013), which further
supports the demonstration of the relationship between technology search and innovation quality.

Second, in terms of empirical methods, this paper provides more rigorous demonstration operation. For the demonstration of the
inverted U-shaped relationship, previous studies mainly focused on the quadratic coefficient and significance of the principal variable.
In addition, this paper verified the change in the slope of the sample boundary with the maximum and minimum values, and proved
whether the change in the inflection point falls within the range of the variable, so as to strengthen the test of the nonlinear rela-
tionship.  For  the  demonstration  of  the  moderating  variable,  we  not  only  pay  attention  to  the  coefficient  and  significance  of  the
interaction term between the moderating variable and the core variables, but also determine whether the inflection point has changed
to verify the moderating effect of absorptive capacity.

Third, it is found that absorptive capacity does not positively moderate the relationship between technology search and innovation
quality, but is closely related to firms’ technology search behavior. Absorptive capacity plays a different moderating role for different
degree of technology searching. This is very different from the previous conclusion that absorptive capacity simply takes a positive or
negative moderating effect, and it also provides valuable ideas and new ideas for further research.

Our research findings are helpful to high-tech manufacturing firm managers in that the results provide guidance and serve as a

reference for them to make innovative strategic decisions for technology search.

First, the high-tech manufacturing firms should focus more on innovation quality in the process of innovation. However, to improve
innovation quality, firms need to search for external technical knowledge. We find that there are inverted U-shaped relationships
between technology search breadth and depth, and innovation quality, i.e., technology search improves innovation quality. There are
optimal technical search breadth and depth, and firms should make the best effort not to deviate from such optimal critical points.
Second, there is a significantly positive correlation between balanced search and innovation quality, which means that high-tech
manufacturing firms should focus on not only the optimal critical points of search breadth and depth, but also the balance point
between search breadth and search depth. On the one hand, it is necessary that firms expand their search breadth to obtain a wider
scope of heterogeneous knowledge elements and to understand the development trend of markets and technologies. On the other hand,
they should enhance the depth of search for familiar knowledge, isolate tacit knowledge, create technical knowledge that is difficult to
imitate, create technical barriers, and maintain a continuous competitive advantage.

Finally, while focusing on improvement of innovation quality, high-tech manufacturing firms should also focus on enhancing their
own absorptive ability, establishing a knowledge management system, developing a good organizational learning mechanism, and
cultivating and improving the ability of employees to acquire and apply knowledge.

5.3. Limitations and directions for future research

First, the sample data of this study are from high-tech manufacturing industry with strong innovation ability, and the relationship
between technology searching behavior and innovation quality of all firms in the same category or the same industry is discussed. To
some extent, this may ignore the heterogeneity of firms (Caner et al., 2017). In future research, we can obtain specific corporate data
through  field  research,  and  study  the  relationship  between  different  firm’s  technology  search  behaviors  and  innovation  quality.
Second, this paper only analyzes the direct relationship between technology search and innovation quality, so the issue as to whether
there is a variable that mediates the relationship is worth further study. Thirdly, we refer to existing researches and consistent with
scholars such as Duan et al. (2021) and Yu et al. (2019) to use patent ratio to measure firm innovation quality. There is no denying that
such a measurement method is controversial, and a more scientific and accurate measurement method can be found in future research.

# Commercialization Gap

## Definition

The efficiency gap in converting research outputs into market products and services. In a system with strong upstream research and institutions, the gap shows up downstream: research does not reliably become market products, firms, or deployment. Evidence from OECD 2022 shows that German public research institutions exploit only ~39% of their patented inventions within two years, and roughly half of all inventions have commercialization plans that remain unachieved. The gap represents lost economic value and entrepreneurial opportunity at the critical TRL 6-9 stage (development, demonstration, deployment).

## Scope boundaries

Included:

- Translation from research results to validated products, firms, licenses, or deployment.
- TRL 6-9 bottlenecks where prototypes need demonstration, regulatory clearance, customer access, and scale-up capital.
- Technology-transfer offices, proof-of-concept funding, spin-outs, industry partnerships, and commercialization grants.
- Interaction with equity finance and public procurement.

Excluded:

- Pure research excellence without a path to adoption.
- General start-up finance unrelated to research commercialization.
- Any late-stage subsidy that protects an incumbent product without solving a market-entry or validation bottleneck.

## Methodological Note on Sources

**Important:** Earlier versions of this note cited "61% research-to-market conversion (Germany) vs. 90%+ (US)" as if OECD 2022 provided this metric. This was an error. OECD 2022 (Chapter 13.1) explicitly cautions against reducing the diagnosis to a single "conversion-rate" metric. The metrics in the "How it is measured" section above (39% patent exploitation, 48% commercialization planned-but-unachieved, 13% no-plan) are the actual OECD 2022 findings. If the 61%/90%+ figures appear elsewhere in the literature, their source needs separate verification.

## Mechanism

**Three core mechanisms** explain weak commercialization:

### 1. **Weak Tech Transfer Infrastructure**
- Underfunded university and research institute technology transfer offices (TTOs)
- Limited IP licensing expertise and market connection networks
- Slow IP commercialization pathways (average 2â€“3 years licensing in Germany vs. <1 year in US)
- Researchers lack entrepreneurship training and incentive structures for commercialization
- Result: Research stays in academic domain; private firms must reinvent discoveries

### 2. **Regulatory Friction in Deployment** (OECD 2022)
- Electric vehicle certification delays (18â€“36 months in Germany vs. 12 months US)
- Building code approval processes slow cleantech adoption
- Medical device regulatory pathways slower than US FDA
- These regulatory delays compound the time-to-market cost and increase capital requirements
- Companies move R&D and production to jurisdictions with faster approval (US, Asia)

### 3. **Public Program Misalignment**
- A project-level hypothesis is that direct funding may be concentrated either in TRL 1-6 or in later TRLs that primarily help established firms finish development.
- Minimal support for TRL 7-9 would mean a missing bridge from demonstration to deployment; concentrated TRL 7-9 support among incumbents would instead mean market-establishment support rather than new-entry support.
- FK (Forderkatalog) historically provides project funding, not commercialization grants.
- No equivalent to US SBIR Phase III (commercialization of Phase I/II research).
- Result: Publicly-funded research can dead-end at the lab stage, or it can be absorbed into incumbent market maintenance; the difference is empirical, not assumed.

## How the papers use, support, challenge, or modify it

OECD and KAS use the commercialization gap as a system diagnosis: Germany creates research and technical knowledge but converts too little of it into high-growth firms or market deployment. BIO Deutschland makes the mechanism institutional by pointing to transfer offices, validation funding, IP incentives, and growth finance. Howell and Audretsch show why public support and entrepreneurship channels matter: grants and spillovers can help discoveries cross the valley between research and market use, but only if the surrounding finance and transfer institutions exist.

## Open questions and tensions

- Is the main bottleneck proof-of-concept validation, regulation, procurement, founder incentives, or growth finance?
- Should public R&D programs directly fund commercialization, or should they hand off to private capital after TRL 6?
- How can commercialization support avoid becoming late-stage incumbent support?
- Can Foerderkatalog project text reveal whether funding reaches demonstration and deployment stages?

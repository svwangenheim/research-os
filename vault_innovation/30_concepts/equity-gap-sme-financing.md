# Equity Gap: SME Financing

## Definition

Germany's structural undersupply of equity capital for small and medium enterprises (SMEs), particularly at growth and scale-up stages (Series B+, €10m–€500m). Equity represents <1% of total SME investment funding vs. 10% OECD average. The gap is **not** seed/early-stage (well-covered by EXIST, WIN Initiative) but **growth-stage** where institutional capital is scarce.

## Scope boundaries

Included:

- Venture capital, growth equity, Series B+ funding, institutional-investor participation, and scale-up finance.
- Equity constraints that prevent innovative SMEs, start-ups, and spin-outs from moving from validation to growth.
- Interactions between public grants, signaling, follow-on finance, and private capital mobilization.

Excluded:

- Bank lending or debt finance unless it substitutes poorly for risk-bearing growth capital.
- Generic SME financing unrelated to innovation or scale-up.
- Seed grants alone when they do not address the later growth-finance bottleneck.

## Mechanism

**Three core mechanisms** create and sustain the equity gap:

### 1. **Absent Institutional Investor Base**
- German pension funds restricted by regulatory requirements (high guarantee levels, actuarial conservatism)
- Insurance companies prohibited from private equity investment (Versicherungsaufsichtsgesetz)
- Family offices fewer and smaller than in US/Switzerland
- No equivalent to Tibi Initiative (France's €1bn+ institutional deployment tool)
- Result: <€10bn annual VC investment vs. 10× that in US/France

### 2. **Regulatory Barriers to VC Mobilization**
- Non-qualified investor (NQI) restrictions on pension/insurance participation
- Burdensome reporting and governance requirements for VC funds
- Differing insolvency law treatment of growth-stage equity (vs. debt)
- No standardized secondary market for illiquid growth-stage holdings
- Taxation of capital gains uncompetitive vs. peers
- Result: VC managers prefer to raise capital and operate outside Germany

### 3. **Demographic/Structural Mismatch**
- Stagnant founding rate (Bitkom 2025: declining opportunity-driven Gründungen)
- SME preference for debt financing (easier to model, preserve control)
- Limited track record of successful exits (IPO/M&A) that generate investor confidence
- Concentration of founders in services vs. high-growth tech sectors
- Limited role of repeat founders (who attract institutional capital)
- Result: Even available capital finds few compelling investment targets

## How it is measured

| Indicator | Germany | OECD Avg | Nordic/France | Gap |
|-----------|---------|----------|---------------|----|
| **Equity % of SME financing** | <1% | 10% | 25–30% | 10–30x |
| **Annual VC investment** | €6.0bn | €15–20bn | €20–30bn (peer) | 3–5x |
| **Series B+ gap** | €50bn | Balanced | Funded | Critical |
| **Avg time to Series B closure** | 18–24 months | 12–18 months | — | Slow |
| **Institutional capital % of VC** | 15% | 50%+ | 70%+ | Very low |

## How the papers use, support, challenge, or modify it

OECD and the Franco-German FIVE report use the equity gap as a structural finance diagnosis: Germany and Europe underprovide patient growth capital. Howell and Kleer modify the concept by showing that public grants can partly compensate through certification or signaling, but those mechanisms do not replace a deep growth-capital market. Bitkom turns the concept into a founder-side constraint: even promising start-ups face fundraising, bureaucracy, and sales barriers that can prevent scaling.

## Open questions and tensions

- Can public R&D grants substitute for missing equity, or do they only delay the scale-up bottleneck?
- Should innovation policy mobilize institutional investors directly?
- Which sectors need the most equity-intensive commercialization path?
- Does Foerderkatalog support create fundable firms, or mainly support projects that remain grant-dependent?

# SME Innovation Participation

## Definition

SME innovation participation measures whether small and medium-sized firms innovate at all. It is different from total innovation expenditure. A country can maintain stable aggregate spending while innovation becomes concentrated in fewer, larger, or more R&D-intensive firms.

## Scope boundaries

Included:

- Innovator share among SMEs.
- Non-R&D innovation based on practical experience, design, process improvement, or learning.
- Distribution of innovation expenditure across SME size classes.
- Barriers to innovation among small firms.

Excluded:

- Large-firm R&D intensity.
- Start-up creation alone, unless tied to SME innovation participation.
- Patent output alone.

## Mechanism

When small firms stop innovating, the innovation system loses broad renewal capacity. Even if the most radical output comes from a smaller group of R&D-intensive firms, broad SME innovation matters for productivity diffusion, incremental process improvement, technology adoption, and resilience. A declining innovator share can therefore weaken transformation capacity while aggregate innovation spending remains stable.

## Papers that discuss this concept

- [[20_summaries/kfw2024-sme-innovation.md]]: reports that the German SME innovator share fell to 39 percent and that the largest 2 percent of SMEs account for 56 percent of innovation expenditure.
- [[20_summaries/prognos2019-zim-evaluation.md]]: shows that ZIM strongly supports R&D-active SMEs but primarily works in depth rather than breadth, with only a narrow share of firms starting R&D because of the program.
- [[20_summaries/rammer_krieger_peters2022-sme-drivers-barriers.md]]: analyzes SME innovation drivers and barriers over a longer period.
- [[20_summaries/bitkom2025-startup-report.md]]: documents barriers facing technology start-ups.

## How the papers use, support, challenge, or modify it

[[20_summaries/kfw2024-sme-innovation.md]] uses the concept as a distributional warning: stable or rising aggregate innovation spending can coexist with fewer innovating SMEs and higher concentration among a small group of large SMEs. [[20_summaries/prognos2019-zim-evaluation.md]] adds the program-design angle: ZIM has meaningful additionality for funded SMEs but mostly intensifies R&D among firms that already have innovation capabilities, so broad SME participation remains a separate policy problem. [[20_summaries/rammer_krieger_peters2022-sme-drivers-barriers.md]] extends the concept by separating innovation types and barriers, showing that non-R&D innovation remains important for smaller firms. [[20_summaries/bitkom2025-startup-report.md]] modifies the boundary toward young technology firms: start-ups are not the same as SMEs, but their barriers show why broad-based renewal can fail even when formal R&D performers remain active.

## Open questions and tensions

- Should policy focus on broad SME innovation or on a smaller number of high-growth radical innovators?
- How much non-R&D innovation should be subsidized?
- Does federal funding reach firms below the formal R&D threshold?

## Relevance for my research

This concept helps evaluate whether the Bundesinnovationshaushalt supports broad-based innovation capacity or mainly firms already capable of applying for R&D funding.

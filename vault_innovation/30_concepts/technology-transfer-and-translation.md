# Technology Transfer and Translation

## Definition

Technology transfer and translation describe the process by which research results become usable economic or social innovations. The process includes invention disclosure, patenting, validation, licensing, industry partnerships, spin-outs, start-up support, regulatory preparation, management recruitment, investor acquisition, and eventual market entry.

In this vault, the concept is the institutional bridge between strong upstream research and weak downstream growth. It is the mechanism behind the "Viel Wissen, wenig Wachstum" framing.

## Scope boundaries

Included:

- University and public-research technology transfer.
- Transfer offices and professional IP/commercialization support.
- Validation studies and proof-of-concept funding.
- Spin-outs, licensing, industry partnerships, incubators, and accelerators.
- Incentives that make transfer a real institutional mission.

Excluded:

- Pure publication output without commercialization pathway.
- Generic entrepreneurship policy unrelated to research translation.
- Later-stage scale-up finance unless it is directly connected to research-based spin-outs.

## Mechanism

Research translation fails when researchers lack incentives, transfer offices are underfunded, validation funding is missing, patents are poorly managed, industry links are weak, or start-ups cannot finance development. The concept therefore sits between early research and growth finance. It is a central mechanism of the [[30_concepts/commercialization-gap.md]].

## Papers that discuss this concept

- [[20_summaries/biodeutschland2024-tech-transfer.md]]: calls for entrepreneurial culture, dual career paths, professional transfer offices, 5 percent budget targets for transfer and validation, transfer indicators, and growth-finance reform.
- [[20_summaries/kas2025-research-to-business.md]]: discusses research-to-business transfer in Germany.
- [[20_summaries/oecd2022-innovation-policy-germany.md]]: frames transfer and commercialization as weaknesses in Germany's innovation system.

## How the papers use, support, challenge, or modify it

[[20_summaries/biodeutschland2024-tech-transfer.md]] uses the concept institutionally: research translation depends on professional transfer offices, validation budgets, IP routines, entrepreneurial incentives, and growth finance. [[20_summaries/kas2025-research-to-business.md]] uses it as a policy and culture problem between research organizations and business formation. [[20_summaries/oecd2022-innovation-policy-germany.md]] places it in the broader national innovation-system diagnosis: Germany has strong research capacity, but transfer and commercialization channels are not strong enough to convert that capacity into market outcomes.

The concept therefore supports but is not identical to [[30_concepts/commercialization-gap.md]]. Technology transfer describes the institutional process; commercialization gap describes the system-level performance shortfall.

### Ansatz A: Pflicht-KMU-Uni-Kooperation

**Schweiz — Innosuisse (stärkste Evidenz):**
Solo-Anträge formal abgelehnt; beide Seiten (Implementierungspartner + Forschungspartner) müssen gemeinsam einreichen. Beck, Lopes-Bento & Schenker-Wicki (2016), *Research Policy* 45(4): 869–883: Pflichtkooperation hat signifikant positiven Effekt auf **radikale Innovation** (Marktneuheiten), keinen signifikanten Effekt auf inkrementelle Innovation. Mechanismus: Qualitätsfilter — verhindert Substitution öffentlicher für private Mittel bei Routineprojekten. CHF 319–335 Mio./Jahr. Schweiz führt Europa beim Spinout-Wert per capita (European Spinouts Report 2025). Einstiegsinstrument: Innovation Cheque (CHF 15.000, 100% öffentlich, für Firmen ohne Forschungserfahrung). Quelle: https://www.innosuisse.admin.ch/en/innovation-project-with-implementation-partner

**Finnland — Business Finland Bridge Call (2025):**
Standard Co-Innovation: Pflicht-Konsortium ≥2 Firmen + Forschungsorganisation. Bridge Call: Nur Forschungsorganisationen erhalten Förderung (80%); ≥3 KMU als Lenkungsgruppe ohne Eigenfinanzierungspflicht. Zielgruppe: KMU, die "noch nie Forschungsexpertise genutzt haben." Senkt Einstiegshürde für Erst-Kooperatoren. Quelle: https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/services/funding/funding-services/cooperation-between-companies-and-research-organizations/co-innovation

### Ansatz B: Physische Transferinfrastruktur

**UK — Catapult-Netzwerk (stärkster Addizionalitätswert):**
9 Catapults in 65+ Standorten: High Value Manufacturing, Digital, Energy Systems, Cell & Gene Therapy, Offshore Renewable Energy, Compound Semiconductor Applications, Connected Places, Medicines Discovery, Satellite Applications. Unabhängige gemeinnützige Gesellschaften; Finanzierung: ⅓ öffentlicher Kern / ⅓ kommerziell / ⅓ kollaborative F&E. Budget: £1,6 Mrd./5 Jahre (April 2023–März 2028). Kumulativ: 11.916 KMU unterstützt, £8 Mrd. BIP-Wirkung, **80% der Projekte wären ohne Catapult nicht entstanden**. CSA Catapult (unabhängige Evaluation Harlow Consulting 2023): engagierte Firmen £82,9 Mio. vs. £17,6 Mio. Kontrafaktum private Investitionen/Jahr; 67% Projekte +1–3 TRL-Stufen. Strukturprinzip: "Neutral convener" — kein einzelner Branchenakteur ist Auftraggeber. Quelle: https://catapult.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Catapult-Network-Prospectus-Spring-2025-DIGITAL.pdf

**Dänemark — GTS-Institute (effizientestes Marktdisziplin-Modell):**
7 Institute (Danish Technological Institute, FORCE Technology, DHI, DBI u.a.): **8% öffentliches Performance-Contract-Einkommen / 92% kommerzielle Einnahmen.** Marktdisziplin stellt Relevanz für KMU sicher; staatliche Kofinanzierung hält Institute an der Technologiegrenze. Skala (2024): DKK 4 Mrd. Umsatz; 20.648 einzigartige Privatkunden/Jahr; **89% KMU-Kunden.** Öffentliches Budget: 369 Mio. DKK/Jahr. Schlüsselunterschied zu Fraunhofer: Institute müssen Großteil ihrer Einnahmen kommerziell erwirtschaften → direkte Rechenschaftspflicht gegenüber Industriebedarf. Quelle: https://gts-net.dk/english/the-gts-system/

**Niederlande — NWO Demonstrator:**
Valley-of-death-Brücke: max. €160.000/Projekt (18 Monate), TRL 4 Ziel. Struktur: "Show" (MVP bauen) + "Transfer" (Demonstration für Drittpartei). Quelle: https://www.nwo.nl/en/researchprogrammes/demonstrator

## Relevance for my research

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project, this concept helps interpret whether federal project funding closes the research-to-market gap or mainly funds research without downstream translation capacity. The key empirical question is whether funding actually moves knowledge into spin-outs, licenses, validation, and market entry, or whether it mostly reinforces established research and industrial routines.

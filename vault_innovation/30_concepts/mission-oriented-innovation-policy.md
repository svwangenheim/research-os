# Mission-Oriented Innovation Policy

## Definition

Mission-oriented innovation policy is an approach to research and innovation policy that starts from societal or strategic transformation goals and organizes public action around them. Instead of funding isolated projects under broad thematic labels, a mission-oriented approach defines concrete goals, creates cross-ministerial governance, coordinates policy instruments, assigns responsibilities, funds implementation capacity, and measures progress through roadmaps and milestones.

In the German context covered by this vault, the concept appears under labels such as "Neue Missionsorientierung," "missionsorientierte F&I-Politik," and mission-oriented transformation policy.

## Scope boundaries

This concept includes:

- concrete transformation missions such as climate neutrality, digital sovereignty, health, mobility, energy, agriculture, or key technologies;
- cross-ministerial mission teams and governance structures;
- roadmaps, milestones, budgets, and policy mixes;
- coordination across research funding, regulation, procurement, data policy, infrastructure, and transfer;
- evaluation of whether mission instruments contribute to transformation goals.

This concept does not include every thematic funding program. A program is not mission-oriented only because it names a broad theme. It becomes mission-oriented when goals, instruments, governance, budgets, measurement, and implementation responsibilities are connected.

## Mechanism

The mechanism is coordination. Transformative innovation usually requires multiple complementary changes: research, infrastructure, standards, regulation, procurement, market formation, finance, skills, and adoption. Individual ministries or project programs can fund pieces of this system, but without a mission structure those pieces can remain misaligned. Mission-oriented policy tries to reduce this coordination failure by bundling action around a shared goal.

EFI 2023 emphasizes that Germany's Zukunftsstrategie contains mission elements but needs concrete handlungsleitende Missionen, measurable transformation goals, roadmaps, and stronger cross-ministerial governance. EFI 2024 adds that mission teams need strategic support and their own budgets, and that mission success often extends beyond a single legislative period.

## How it is measured

Mission orientation is not measured by one simple indicator. Useful measures include:

- whether a mission has concrete, measurable goals;
- whether measures across ministries are mapped to a shared roadmap;
- whether mission teams have budgets, authority, and senior-level backing;
- whether project funding can be linked to mission milestones;
- whether evaluation designs can assess contribution to mission outcomes;
- whether the portfolio covers the necessary TRL stages, recipient types, technologies, and complementary capabilities.

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project, mission orientation can be operationalized by classifying projects by theme, TRL, recipient type, policy instrument, and relation to declared mission goals.

## How the papers use the concept

EFI uses the concept as a governance reform agenda. OECD uses related language to diagnose weaknesses in coordination and policy-system design. DIW and Duso et al. use a related logic when they argue that strategic technologies and transformation bottlenecks require coordinated investment rather than generic relief. BDI/Rammer use the idea more as a policy implication from benchmarking: Germany should pursue innovation-policy goals more consistently and align instruments.

## Open questions and tensions

- Missions can improve coordination, but they can also become vague labels if not tied to budgets and measurable goals.
- Mission-oriented policy must balance directionality with openness to firm experimentation and unexpected technological paths.
- Strong missions can be captured by incumbents if governance does not protect entrant and SME participation.
- Project-level TRL analysis can reveal maturity distribution, but not by itself whether a portfolio is mission-coherent.

# Green Innovation and Transition Policy

## Definition

Green innovation and transition policy covers technological, organizational, and business-model innovation aimed at climate mitigation, environmental performance, energy efficiency, renewable-energy integration, resource productivity, circularity, and industrial decarbonization. It also covers the public policies that redirect innovation toward these goals: regulation, carbon pricing, mission policy, subsidies, standards, infrastructure, demand creation, and strategic industrial policy.

## Scope boundaries

Included:

- Climate-change-driven innovation and eco-innovation.
- Firm responses to energy-price shocks and transition pressure.
- Green industrial policy, transition missions, and decarbonization technologies.
- Links between environmental regulation, demand, energy costs, and innovation behavior.
- Green technology fields inside key-technology or HTAD taxonomies.

Excluded:

- General environmental policy without an innovation mechanism.
- Energy-price effects on output or costs when no innovation response is involved.
- Generic strategic industrial policy unless the transformation target is climate or environmental transition.

## How the papers use, support, challenge, or modify it

The Horbach/Rammer line treats green innovation as firm behavior shaped by climate exposure, demand, energy prices, and sector conditions. Strategic-industrial-policy sources treat green innovation as a coordination problem: firms may underinvest if infrastructure, standards, demand, finance, or complementary technologies are missing. EU benchmarking sources show that transition capacity is increasingly part of innovation-system performance rather than a separate environmental issue.

The tension is that short-term energy shocks can reduce investment capacity even when they increase transition pressure. Green innovation therefore requires instruments that do more than raise costs; they must create credible demand, reduce risk, and support the movement from research to deployment.

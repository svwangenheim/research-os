# Innovation Policy Governance and Evaluation

## Scope

This synthesis connects the governance and evaluation layer of the German innovation-policy wiki. It is the retrieval bridge for questions about missions, DATI, SPRIND, project-agency reform, Reallabore, Forschungszulage, IP transfer, data access, and causal evaluation.

## Main synthesis

The vault's innovation-policy evidence no longer points only to a funding-volume problem. Across EFI, OECD, DIW/Duso, KfW, ZIM evaluation, and technology-transfer sources, the stronger diagnosis is that Germany needs better allocation, coordination, translation, and learning.

EFI 2023 makes the governance argument: Germany needs a mission-oriented F&I-policy shift with cross-ministerial mission teams, roadmaps, and strategic steering. EFI 2024 adds implementation and learning: missions need budgets and continuity; Reallabore can reduce uncertainty; DATI and SPRIND need openness and autonomy; IP-transfer incentives should favor spin-out success; and the effects of instruments such as the Forschungszulage need credible evaluation.

DIW and Duso et al. reinforce the coordination-failure side of the argument. Strategic technologies and transformation bottlenecks require coordinated investment, infrastructure, standards, regulation, and market formation rather than generic subsidies alone. OECD 2022 provides the broader innovation-system diagnosis that Germany has strong capabilities but needs better transfer and coordination.

The evaluation layer is the main addition from EFI 2024. Many policy evaluations can describe program activity but cannot say whether a measure caused the observed outcome. [[20_summaries/prognos2019-zim-evaluation.md]] shows a stronger mixed-methods evaluation pattern for ZIM: monitoring, surveys, qualitative evidence, and econometric estimates are combined, but robustness and sample limits still matter. [[40_methods/causal-evaluation-innovation-policy.md]] therefore becomes a central method note for the wiki. It separates portfolio description from impact evaluation and ties policy learning to data access.

## Agreements across sources

- Mission-oriented policy needs concrete goals, roadmaps, budgets, and cross-ministerial authority.
- Transfer and commercialization are institutional bottlenecks, not just firm-level problems.
- Strategic industrial policy should solve coordination failures rather than only lower costs.
- Better data access is needed both for innovation, especially AI and digital applications, and for evaluation.
- Descriptive monitoring is insufficient for claims about policy effects.

## Tensions and open questions

- Directionality versus experimentation: missions need goals, but firms and researchers need freedom to discover solutions.
- Strategic policy versus incumbent capture: coordinated investment can help, but it can also protect established actors if governance is weak.
- Short legislative cycles versus long mission timelines: many innovation effects arrive after the government that initiated them.
- Privacy and data protection versus evaluation and innovation: better data access needs legitimate governance, not deregulation by default.
- TRL portfolio mapping versus causal impact: classifying projects by maturity is valuable but does not estimate additionality.

## Implications for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project

The project should separate three layers:

1. Portfolio description: Which technologies, recipients, ministries, missions, and TRL stages receive funding?
2. Governance interpretation: Do projects align with missions, transfer bottlenecks, data infrastructure, key technologies, and commercialization needs?
3. Impact evaluation: Did a funding instrument cause additional R&D, innovation, commercialization, productivity, or private investment?

The current LLM/TRL workflow mainly supports layer 1 and part of layer 2. EFI 2024 shows that layer 3 requires causal designs and better linked outcome data.

## EIC vs. DARPA — Governance-Lücke (neu 2026-05-05)

[[20_summaries/fuest_et_al_2024_how_to_escape_the_middle_technology_trap.md]] liefert die detaillierteste verfügbare Analyse der EU-Innovationsgovernance-Lücke. Kernaussagen:

- **TRL-Fokus:** ARPAs fördern TRL 1–4 (Grundlagen- und angewandte Forschung ohne kommerzielle Anwendung); EIC fokussiert auf TRL 5+. Weniger als 5% des Horizon-Europe-Budgets (~€460 Mio./Jahr) fließt in echte Durchbruchs-Innovation.
- **Programm-Manager-Defizit:** DARPA hat ~100 Programm-Manager für ein ähnliches Budget wie der EIC; der EIC hat ~11. DARPA-PMs sind Top-Wissenschaftler mit hoher Entscheidungsautonomie; EIC-PMs operieren unter Kommissionsaufsicht mit begrenzten Kompetenzen.
- **Budgetzusammensetzung Horizon Europe:** ~€11 Mrd./Jahr Gesamtbudget; EIC Pathfinder (~€350 Mio.) + Transition (~€110 Mio.) sind die einzigen ARPA-ähnlichen Programme; EIC Accelerator (~€410 Mio.) ist primär KMU-Risikokapital; EIT (~€600 Mio./Jahr) ohne klaren Effizienznachweis.
- **Reformvorschlag (budgetneutral):** EIT-Budget zum überwiegenden Teil zum EIC Pathfinder umlenken; Wissenschaftler in EIC-Vorstand; KV-Aktivitäten auslagern; Pflicht zur grenzüberschreitenden Zusammenarbeit abschaffen; Auszahlungsprozesse beschleunigen.
- **Crowding-out-Hinweis:** In der EU generiert €1 öffentliche FuE €2 private FuE (Multiplikator); in den USA €3. Marino et al. (2016) und Szücs (2020) deuten darauf hin, dass öffentliche Förderung in FuE-extensiven Branchen stärker verdrängt — mögliche Erklärung für den niedrigeren EU-Multiplikator.

**Einordnung in die Syntheselage:** Fuest et al. (2024) bestätigt und schärft das EFI-Argument (governance statt nur Volumen), aber mit EU-Fokus statt DE-Fokus und mit einer konkreteren institutionellen Reformempfehlung als EFI 2023/2024. Die beiden Perspektiven ergänzen sich: EFI adressiert die nationale Ebene (DATI, SPRIND, Forschungszulage), Fuest et al. die EU-Ebene (EIC, Horizon Europe). [[30_concepts/mitteltechnologie-falle.md]] ist das übergreifende Diagnosekonzept, das beide Ebenen verbindet.

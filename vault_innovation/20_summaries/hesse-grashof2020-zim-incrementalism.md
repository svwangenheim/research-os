## Detailed summary

Hesse and Grashof (2020) evaluate the Central Innovation Programme for SMEs (ZIM), Germany's largest SME R&D grant programme, using Fraunhofer ISI survey and project data. The paper finds positive effects on innovation output (additionality confirmed) but its key contribution for the Foerderkatalog project is the finding about the type of innovation produced: most ZIM projects are incremental improvements rather than radical innovations.

ZIM is the programme most directly associated with Mid TRL (4–6) bridging in Germany — it targets "industrielle Forschung und experimentelle Entwicklung," explicitly spanning the valley-of-death zone. Hesse and Grashof's finding therefore implies:
- ZIM addresses the right TRL range (Mid TRL 4–6, valley of death)
- ZIM reaches the right recipients (SMEs)
- But the resulting innovations are mostly incremental improvements of existing technologies, not radical departures from existing trajectories

This finding connects to Germany's documented incremental innovation bias (KfW 2024: only ~7% of SMEs generate genuinely new-to-market products; OECD 2022: relative weakness in disruptive innovation). TRL targeting and novelty targeting are partially independent policy dimensions: a programme can be well-targeted by TRL stage and firm size while still producing predominantly incremental output, if the underlying firm-level innovation mode is already incremental.

The trl_research_briefing uses this paper as evidence that even a correctly-targeted valley-of-death programme (ZIM) may not systematically generate radical innovation. The diagnostic matrix (TRL × firm size × sector × novelty) needs all four dimensions; TRL alone is not sufficient.

## Research question

Does ZIM funding increase firm innovation activity, and what types of innovations (incremental vs. radical) does ZIM primarily produce?

## Core argument or contribution

ZIM produces positive innovation effects but predominantly incremental innovation outputs. This is the evidential basis for the concern that Germany's Mid TRL public support — even when correctly targeting the valley-of-death stage and SME recipients — does not systematically produce the radical or breakthrough innovations that generate the highest social returns.

## Key findings

- ZIM funding has positive effects on innovation output (additionality confirmed)
- Most ZIM projects produce **incremental improvements** rather than radical innovations
- Consistent with KfW (2024): only ~7% of German SMEs generate genuinely new-to-market products
- Consistent with OECD (2022): Germany's relative weakness in disruptive innovation despite strong SME sector
- The incremental character of ZIM output holds despite ZIM targeting the Mid TRL valley-of-death stage

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For [[70_projects/bundesinnovationshaushalt-trl.md]], this paper adds an important caveat to the TRL interpretation. TRL targeting alone is not sufficient to ensure radical innovation output. Even a well-designed Mid TRL programme (ZIM) reaching the right recipients (SMEs) can produce predominantly incremental output if the underlying SME innovation mode is already incremental.

The policy brief's interpretation of TRL classification results should distinguish two dimensions:
1. TRL stage bias (High TRL concentration → near-market, lower spillovers) — directly testable via the Förderprojekte classification
2. Project-level novelty bias (even at correct TRL stages, innovations may be incremental) — requires supplementary data beyond Förderprojekte fields

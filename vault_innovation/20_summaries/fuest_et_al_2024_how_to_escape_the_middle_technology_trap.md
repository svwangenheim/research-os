## Detailed summary

The most comprehensive treatment of the Mitteltechnologie-Falle concept. The report argues that the EU is losing the global innovation race through a structural path-dependency trap: for over 20 years, the same automotive companies have dominated EU private R&D while US leadership shifted from automotive to software. The report diagnoses the problem quantitatively and proposes a budget-neutral institutional reform.

**I. Innovation spending compared:** EU BERD is 1.2% of GDP vs. 2.3% in the US. High-tech accounts for 85% of US BERD but only ~50% of EU BERD. After 2013, US high-tech R&D intensity increased sharply while EU stagnated. Shift-share decomposition: 60% of the US-EU R&D intensity gap is due to sectoral composition (high-tech sector is simply larger in the US), and 40% to genuinely higher within-sector R&D intensity — the within-sector gap is post-2013 and worsening. In software capex, US companies spent €104bn in 2022 vs. EU €1bn — a 100-fold gap that makes catching up in generative AI effectively impossible without structural change.

**II. Can the EIC help escape the mid-tech trap?** The ARPA model (DARPA, ARPA-E, ARPA-H) is the benchmark: ARPAs fund breakthrough research at TRL 1–4, have empowered program managers drawn from top science/engineering, operate with minimal bureaucracy, and focus on research institutions. The EIC falls short on all dimensions: it targets TRL 5+; is governed by EU officials not top scientists; has bureaucratic selection processes with mandatory cross-border collaboration requirements; devotes a large budget share to SME venture capital rather than basic/applied research; and disburses funding slowly.

**III. Anatomy of Horizon Europe:** Of Horizon Europe's ~€11bn/year, less than 5% funds breakthrough innovation. EIC Pathfinder (~€350m) and Transition (~€110m) are the only ARPA-like programs. EIC Accelerator (~€410m) is primarily SME venture capital. The European Institute of Innovation and Technology (EIT, ~€600m/year) lacks a clear effectiveness record. The Innovation Fund (~€680m/year) is a climate/energy instrument, not a general innovation vehicle.

**IV. Budget-neutral reform proposal:**
- Expand EIC Pathfinder and ARPA-style programs by redirecting EIT budget (which lacks effectiveness evidence)
- Give leading scientists central roles on the EIC Board and in project selection
- Shift decision-making power from the Commission to more independent program managers (DARPA has ~100; EIC has ~11)
- Outsource EIC venture capital activities to a specialized external fund
- Remove mandatory cross-border collaboration requirements from eligibility criteria
- Speed up disbursement processes

## Research question

Can the EIC as currently designed help Europe escape the middle technology trap, and if not, what budget-neutral reforms would make it effective?

## Core argument or contribution

Two contributions: (1) a shift-share decomposition of the US-EU R&D intensity gap that identifies both a composition effect (60%) and a growing within-sector intensity effect (40%, post-2013); (2) a concrete budget-neutral institutional reform proposal that restructures Horizon Europe toward ARPA-style breakthrough innovation governance with scientist-led program management.

## Methodology

- Quantitative descriptive analysis: EU Industrial R&D Scoreboard (top 2,500 companies), WIPO PCT data, OECD data
- Shift-share decomposition of R&D intensity gap (composition vs. within-sector intensity)
- Budget analysis of Horizon Europe programs (CORDIS grants data, 2021–2022 averages)
- Institutional comparison: EIC vs. DARPA governance, budget, personnel, selection processes

## Key findings

1. **Decomposition of R&D gap:** 60% of the US-EU BERD intensity gap is sectoral composition; 40% is within-sector intensity. Before 2013, composition explained >90% — the within-sector gap is recent and accelerating.

2. **Software capex chasm:** US software sector capex €104bn vs. EU €1bn in 2022 — 100-fold difference representing the AI infrastructure investment gap.

3. **Natural oligopoly lock-in:** R&D-intensive industries exhibit natural oligopoly dynamics (large market shares → R&D → large market shares). EU failure to enter these sectors early creates compounding disadvantage; entry becomes progressively harder.

4. **EIC TRL positioning:** ARPAs focus on TRL 1–4. EIC focuses on TRL 5+. Less than 5% of Horizon Europe supports true breakthrough innovation; the rest targets technologies already close to commercial application.

5. **EIC program manager deficit:** DARPA has ~100 program managers for a similar budget to EIC; EIC has ~11. Program managers at DARPA are expert scientists with significant autonomy; EIC program managers operate under EU Commission oversight with limited decision power.

6. **EIT budget without evidence of effectiveness:** EIT has a ~€600m/year budget (comparable to EIC) but lacks evidence of innovation impact. The report proposes reallocating most of this to EIC Pathfinder.

7. **Public R&D multiplier gap:** In the EU, €1 of public R&D is associated with €2 of private R&D; in the US, the multiplier is €3. This gap may partly reflect crowding-out in low-R&D-intensity sectors (Marino et al. 2016; Szücs 2020).

8. **EU defence industry as partial exception:** Aerospace & defence is the one sector where EU R&D spending approaches US levels (€8.7bn vs. €9.4bn), though the market size gap is large. The European Defence Fund should allocate some budget to DARPA-style bold projects rather than only near-commercial technologies.

## Limitations

- Institutional reform proposals are normative; no causal evidence that ARPA-style governance works in European institutional contexts
- Budget-neutral assumption constrains ambition — the volume gap (total EU vs. US R&D) is acknowledged but left to national governments
- No counterfactual analysis of what EIC grants have actually achieved
- Sectoral high/mid-tech taxonomy follows EU/OECD convention but is contested for software-intensive manufacturers (e.g., German automotive)

## S-curve and TRL framing (added 2026-05-11)

The TRL research briefing (`explorations/trl_research_briefing.md`, Section 2.1) cites "Fuest, Meister & Tirole (2024) — ifo/TSE Working Paper" for a specific theoretical framework: the S-curve of innovation as the link between TRL and the radical/incremental distinction.

- **Low TRL = early phase of the S-curve:** high effort per unit progress, high uncertainty, high potential for breakthrough — higher ex ante probability of radical outcome
- **High TRL = mature phase:** returns to effort are diminishing, uncertainty is resolved — incremental improvement of known approaches dominates

**Co-author disambiguation note:** The briefing cites "Fuest, Meister & Tirole" as a distinct paper from the full policy report group (Fuest, Gros, Mengel, Presidente, Tirole) summarized in this vault note. The "Fuest, Meister & Tirole" citation may refer to a separate ifo Working Paper or ifo Schnelldienst contribution focused specifically on optimal public R&D funding conditions across the innovation S-curve, with the German case as empirical illustration. The vault's current summary covers the full "How to Escape the Middle Technology Trap" policy report. If the S-curve welfare analysis is from a different document, a separate summary may be needed. This requires source verification.

The S-curve framework provides formal theoretical grounding for why TRL distribution matters for innovation welfare: at Low TRL, public R&D moves a project along the steep, high-uncertainty part of the curve where breakthrough outcomes are possible; at High TRL, public R&D moves a project along the mature, near-linear part where returns are predictable but bounded by the existing technological paradigm. This directly motivates the policy interpretation of the Förderprojekte TRL classification results.

## Implications for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project

1. **TRL hypothesis grounding:** The ARPA-vs-EIC framework (TRL 1–4 vs. TRL 5+) directly motivates the project's TRL classification of the Förderkatalog. The report is the most authoritative source for why TRL distribution matters for public innovation policy.
2. **Shift-share decomposition template:** The composition vs. intensity decomposition can be applied to the Förderkatalog — is the German public funding gap primarily a sectoral composition problem (too much automotive/mid-tech) or a TRL distribution problem (too much TRL 6–9)?
3. **Governance benchmark:** The EIC vs. DARPA institutional comparison provides a template for evaluating German national instruments (SPRIND, project agencies, Forschungszulage) against the ARPA standard.
4. **Budget-neutral framing:** The report demonstrates that institutional redesign can shift funding toward breakthrough innovation without requiring additional appropriations — directly relevant for innovation policy reform recommendations.

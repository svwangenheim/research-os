## Key findings

**Typical recipient:** Founded 1993 (median), 52 employees, €8.7M revenue (2014), €444,000 R&D expenditure - **Prior R&D activity:** 54% had regular R&D before project; 68% at survey time — ZIM induces R&D engagement - **Additionality:** 65% of projects would not have been conducted without ZIM; virtually all others done faster/larger - **Technical success:** 82% of projects largely or fully achieved technical objectives - **Market introduction:** 82% of projects led to results currently or planned for market; 25.5% by end of 2016, 33.9% in 2017 - **Revenue:** Average revenue from ZIM-developed products: €173k in 2017, expected €392k in 2019 - **Employment:** Average 1.2 new jobs per project; 5.3 jobs secured per project (0.6 / 1.6 in R&D) - **Cooperation:** 65-84% of projects involved research institution cooperation; rising trend - **First-time recipients:** ~54% first-time ZIM recipients in 2014 cohort, 51% in 2017 — substantial turnover

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

The 65% additionality finding is the key result: more than half of ZIM-funded projects represent genuine additional innovation that market alone would not generate. This supports the view that even the current (potentially biased) allocation has real impact. However, the lack of TRL disaggregation prevents assessment of whether this additionality is concentrated in early or late R&D stages. Our TRL classification can fill this gap.

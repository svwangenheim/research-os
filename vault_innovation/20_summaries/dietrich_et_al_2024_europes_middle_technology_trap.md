## Detailed summary

This policy article presents evidence that European private R&D is structurally locked into mid-technology sectors — primarily automotive, industrial machinery, and chemicals — while the US has shifted decisively into high-technology industries (software, ICT, pharmaceuticals/biotechnology). The resulting "Middle-Technology Trap" explains why Europe's productivity and competitiveness have lagged the US since roughly 2000 despite reasonable aggregate R&D spending.

The diagnosis draws on the EU Industrial R&D Scoreboard (BERD composition), WIPO PCT patent data (innovation output by technology field), and the OECD Long-Term Productivity Database. The article documents the trap across inputs (R&D spending), outputs (patents), and economic outcomes (labor productivity, profit margins). It closes with a reform agenda: the EIC must shift toward DARPA-style breakthrough innovation (low TRL) and reduce its focus on SME venture capital (high TRL).

## Research question

Is Europe's concentration of private R&D in mid-technology sectors a structural trap with growth consequences, and what does this imply for EU innovation policy?

## Core argument or contribution

Europe is not simply behind in R&D volume — it is behind in R&D *direction*. The US reorganized private R&D around high-tech industries (software, pharma/biotech) while the EU, Japan, and Germany remained locked in automotive. This reflects path dependence rather than comparative advantage, because high-tech industries show higher profit margins (~7pp above mid-tech in the US), faster revenue growth, and faster patent growth than mid-tech. EU policymakers must redirect innovation funding toward disruptive, low-TRL breakthrough research.

## Methodology

Descriptive comparative analysis using:
- EU Industrial R&D Scoreboard (2023 edition, top 2,500 companies): BERD by sector and region over time
- WIPO PCT applications by technology field (1995–2023)
- OECD Long-Term Productivity Database 2024
- EU Industrial R&D Scoreboard profit-margin data (2020–2022 averages)

No causal identification. Policy brief format.

## Key findings

1. **R&D volume gap:** EU total R&D was 2.2% of GDP in 2021 vs. 3.5% in the US and 3.3% in Japan. China overtook the EU at 2.4%. The Lisbon 3% target remains unmet.

2. **Private sector gap:** BERD was 1.2% of GDP in EU vs. 2.3% in US. US firms account for 67% of their country's total R&D; EU firms 57%.

3. **Sectoral composition:** US firms direct 85% of BERD to high-tech (software, pharma/biotech). EU firms split ~45%/45% between high-tech and mid-tech. Germany is the most extreme: 57% mid-tech (automotive dominates).

4. **Path dependence:** Top-3 EU R&D spenders in 2003 were VW, Mercedes, Siemens; in 2022 still VW, Mercedes, Bosch. In the US, top-3 shifted from Ford/Pfizer/GM to Amazon/Alphabet/Meta. This 20-year comparison illustrates structural lock-in consistent with Acemoglu (2023).

5. **Patent output:** EU patent growth has stagnated in ICT (computer technology, digital communications), pharma/biotech over 2010–2023. US leads in ICT and pharma. EU leads only in transportation — a category whose global share fell from 7.7% (2010) to 6.7% (2023) of PCT applications.

6. **Profit margins:** High-tech margins exceed mid-tech in all regions. In Europe, the gap is ~3pp; in the US ~7pp. Lower relative incentive in the EU to shift resources from mid- to high-tech is consistent with path-dependent lock-in.

7. **Productivity consequences:** EU labor productivity was ~20% below the US in 2022. Germany reached 94% of US level; France 89%; Italy 74%; Spain 72%. Since 1995, US productivity rose 53% vs. 34% in Germany and 9% in Italy.

8. **EIC vs. DARPA governance gap:** DARPA spends ~€4bn/year with ~60% on basic/applied research. EIC allocates only ~€470m to DARPA-like breakthrough innovation (<5% of EU annual R&D budget). EIC governance is bureaucratic, Commission-dominated, over-weighted toward SME venture capital, and underpowered in program managers.

## Limitations

- Descriptive only — no causal identification
- Sectoral high/mid-tech classification is contested (automotive increasingly incorporates software/ICT)
- Aggregation masks within-sector heterogeneity
- EU BERD data track headquarters, not R&D location
- China PCT figures may be inflated by domestic subsidies

## Implications for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project

This paper is the **primary conceptual foundation** for the Mitteltechnologie-Falle concept as used in the project. Key implications:

1. The sectoral composition of the Förderkatalog should be checked: does public funding reinforce automotive/mid-tech incumbents or support high-tech/disruptive entrants?
2. The TRL distribution in funded projects should be compared against the ARPA ideal (TRL 1–4 focus) and the EIC critique (TRL 5+ dominance).
3. Germany is not just an EU outlier — it is the extreme case of mid-tech lock-in within an already mid-tech-heavy EU.
4. The EIC vs. DARPA comparison provides a governance benchmark for what a breakthrough-oriented funding agency looks like.

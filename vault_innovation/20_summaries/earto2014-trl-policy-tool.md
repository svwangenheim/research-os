## Detailed summary

EARTO's 2014 recommendations paper established the three-group TRL scheme as the standard for European research and innovation programme evaluation. The paper argues for using TRL as an operational portfolio-monitoring and programme-design tool, covering how to define programme scope, track portfolio balance across the pipeline, and align public support with market-failure rationales.

The document proposes the canonical three-phase grouping:

**Low TRL (1–3) — Basic Research.** Science-based concept and proof-of-concept work. Knowledge is generic and widely applicable. Spillovers are highest because early-stage research generates insights that many firms can use and that are difficult to patent or appropriate. Private investment is systematically undersupplied here because of weak appropriability. This is the canonical public-goods zone where government R&D support has the strongest market-failure justification.

**Mid TRL (4–6) — Development / Industrial Research.** Technology validated in laboratory and relevant environments. This is the "valley of death" zone where laboratory results fail to translate into commercial products. Private capital faces highest information asymmetry about which technologies will succeed. Additionality is strongest for government funding that bridges this gap — especially for SMEs that cannot self-fund development risk.

**High TRL (7–9) — Demonstration and Deployment / Experimental Development.** Near-commercial or commercially deployed systems. Market failure is weakest: private investors can assess risk, patents are more defined, and commercial returns are visible. Government subsidies at this stage most closely resemble industrial policy rather than market-failure correction. The risk of crowding out private investment is highest here.

EARTO maps these three TRL groups to EU State Aid framework categories:
- TRL 1–3 → Basic Research (highest EU subsidy intensity: up to 100%)
- TRL 4–6 → Industrial Research (up to 50–65%)
- TRL 7–9 → Experimental Development (up to 25–40%)

The Frascati Manual mapping is also made explicit: basic research / applied research / experimental development corresponds approximately to TRL 1–3 / 4–6 / 7–8, with TRL 9 treated as commercialization outside the R&D statistical boundary.

EARTO recommends using TRL distribution as a portfolio monitoring tool — examining whether a programme portfolio covers the full pipeline or concentrates at specific stages. A portfolio heavily concentrated at High TRL may indicate that public funding is financing activities that private markets can fund, rather than addressing genuine market failures at earlier stages.

## Research question

How should the TRL scale be used as an operational policy tool for EU research and innovation programmes, and what three-group classification is appropriate for programme evaluation and portfolio monitoring?

## Core argument or contribution

EARTO establishes the canonical three-group TRL scheme (Low 1–3 / Mid 4–6 / High 7–9) as the standard for EU R&I programme evaluation. It maps TRL levels to EU State Aid categories and to the Frascati Manual, providing regulatory and statistical grounding for TRL-based policy analysis. The core policy claim is that public support has the strongest justification at Low TRL (highest spillovers) and Mid TRL (valley of death), and the weakest justification at High TRL (near-commercial).

## Methodology

Policy recommendations paper; not an empirical study. Evidence base combines EU State Aid rules, Frascati Manual definitions, programme evaluation experience, and policy analysis.

## Key findings

- Three canonical TRL groups: Low (1–3) / Mid (4–6) / High (7–9) — the standard scheme for EU programme evaluation.
- TRL groups map to EU State Aid categories, providing regulatory grounding for programme design and subsidy intensity limits.
- TRL groups map approximately to Frascati Manual categories (basic / applied / experimental development).
- Low TRL has the strongest market-failure justification for public support; High TRL the weakest.
- Portfolio monitoring using TRL distribution is recommended as a standard programme evaluation tool.
- The "valley of death" at Mid TRL is identified as the stage where additionality is highest.

## Limitations

- Policy recommendations paper, not empirical evidence on TRL distribution outcomes or treatment effects.
- The TRL 3/4 boundary (concept proof vs. applied development) and TRL 6/7 boundary (lab demonstration vs. operational demo) involve judgment calls and are the points where self-reported TRL diverges most from independent assessment.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For [[70_projects/bundesinnovationshaushalt-trl.md]], EARTO (2014) is the primary literature justification for using Scheme 1 (Low/Mid/High = TRL 1–3/4–6/7–9). It grounds the three-group scheme in EU State Aid law, Frascati Manual definitions, and programme evaluation practice — three independent legitimating frameworks. When reporting TRL classification results in the policy brief, EARTO is the most authoritative citation for why the Low/Mid/High grouping is standard and why TRL concentration has policy-relevance.

## Detailed summary

The KfW Gruendungsmonitor 2024 is a descriptive annual report on entrepreneurial activity in Germany. Its headline diagnosis is that macroeconomic impulses for start-up activity were weak in 2023. Start-up activity rose slightly, but the composition worsened from a full-time entrepreneurship perspective: the number of total business formations increased to 568,000, up 3 percent from 550,000 in 2022, while full-time start-ups fell to 205,000, down 8 percent, and part-time start-ups rose to 363,000, up 11 percent.

The report's data basis is a representative population survey. Each year, 50,000 randomly selected residents in Germany are interviewed. The survey defines founders broadly, including full-time and part-time start-ups, freelance and commercial activities, new foundations and business takeovers. KfW states that the Gruendungsmonitor is a scientific dataset for macroeconomic analysis of start-up activity and is available to external researchers under access conditions.

The report warns that 2024 start-up activity is likely to be burdened by weaker planning activity in 2023. The active start-up planning rate among the population aged 18 to 64 fell from 4.5 percent in 2022 to 3.6 percent in 2023, the lowest value reported in the series. The share with likely realization within the next 12 months fell to 2.2 percent. Since planning activity typically signals next-year formation activity, KfW treats this as a negative leading indicator.

Founder composition and motivation are central themes. The share of female founders rose to 44 percent in 2023, a high value in the series, though the report cautions that the founder share fluctuates and shows no clear long-run trend. Entrepreneurial role models matter: people with self-employed or entrepreneurial contacts and identification figures are more likely to realize start-up plans, and the report suggests that the role-model effect is especially important for women and younger founders. The report also states that only 24 percent of adults up to age 67 would generally prefer self-employment to employment, showing that self-employment preference remains low.

The structure of start-ups is described as stable but with some shifts. New foundations dominate over takeovers or participations: new foundations reached 87 percent of all business formations in 2023, which the report identifies as another high point. This creates a succession problem for SMEs because takeovers and participations account for only a small part of formation activity while demographic aging increases the need for business succession. Solo start-ups remain common, but the share of solo self-employed start-ups has declined and the employer share among all start-ups rose to 39 percent in 2023.

The sector structure remains service-heavy. In 2023, 69 percent of start-ups were in services, 22 percent in trade, and 9 percent in the producing sector. Digital and internet-based business models remain important but no longer show the temporary post-pandemic acceleration. In 2023, more than four in ten start-ups were digital or internet-based, and 22 percent were both.

The report also covers perceived location conditions and barriers. Founders rated Germany somewhat less critically in 2023 than in 2022, but several conditions still received weak marks, especially political engagement, legal regulation, tax burden, bureaucracy, and the education system. Among people without self-employment experience and without start-up plans, the top barriers include financial risks, workplace-security concerns, bureaucracy, and financing problems.

## Research question

The report asks how start-up activity in Germany developed in 2023, what the leading indicators imply for 2024, how founder composition and motivation changed, and which barriers and framework conditions shape the willingness to become self-employed.

## Core argument or contribution

The report's contribution is a broad empirical snapshot of German entrepreneurship. It argues that start-up activity remains weak because macroeconomic impulses are missing, planning activity has fallen sharply, self-employment preference is low, and major barriers around risk, bureaucracy, and finance persist.

## Methodology

The report is based on representative annual population surveys of 50,000 randomly selected residents in Germany. It reports descriptive time-series and cross-sectional statistics on founders, planners, self-employment preference, motives, sectors, digital/internet-based business models, role models, location assessments, and barriers.

## Datasets/materials used

- KfW-Gruendungsmonitor representative population survey.
- Annual interviews with 50,000 randomly selected residents in Germany.
- Supplementary founder-platform survey evidence is used for some location-factor assessments.

## Key findings

- Total business formations rose to 568,000 in 2023, up 3 percent from 2022.
- Full-time start-ups fell to 205,000, down 8 percent.
- Part-time start-ups rose to 363,000, up 11 percent.
- Active planning activity fell from 4.5 percent to 3.6 percent of the population aged 18 to 64.
- Only 2.2 percent reported start-up plans likely to be realized within the next 12 months.
- Female founders accounted for 44 percent of start-up activity in 2023.
- New foundations accounted for 87 percent of business formations, while takeovers and participations remained small.
- Services dominated the sector mix at 69 percent of start-ups; trade accounted for 22 percent and the producing sector for 9 percent.
- More than four in ten start-ups were digital or internet-based, with 22 percent both digital and internet-based.
- The main barriers to self-employment include financial risk, job-security concerns, bureaucracy, and financing problems.

## Limitations

- The report is descriptive and does not causally estimate the effect of policy, macroeconomic conditions, or role models.
- The survey captures broad business formations, not only high-growth, technology-intensive, or science-based start-ups.
- Some location-factor assessments come from a founder-platform survey and may not represent all founders equally.
- The source should not be used as direct evidence on European peer comparisons or high-tech start-up deficits unless those claims are separately sourced.

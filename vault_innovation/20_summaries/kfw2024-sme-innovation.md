## Detailed summary

The KfW SME Innovation Report 2024 describes recent innovation activity in Germany's small and medium-sized enterprises. Its headline result is that the share of innovators fell by one percentage point from the previous survey to 39 percent. This corresponds to just under 1.5 million innovative SMEs. KfW links the latest decline to weak economic conditions in 2023, including deteriorating business climate, weaker turnover growth, and lower profit margins.

The medium-term pattern is more important than the one-year movement. Since the 2018-2020 period, the share of innovators has fallen by three percentage points. The report identifies very small firms as a key driver: enterprises with fewer than five employees have continuously produced fewer innovations, and small businesses with fewer than ten employees were responsible for the latest decline. At the same time, the share of innovators among larger SMEs with ten or more employees increased.

The report stresses that innovation is broader than formal R&D. It uses the newer OECD innovation definition, which includes product, process, marketing, and organizational innovation, and warns that the definition change limits direct comparison with older survey periods. For SMEs, innovation often comes from normal production processes, cooperation with customers or suppliers, adoption and adaptation of existing solutions, and learning by doing, using, and interacting. These non-R&D innovations are often incremental or imitative, but they matter for diffusion and economy-wide competitiveness.

Innovation expenditure tells a different story from the innovator share. Total SME innovation expenditure remained roughly stable at just under EUR 34 billion, even though fewer firms were innovating. KfW interprets this as concentration: 2 percent of the largest medium-sized enterprises account for 56 percent of SME innovation expenditure, while firms with fewer than five employees make up 81 percent of SMEs but account for only 13 percent of innovation expenditure. Large SMEs with 50 or more employees increased innovation expenditure to just under EUR 19 billion; smaller SMEs reduced theirs slightly.

R&D activity is rare but important. In the 2021-2023 period, 4 percent of SMEs conducted continuous R&D and another 4 percent conducted occasional R&D, totaling just over 300,000 SMEs. Among innovative SMEs, only 20 percent conduct their own R&D, while 80 percent innovate without own R&D. Larger SMEs are much more likely to conduct R&D than very small firms: 31 percent of firms with 50 or more employees conduct own R&D compared with 7 percent of firms with fewer than five employees. R&D-intensive manufacturing SMEs are the most R&D-active sector, with 28 percent conducting own R&D, but the report notes a continuing decline in R&D activity in this segment.

The policy discussion distinguishes frontier innovation from broad-based innovation. KfW argues that finance barriers affect all SMEs, but support needs to differ by innovation mode. R&D support is appropriate for pioneering businesses, while the broad SME sector often needs support below the formal R&D threshold, for example expenditure on product or service design. Other key barriers are skilled-labour shortages, insufficient digital and soft skills, weak internal learning and knowledge-flow practices, and limited strategic orientation. Firms without pronounced strategic orientation are described as unlikely to innovate.

## Research question

How has innovation activity among German SMEs developed, which firm groups are retreating or expanding, how concentrated is innovation expenditure, and what policy bottlenecks constrain broad-based SME innovation?

## Core argument or contribution

The report argues that Germany's SME innovation base is narrowing. Innovation spending has not collapsed, but fewer firms are innovating, and the activity is increasingly concentrated in larger SMEs. This threatens diffusion, productivity growth, and transformative capacity because much SME innovation occurs outside formal R&D and across the broad business base.

## Methodology

The report uses descriptive analysis of the KfW SME Panel. It reports extrapolated shares of innovators, innovation expenditure, and R&D activity by firm size, sector, sales region, and R&D status under the newer OECD innovation definition.

## Datasets/materials used

- KfW SME Panel.
- KfW-ifo SME Barometer and KfW SME Panel context indicators are referenced for business-cycle interpretation.
- Innovation expenditure is reported under the OECD definition and includes marketing and organizational innovation expenditure.

## Key findings

- The SME innovator share fell to 39 percent.
- The share of innovators has fallen by three percentage points since the 2018-2020 period.
- The latest decline is concentrated among firms with fewer than ten employees.
- Firms with fewer than five employees have reduced innovation activity by four percentage points since the 2018-2020 period.
- Total SME innovation expenditure remained just under EUR 34 billion.
- Innovation expenditure is highly concentrated: firms with 50 or more employees represent 2 percent of SMEs but account for 56 percent of SME innovation expenditure.
- Firms with fewer than five employees represent 81 percent of SMEs but account for 13 percent of SME innovation expenditure.
- Only 8 percent of SMEs conduct own R&D, split evenly between continuous and occasional R&D.
- Eighty percent of innovative SMEs innovate without own R&D.
- R&D-active SMEs produce innovations more often and are much more likely to introduce new-to-market products.

## Limitations

- The report is descriptive and should not be used as causal evidence that specific policies or macroeconomic shocks caused the decline.
- The newer OECD innovation definition means direct comparisons with pre-2018/2020 measurements are limited.
- The report focuses on SMEs and does not cover large-enterprise innovation expenditure except as context.
- Innovation expenditure is nominal unless otherwise specified; inflation and price effects matter for interpretation.

## Relevance to TRL and incremental innovation bias (added 2026-05-11)

The TRL research briefing (`explorations/trl_research_briefing.md`, Section 2.2) uses KfW (2024) as the primary empirical anchor for two specific statistics documenting Germany's structural incremental innovation bias:

- **82% of German SMEs innovate without their own R&D department** — confirming that most SME innovation is process improvement or product adaptation, not exploratory R&D
- **Only ~7% of German SMEs generate genuinely new-to-market products** — this is the key figure for the radical vs. incremental diagnosis in RQ2 of the project

The 7% figure quantifies Germany's radical innovation deficit at the SME level. It implies that if the Förderprojekte portfolio is concentrated at High TRL, this reflects a system in which the vast majority of innovations are incremental — and public support follows rather than corrects that structural orientation.

These two statistics constitute one of three empirical pillars in the briefing's innovation paradox narrative: pillar (b) is Germany's incremental innovation dominance, documented here at the SME level. Pillar (a) is large-firm dominance in grant receipt (Aschhoff 2010). Pillar (c) is the expected TRL concentration at near-market stages (pending Förderprojekte classification).

The briefing also cites this paper alongside the OECD (2022) Innovation Policy Review Germany for the claim that "Germany's documented middle-tech trap" includes incremental SME innovation as a structural feature, consistent with the Mitteltechnologie-Falle concept ([[30_concepts/mitteltechnologie-falle.md]]).

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For [[70_projects/bundesinnovationshaushalt-trl.md]], this source is important for distinguishing two policy targets: high-novelty R&D-active SMEs and the much larger set of non-R&D SME innovators that need diffusion, design, management, skill, and learning support. Project retrieval should therefore avoid equating "innovation" only with formal R&D or high TRL product development.

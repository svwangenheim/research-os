## Detailed summary

The EIB Energy Transition Finance Report (2022) analyzes investment flows and financing gaps in European clean energy technology. Its key finding for the Foerderkatalog project is the identification of a specific valley of death at **TRL 5–6** in the European energy technology development pipeline. TRL 5 corresponds to "technology validated in relevant environment" and TRL 6 to "technology demonstrated in relevant environment" — these are the stages where laboratory demonstration has been achieved but the technology has not crossed the threshold to commercial-scale operation.

The report finds that this TRL 5–6 gap is the most acute investment gap in European clean energy:
- At TRL 1–4, public research funding (Horizon Europe, national basic research programmes) provides adequate coverage.
- At TRL 7–9, private investment, development banks, and infrastructure finance can assess risk and deploy capital.
- At TRL 5–6, technology has moved beyond what public research funds typically support but has not reached the maturity where private capital can assess risk. The gap is specifically about technologies that work at laboratory scale but whose performance at operational scale is uncertain.

The report uses a four-group TRL scheme providing finer resolution in the deployment zone:
- **Research (TRL 1–3):** basic to proof-of-concept research
- **Development (TRL 3–6):** laboratory to relevant-environment validation
- **Innovation (TRL 6–8):** relevant-environment demonstration to qualified systems
- **Production Support (TRL 9):** fully commercial systems

The EIB identifies this four-group scheme as useful for energy-technology portfolio analysis, particularly for distinguishing legitimate first-of-kind demonstrations (TRL 7–8, the "Innovation" group) from fully commercial production (TRL 9, Production Support). This distinction matters for evaluating Germany's Reallabore and first-of-kind clean energy deployment programmes: TRL 7–8 demonstration projects have a public support rationale because they are genuinely first-of-kind and involve unresolvable private risk, while TRL 9 projects that are commercial-standard are harder to justify on market-failure grounds.

Germany's Reallabore (regulatory sandboxes), offshore wind demonstration projects, and hydrogen electrolyser programs operate primarily at TRL 7–8 under EIB's scheme — the "Innovation" category that the report treats as legitimate public-finance territory. Serial, non-first-of-kind technology subsidies occupying the same TRL 7–8 space would be more questionable from a market-failure perspective.

## Research question

Where are the critical investment gaps in the European clean energy technology pipeline, and how should public finance institutions (EIB) respond to the valley of death at TRL 5–6?

## Core argument or contribution

The report identifies TRL 5–6 as the most acute clean energy investment gap in Europe. Public development-bank financing should specifically target this TRL zone — above what public research programmes fund, below what private capital can reach. The four-group TRL scheme (including a distinct "Innovation" group at TRL 6–8) allows distinguishing legitimate demonstration support from near-commercial production subsidies.

## Methodology

Finance and investment analysis report. Combines EIB portfolio data, European energy technology pipeline assessments, and policy diagnosis. Not an econometric study; no causal identification design.

## Datasets/materials used

- Energy technology investment data from IEA, IRENA, and EIB tracking systems

## Key findings

- The valley of death in European clean energy is most acute at **TRL 5–6** (technology validated in relevant environment but not demonstrated at operational scale)
- TRL 1–4 is covered by public research funding; TRL 7–9 can access private capital; TRL 5–6 is the gap where neither source reliably covers costs
- Four-group TRL scheme: Research (1–3) / Development (3–6) / Innovation (6–8) / Production Support (9)
- First-of-kind demonstrations at TRL 7–8 (the "Innovation" category) are legitimate EIB/public-finance targets
- Serial production subsidies at TRL 9 (Production Support) are harder to justify on market-failure grounds
- Germany's Reallabore and energy transition demonstration programmes operate primarily in the TRL 7–8 Innovation zone

## Limitations

- Covers clean energy technologies specifically; TRL 5–6 valley-of-death finding may not generalize to other sectors
- Finance report, not a causal evaluation; no counterfactual analysis of without-EIB scenarios
- The four-group scheme differs from the three-group Scheme 1 used in the Foerderkatalog project; the Development group (TRL 3–6) spans the Low/Mid boundary in the three-group scheme

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For [[70_projects/bundesinnovationshaushalt-trl.md]], the EIB report provides two calibration points:

1. The valley of death is most acute at TRL 5–6, not at TRL 4–6 as a whole. If the Förderprojekte portfolio shows concentration at Mid TRL (4–6), the key question is whether this is driven by TRL 5–6 projects (genuine valley-of-death bridging) or by TRL 3–4 applied research. The three-group classifier cannot distinguish these; programme-level labeling can help.

2. The first-of-kind / serial distinction (EIB's Innovation vs. Production Support) is critical for interpreting High TRL concentration in BMWK energy programmes. A Reallabore project at TRL 8 is not the same as serial production subsidies. Programme names ("Reallabor," "Demonstrationsanlage," "Investitionszuschuss") can help distinguish in the Förderprojekte data.

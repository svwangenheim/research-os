## Detailed summary

This OECD working paper provides the authoritative taxonomy for classifying economic sectors by research and development intensity. It updates earlier OECD technology classifications (ISIC Rev. 2-3) with a comprehensive framework based on ISIC Revision 4, covering manufacturing and non-manufacturing for the first time.

**Core definition:** R&D intensity = business R&D expenditure / gross value added (GVA), measured at the industry level. The 2016 taxonomy explicitly rejects the notion that R&D intensity alone captures "technology" or broader innovation; instead, it provides a practical, internationally comparable discriminant for sectoral clustering.

**Five-category empirical classification:**
- **High R&D intensity:** Air/spacecraft, pharmaceuticals, computer/electronic/optical products, software publishing, scientific research (R&D intensity ~18–31%)
- **Medium-high R&D intensity:** Motor vehicles, machinery, electrical equipment, weapons (~9–15%)
- **Medium R&D intensity:** Fabricated metals, chemicals, rubber/plastics (~6–7%)
- **Medium-low R&D intensity:** Textiles, minerals, paper (~2–3%)
- **Low R&D intensity:** Food/beverages, leather, wood, furniture (<2%)

**Key innovation vs. prior OECD work:** The paper rejects predetermined percentage thresholds (e.g., 1980s rule: 1–4% = mid-tech, >4% = high-tech). Instead, groups are determined empirically from clustering, making the classification flexible but less mechanically applicable.

**Services inclusion:** For the first time, the taxonomy systematically covers non-manufacturing. Most services are low R&D intensity, but information/communication services, professional/scientific/technical activities, and publishing emerge as R&D-intensive sectors.

## Research question

How can economic activities be systematically classified by R&D intensity in a way that is comparable across OECD countries and covers both manufacturing and services?

## Core argument or contribution

Three-part contribution: (1) establish R&D intensity (R&D/GVA) as the sole criterion for five-category sectoral classification valid across OECD; (2) update the classification to ISIC Rev. 4 to capture structural shifts (software becoming a distinct high-tech sector); (3) extend the classification for the first time to non-manufacturing industries.

## Methodology

- **Definition:** R&D intensity = business R&D / GVA at industry level
- **Data:** OECD ANBERD database (2011 reference year, 30+ OECD countries and partner economies)
- **Classification:** ISIC Rev. 4 at 2-digit and 3-digit levels
- **Robustness:** Alternative measurements tested (gross output vs. GVA, SNA 2008 vs. 1993, median vs. weighted mean)
- **No causal estimation;** purely descriptive clustering

## Datasets/materials used

- OECD ANBERD (Analytical Business Enterprise R&D) database
- OECD National Accounts (SNA 1993 and SNA 2008)
- ISIC Rev. 4 industrial classifications

## Key findings

1. **Five-group empirical taxonomy:** Manufacturing clusters in high/medium; non-manufacturing shows wider scatter.

2. **High-tech manufacturing stability:** Pharmaceuticals (24%), electronics (19%), chemicals, machinery maintain R&D-intensive status. Weapons manufacturing uniquely positioned in medium-high.

3. **Emerging service sectors:** Software publishing (28%), scientific research (31.7%), IT/information services rank among the highest R&D-intensive sectors globally.

4. **Services heterogeneity:** Information/communication and professional/scientific/technical activities are R&D-intensive; most other services are low R&D intensity.

5. **Country variation:** Substantial within-industry heterogeneity across countries. A sector can rank differently depending on national R&D specialization—indicating room for countries to increase R&D intensity in their industries.

6. **Classification robustness:** Alternative measurement approaches produce stable groupings. The boundary between medium and medium-low R&D intensity is least stable.

7. **Consistency across time:** For manufacturing, 2016 taxonomy aligns with earlier OECD classifications (ISIC Rev. 3), demonstrating stability over 20+ years.

## Limitations

- R&D intensity is an imperfect proxy for technological sophistication, innovation capacity, or broader knowledge intensity
- Does not capture indirect R&D embodied in purchased inputs (input-output tables for ISIC Rev. 4 not available)
- 2011 reference year chosen for data availability, not to capture latest trends
- Within-country sectoral heterogeneity not visible in aggregate rankings
- Focuses on business R&D; excludes public research sector

## Implications for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project

1. **Sectoral classification:** The five-group OECD taxonomy directly applies to classify Förderkatalog projects. German strengths in automotive, machinery, chemicals map to medium-high R&D intensity; software and scientific research to high.

2. **Definition for analysis:** R&D intensity operationalizes the mid-tech vs. high-tech distinction. Sectors >15% R&D intensity support breakthrough innovation; 6–10% support incremental improvement.

3. **Benchmarking:** Country-by-country R&D intensity data enables comparison of German automotive/machinery intensity vs. USA, France, Japan.

4. **Policy implication:** If the Förderkatalog concentrates funding in medium-high sectors (automotive, machinery) at high R&D intensities within those sectors, it may reinforce rather than escape the middle-technology trap.

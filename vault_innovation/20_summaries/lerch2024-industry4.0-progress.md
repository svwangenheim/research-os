## Detailed summary

Lerch, Jaeger, and Horvat analyze whether German manufacturing continued to make progress in Industry 4.0 after the strong diffusion phase observed between 2015 and 2018. Earlier Fraunhofer ISI reports found a digital divide between small and large firms in 2015, followed by catch-up by small and medium-sized firms by 2018. This 2024 report asks whether that broad-based progress continued through 2022.

The study uses the Fraunhofer ISI survey "Modernisierung der Produktion" for 2015, 2018, and 2022 and introduces results from the ISI industry panel linking firms across waves. The 2022 survey covers all manufacturing sectors; 14,045 firms were contacted and 1,334 usable questionnaires were returned. For the linked panel analysis, the report uses smaller repeated-observation subsets: 2015-2018, 2018-2022, and 2015-2022.

The central measurement tool is the Fraunhofer ISI I4.0 Readiness Index, first developed in 2015. It is based on seven digital production technologies grouped into three technology fields: digital management systems, wireless human-machine interaction, and cyber-physical-system-related processes. The index distinguishes non-users, basic users, and advanced users, and gives an index value from 0 to 5.

The main finding is a marked slowdown. The I4.0 index value for German manufacturing is 2.2 in 2022, compared with 2.1 in 2018 and 1.8 in 2015. Expressed relative to the 2015 baseline, the report states that digital progress was 12 percent between 2015 and 2018 but only 5 percent between 2018 and 2022. The earlier period was driven by broad entry into basic I4.0 applications, especially among small firms and previously less digitized industries. The later period was much more concentrated: small and medium-sized firms improved only slightly, while large firms continued to expand their digital production capabilities.

The firm-size pattern is a core result. In 2022, more than two thirds of large firms are classified as I4.0 advanced users, while the share of basic users among large firms has continuously declined and the share of non-users has almost disappeared. Among SMEs, by contrast, post-2018 progress is weaker and reversals are more visible. Panel evidence shows that from 2015 to 2018, about 31 percent of firms improved their I4.0 readiness and 11 percent declined, producing a net progress of 20 percentage points. From 2018 to 2022, only 25 percent improved, while regressions were much more frequent and net progress fell to 5 percentage points.

The report argues that the slowdown is linked to both structural characteristics and economic conditions. COVID-19 disruptions and weaker investment capacity are presented as plausible explanations for why many SMEs invested less in modernization after 2018. Firms that already had above-average productivity and sales development in 2015 were more likely to increase I4.0 readiness by 2022, suggesting that financial strength and economic performance enabled continued digital modernization.

The conclusion is not that Industry 4.0 stopped progressing, but that progress became less broad-based. Large firms and certain supplier or equipment-related sectors continue to advance, while many smaller firms struggle to implement and maintain I4.0 technologies over time. The policy implication is that Germany should not only support frontier applications and advanced technologies; it also needs measures that spread basic applications across the manufacturing base and help firms sustain modernization investments.

## Research question

How did Industry 4.0 readiness in German manufacturing develop between 2015 and 2022, and which firm groups, sectors, and economic conditions explain the slowdown after 2018?

## Core argument or contribution

The report contributes longitudinal evidence on Industry 4.0 diffusion in German manufacturing. It shows that the broad catch-up dynamic observed between 2015 and 2018 weakened after 2018, with I4.0 progress increasingly concentrated among larger and economically stronger firms.

## Methodology

- Data source: Fraunhofer ISI "Modernisierung der Produktion" surveys for 2015, 2018, and 2022.
- Panel source: ISI industry panel linking subsets of firms across waves.
- Measurement: I4.0 Readiness Index based on seven digital production technologies across three technology fields.
- Groups: non-users, basic users, and advanced users.
- Comparisons: 2015-2018, 2018-2022, and 2015-2022 trends by firm size, sector, value-chain position, and economic situation.
- Evidence type: descriptive survey and panel analysis; not a causal identification design.

## Datasets/materials used

- Fraunhofer ISI "Modernisierung der Produktion" surveys, 2015, 2018, and 2022.
- 2022 survey: 14,045 contacted manufacturing firms and 1,334 usable responses.
- ISI industry panel subsets linking repeated observations across waves.
- I4.0 Readiness Index indicators covering seven digital production technologies.

## Key findings

- The I4.0 index value rose from 1.8 in 2015 to 2.1 in 2018 and 2.2 in 2022.
- Relative progress slowed from 12 percent in 2015-2018 to 5 percent in 2018-2022.
- The 2015-2018 phase was driven by broad adoption of basic I4.0 applications, especially by small firms and less digitized sectors.
- After 2018, progress was concentrated among larger firms; SMEs improved only slightly and more often showed stagnation or decline.
- In 2022, 68 percent of large firms are reported as I4.0 advanced users.
- Panel evidence shows net I4.0 progress falling from 20 percentage points in 2015-2018 to 5 percentage points in 2018-2022.
- For large firms, the report finds no comparable reversal; for small and medium-sized firms, a relevant share reduced I4.0 readiness after 2018.
- Firms with stronger productivity and sales development in 2015 were more likely to increase I4.0 readiness by 2022.
- The report links the slowdown partly to COVID-19 disruptions, investment constraints, and the difficulty of integrating I4.0 technologies profitably over time.

## Limitations

- The analysis is descriptive and does not identify causal effects of I4.0 adoption on performance.
- The panel subsamples are small and are not presented as fully representative of the entire manufacturing population.
- I4.0 readiness is measured through a technology-adoption index, not through direct productivity or innovation-output effects.
- The source focuses on German manufacturing and should not be generalized to all sectors.
- Some interpretations about COVID-19 and investment constraints are plausible explanations, not causal estimates.
- The report does not classify public funding projects, TRLs, or policy-program effectiveness.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt/TRL project, this source is relevant as background on where German manufacturing modernization bottlenecks appear. It supports retrieval for questions about diffusion, SME capability constraints, and whether innovation policy should focus only on frontier technologies or also on broad adoption of basic production technologies.

It does not provide a method for classifying project abstracts or estimating TRLs. The project-level implication is substantive: a funding portfolio focused only on high-end frontier applications could miss the broad adoption bottleneck that this report identifies.

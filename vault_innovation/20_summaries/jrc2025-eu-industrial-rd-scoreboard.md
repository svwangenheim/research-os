## Detailed summary

The 2025 EU Industrial R&D Investment Scoreboard is the annual flagship publication monitoring corporate R&D investment globally, with a focus on EU competitiveness. It covers the top 2,000 firms by R&D investment (>90% of business-funded R&D globally) based on audited financial accounts, plus a deeper EU 800 sample. This edition adds new analyses linking company performance to European Innovation Scoreboard (EIS) country groups and novel size-class breakdowns (SMEs, small mid-caps, mid-caps, large).

**Global R&D dynamics (2024):** Global corporate R&D grew 6.3% nominally to EUR 1,446 billion. Growth was led by ROW companies (8.1%), followed by the US (7.8%) and Japan (7.1%). EU companies grew only 2.9% — the lowest since COVID-19 — and Chinese companies grew 3.9% (slowest on record for China).

**Sectoral concentration:** Four sectors dominate (>80% of total R&D): ICT software (24.9%), ICT hardware (22.0%), health (19.9%), and automotive (13.6%). US firms dominate ICT software, ICT hardware, and health. EU firms maintain leadership only in automotive (EUR 87 bn in 2024, more than twice US or Japanese firms), but EU automotive R&D growth was only 0.8% vs. China's 11.9% and Japan's 12.3%. EU ICT software fell 8.9% — a structural weakness as this was the fastest-growing sector globally over the past decade.

Positive exceptions: EU electricity/renewables R&D up 19.8%; aerospace and defence up 4.8%; health up 13% (significantly above US 7.1%, Japan 9.1%, China 0.1%).

**Rising concentration at the top:** The top 5 (US tech: Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Apple) now account for ~15% of all Scoreboard R&D — nearly double their 2014 share. Amazon tops the 2025 ranking (EUR 65.3 bn; included for the first time). Volkswagen is the largest EU firm at rank 8 (EUR 20.9 bn). Top 50 share increased more modestly (+1.5 pp to 43%).

**EU 800 by EIS country group:** Companies in EIS innovation leader countries (Denmark, Finland, Netherlands, Sweden) achieved 6.8% annual R&D growth over 2014–2024, vs. 4.6% for strong innovators (including Germany, France) and 2.5% for moderate innovators. Framework conditions in leader countries favor the creation and scaling of small, R&D-intensive firms. Moderate innovator countries see fewer R&D-intensive entrants and lower R&D investment per employee among incumbents.

**R&D intensity gap:** US R&D intensity (R&D/net sales) is 8.4% vs. EU 4.4%, Japan 4.2%, China 4.0%, ROW 3.8%. US R&D per employee is EUR 48,700 vs. EU EUR 16,800. Sectoral specialization explains most of this gap.

**R&D internationalisation:** EU multinationals increased R&D abroad by 65% (2013–2022) but the EU's share of US R&D abroad fell by ~25% to 30.6%. EU multinational affiliates in the US account for 14.3% of EU corporate R&D, while US affiliates in the EU account for only 3.9% of US corporate R&D — EU runs a growing R&D investment deficit with the US.

**Green technology patents:** EU leads globally (alongside Japan) in cumulative green patent filings, with specialization in energy-intensive industries, circularity, and clean transport (second to Japan). The EU underperforms in twin transition (digital + green combined). Germany and France dominate green patents within EU; Sweden, Finland, and Ireland show strength in twin transition.

## Research question

How is corporate R&D investment distributed globally, by company, sector, and geography? How do EU companies compare to US, Chinese, and Japanese competitors? What explains performance gaps?

## Core argument or contribution

EU corporate R&D growth has slowed structurally. The gap with the US reflects sectoral specialization (automotive/manufacturing vs. ICT/health) more than overall effort. Within the EU, innovation leader countries outperform strong/moderate innovators because framework conditions support R&D-intensive entrants, not only incumbents. R&D concentration is rising at the global top (US tech), while EU faces structural challenges in digital sovereignty.

## Methodology

- Annual collection of audited financial accounts (R&D investment, net sales, capital expenditure, employment, market capitalization, operating profits) for top 2,000 global R&D investors
- Country-of-headquarters classification; EIS country group overlay for EU 800
- New size class analysis (SMEs, small mid-caps, mid-caps, large)
- R&D internationalisation: BEA data on US MNE affiliates abroad (2013–2022); Eurostat FATS data for EU multinationals
- Green patent analysis: PATSTAT; four technology areas aligned with decarbonization agenda

## Datasets/materials used

- Alepro company financial data (audited accounts for top 2,000 firms)
- European Innovation Scoreboard (EIS) country groups
- BEA (Bureau of Economic Analysis) data on R&D investment flows of US MNEs
- PATSTAT patent data (2021 as latest reliable year for green patents)
- Eurostat FATS (Foreign Affiliates Statistics)

## Key findings

1. EU corporate R&D grew only 2.9% in 2024 — lowest since COVID-19; structural slowdown.
2. US dominates high-growth, high-intensity sectors (ICT software, health); EU locked into automotive with slower growth.
3. EU ICT software R&D fell 8.9% — acute structural weakness in digital sovereignty.
4. Positive: EU energy/renewables (+19.8%), health (+13%), aerospace/defence (+4.8%) all outperformed.
5. Top 5 US tech firms now account for ~15% of global top-2,000 R&D; concentration intensifying.
6. EU Innovation Leaders (DK, FI, NL, SE) outgrow Strong Innovators (Germany/France) 6.8% vs. 4.6% annually — framework conditions favor R&D-intensive entrants in leader countries.
7. EU R&D intensity gap with US (4.4% vs. 8.4%) explained largely by sectoral specialization.
8. EU runs growing R&D investment deficit with the US; EU multinationals relocate more R&D to US (14.3% share) than US firms to EU (3.9%).
9. EU leads green patents but lags twin transition — risks becoming a green-but-not-digital economy.

## Limitations

- Based on audited accounts — lags actual R&D by ~1 year; very recent investments not captured.
- Patent data for 2022–2024 incomplete due to publication lags in the patent process.
- Country-of-headquarters classification may misattribute R&D of multinationals.
- R&D internationalisation data (BEA/FATS) only available through 2022.
- Does not cover public/academic R&D; captures only corporate R&D.

## Implications for the project domain

**Moderate-high relevance** for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt TRL project:

1. **International comparison benchmark**: Confirms Germany's status as a Strong Innovator (not a Leader) in corporate R&D performance. Innovation leader countries generate more R&D-intensive entrants — consistent with the thesis that framework conditions shape entrant vs. incumbent patterns.

2. **Sectoral lock-in evidence**: EU/German corporate R&D concentrated in automotive/manufacturing while US leads in ICT software — directly consistent with the project's hypothesis that German federal funding may reinforce incumbent-dominated, mature-technology sectors.

3. **Competitiveness Compass context**: Provides the EU policy framing under which German federal innovation policy is being evaluated (2025 policy landscape).

4. **EIS–Scoreboard integration**: New EIS country group analysis connects macro country-level benchmarking with firm-level R&D data — useful for framing "why does Germany underperform leaders?" in the policy brief.

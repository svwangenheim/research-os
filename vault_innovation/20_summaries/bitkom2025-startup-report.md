## Detailed summary

The Bitkom Startup Report 2025 documents how German tech startups perceive financing conditions, regulatory burdens, staffing constraints, technology adoption, and policy needs in 2025. It is best used as a current ecosystem survey rather than as causal evidence about the effects of subsidies or regulation.

The report's central empirical message is that German startups see their operating environment as difficult even while many individual firms still report growth ambitions. Forty-one percent of respondents say the general startup situation in Germany has worsened over the previous two years, while 21 percent see improvement. The three largest reported challenges are bureaucracy, cited by 63 percent; sales and customer acquisition, cited by 61 percent; and financing or capital acquisition, cited by 52 percent. Public-procurement access is also a major issue, cited by 39 percent.

Financing is treated as a growth bottleneck rather than as a general absence of entrepreneurial intent. Among startups with capital needs, the average expected venture-capital requirement for the next two years is 2.5 million euros, close to the 2024 survey value of 2.7 million euros. Seventy-nine percent of startups with capital needs consider it likely that they will raise capital in the next two years. The report also notes that German startups raised about 7.4 billion euros across 1,407 financing rounds in 2024, while respondents nevertheless perceive investors as more cautious.

The report describes startups as technology-intensive, especially in artificial intelligence. Eighty-two percent of surveyed startups use AI, with 63 percent using it for internal business operations and 62 percent integrating it into their product offering. Among AI-using startups, 87 percent use generative AI; 71 percent use generative AI internally and 54 percent use it in products. Cloud computing and big data/analytics are also widely used, while technologies such as IoT, 5G, robotics, autonomous driving, and quantum computing are less widespread or more often only planned.

Policy and regulation are a second major theme. Sixty-three percent of respondents agree that excessive regulation is a reason why products such as ChatGPT were not developed in the EU. Forty-five percent expect the AI Act to restrict their startup's use or development of AI, and 43 percent expect an AI-related competitive disadvantage relative to firms from the United States or China. The report therefore frames regulation not merely as compliance cost, but as a perceived competitiveness issue for digital and AI startups.

The policy section reports strong support for bureaucracy reduction, easier early-stage administration, simpler public procurement, more startup-Mittelstand collaboration, stronger venture-capital channels, employee equity improvements, easier skilled migration, better data access, and improved transfer infrastructure at universities and research institutions. For example, 96 percent rate bureaucracy reduction as very or rather important, 87 percent support temporary relief from legal obligations and bureaucracy in the first two years after founding, 91 percent rate simplified public-procurement access as very or rather important, and 86 percent support stronger funding programs for cooperation between startups and Mittelstand.

The report also covers team composition and diversity. The average startup employs about 13 people, with three out of four startups planning new hires and an average planned increase of 5.7 employees among those expecting staff growth. Women remain underrepresented: the average female-founder count is about 0.4-0.5 per founding team, and 38 percent of surveyed startups have no woman among the founders.

## Research question

The report asks how German tech startups assess their financing conditions, business and regulatory challenges, technology use, staffing situation, diversity, and desired policy measures in 2025.

## Core argument or contribution

The source contributes a current, non-representative survey picture of German tech-startup constraints. It argues that startups remain growth- and technology-oriented, but face a combination of bureaucracy, market-access barriers, financing needs, regulatory uncertainty, and talent/diversity constraints that policy should address if Germany wants a stronger startup ecosystem.

## Methodology

Bitkom Research conducted an online survey of founders of German tech startups that are members of Bitkom's Get Started initiative. The 2025 sample contains 152 respondents and was fielded in calendar weeks 12-21 of 2025. The report compares some indicators with earlier Bitkom startup surveys from 2023 and 2024 and with Bitkom surveys of the broader German economy, but the 2025 startup survey is explicitly non-representative.

This is descriptive survey evidence. It does not estimate causal effects of regulation, subsidy programs, procurement, venture capital, or AI adoption.

## Key findings

- Forty-one percent of surveyed startups say the general startup situation in Germany has worsened over the previous two years; 21 percent say it improved.
- The largest reported challenges are bureaucracy (63 percent), sales and customer acquisition (61 percent), financing/capital acquisition (52 percent), scaling and growth (39 percent), and access to public contracts (39 percent).
- Among startups with capital needs, the average expected venture-capital requirement for the next two years is 2.5 million euros; 79 percent consider it likely that they will raise capital in that period.
- The average startup employs about 13 people; three quarters planned new hires at the time of the survey, with average planned employment growth of 5.7 employees among firms expecting staff growth.
- Women remain underrepresented in founding teams: average female-founder participation is about 0.4-0.5 per team, and 38 percent of startups have no woman founder.
- Eighty-two percent of surveyed startups use AI; 63 percent use AI internally and 62 percent include it in products. Among startups using AI, 87 percent use generative AI.
- Respondents are concerned about AI regulation: 63 percent link excessive regulation to the absence of ChatGPT-like EU products, 45 percent expect AI Act restrictions, and 43 percent expect AI Act-related competitive disadvantages relative to US or Chinese competitors.
- Policy measures with especially high support include bureaucracy reduction, simplified procurement access, temporary relief from early-stage legal/bureaucratic obligations, startup-Mittelstand cooperation funding, and stronger venture-capital channels through instruments such as the Zukunftsfonds and WIN initiative.

## Limitations

- The startup survey is not representative and should not be generalized to all German startups without caution.
- The respondent group consists of founders from German tech startups connected to Bitkom's Get Started initiative, which may overrepresent digitally oriented firms.
- Findings are self-reported perceptions and expectations, not administrative measurements or causal estimates.
- The source does not evaluate a specific subsidy program, does not classify projects by TRL, and does not analyze Foerderkatalog allocation data.
- Policy preferences in the report reflect respondent opinions and Bitkom's interpretation, not demonstrated policy impacts.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt/TRL project, this source is useful as contextual evidence that German tech startups perceive financing, bureaucracy, procurement access, regulation, and transfer infrastructure as binding constraints. It supports adding startup-facing retrieval terms and concept links around venture-capital needs, public-procurement access, startup-Mittelstand cooperation, AI adoption, and commercialization barriers.

It should not be used as direct evidence that specific Foerderkatalog allocations are mis-targeted, that TRL 3-5 is the correct funding range, or that incumbents are crowding out startups unless those claims are supported by other sources. The defensible use is narrower: Bitkom reports that surveyed startups see capital acquisition, bureaucracy, public procurement, and AI regulation as important constraints.

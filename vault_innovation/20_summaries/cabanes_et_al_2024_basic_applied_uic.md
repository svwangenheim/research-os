## Detailed summary

The standard narrative about university–industry collaboration (UIC) assumes that industry partners push research toward applied outcomes while universities pull toward basic science — creating tension. This paper challenges that narrative directly. Using 631 collaborative PhD projects from the French Cifre programme (ANRT — National Association for Research and Technology), the authors code each project's research orientation (basic, applied, or use-inspired basic research) through manual double-blinded classification.

The headline finding is striking: **61% of industry-sponsored collaborative projects prioritize basic research** — matching academic conventions, not commercial expectations. This holds across scientific disciplines (STEM and social sciences), company sizes (startups, SMEs, large groups), university types, and French regions. Companies new to the Cifre programme show a higher tendency toward use-inspired basic research (Pasteur's Quadrant), suggesting that initial industry engagement gravitates toward frontier exploration rather than immediate commercialization.

The paper distinguishes two UIC models: (1) *knowledge transfer* — transferring already-produced knowledge via patents, licensing, spinoffs; (2) *knowledge creation* — co-creating new scientific knowledge through joint research. The Cifre model belongs firmly to the knowledge creation category. In this context, basic research orientation is not exceptional — it is the norm. The authors conclude that cultural differences between academia and industry do not necessarily impede knowledge creation partnerships, and that industry's preference for basic research reflects long-term innovation strategy.

## Research question

What research orientation — basic, applied, or use-inspired — do university–industry collaborative research projects actually adopt? Does this depend on scientific discipline, company size, or university type?

## Core argument or contribution

1. **61% of industry-sponsored UIC projects prioritize basic research** — contradicting the applied-push assumption.
2. Basic research orientation is robust across disciplines, firm sizes, and university types.
3. Companies new to UIC engage in more use-inspired basic research (Pasteur's Quadrant) as an entry mode.
4. Industry's preference for basic research in UIC reflects deliberate long-term innovation strategy.
5. Knowledge creation UIC (co-research) behaves differently from knowledge transfer UIC — requires separate policy treatment.

## Methodology

- **Sample**: 631 collaborative PhD projects, French Cifre programme (ANRT)
- **Classification**: Manual double-blinded coding of project research orientation (basic / applied / use-inspired)
- **Approach**: Descriptive analysis with frequency comparisons across firm/discipline/region categories
- **Contextual variables**: Scientific discipline, company size, university type, firm experience with programme, financial amount, time allocation

## Datasets/materials used

- French Cifre programme administrative data (ANRT): 631 projects; project descriptions, firm characteristics, financial data, time allocation

## Key findings

1. **61% of projects prioritize basic research** in line with academic conventions.
2. Basic research preference is independent of scientific discipline, company size, and university type.
3. New-to-programme companies tend toward use-inspired basic research (Pasteur's Quadrant).
4. Cultural differences between academia and industry do not inherently block knowledge creation collaboration.
5. Industry partners willingly adopt academic research norms in collaborative PhD settings.

## Limitations

- French Cifre programme only — collaborative PhD format; generalizability to other UIC forms uncertain
- Descriptive design; no causal identification of what drives orientation choices
- Manual coding (inter-rater reliability addressed through double-blinding)
- No outcome data — research orientation measured, not innovation output

## Implications for the project domain

- **Bundesinnovationshaushalt TRL analysis**: even industry-sponsored UIC (61% basic) operates at TRL 1–3 in practice. Förderkatalog programs co-financing collaborative research may generate more basic-stage output than their official TRL labels suggest.
- **Germany's Fraunhofer/collaborative research model**: the Cifre parallel (DFG collaborative research centers with industrial partners, Fraunhofer projects) may also tilt more basic than their applied-research mandate implies — a testable hypothesis.
- **Policy design**: steering UIC toward "practical applications" may be counterproductive — it suppresses the basic research orientation that makes UIC valuable for long-run AC building. The problem is not excess basic research but inadequate knowledge translation mechanisms downstream.
- **Counter-narrative**: Germany's diagnosis that public R&D is too basic and not commercialized quickly enough may be misdiagnosed. If even industry-sponsored projects are basic, the bottleneck is knowledge transfer capacity, not research orientation.

## Detailed summary

This OECD country review assesses Germany's science, technology, and innovation system at a moment when two transition pressures had become urgent: digital transformation and the green transition. The foreword and overall assessment frame the COVID-19 pandemic and Russia's war against Ukraine as shocks that exposed weaknesses in Germany's economic model, including energy dependence, fossil-fuel reliance, delayed digitalisation, and vulnerable supply chains. The report does not argue that Germany lacks innovation capacity. Its starting point is the opposite: Germany has one of the world's strongest STI systems, a deep manufacturing base, major public research institutions, high R&D capacity, and established policy instruments. The central concern is whether this successful system is agile enough for technological and geopolitical change that may undermine the sources of its past success.

The review argues that German innovation policy must shift from a model oriented mainly toward incremental industrial strength and established sectoral capabilities toward a more experimental, risk-tolerant, cross-sectoral, and mission-capable policy approach. Digital technologies threaten to change where value is created in sectors such as automotive, machinery, chemicals, and pharmaceuticals. In vehicles, for example, the report highlights that value may increasingly move toward software, data, services, and digital components rather than only mechanical engineering. The green transition similarly requires industrial transformation, new infrastructure, new demand-side policy instruments, and faster diffusion of sustainable technologies.

The report's recommendations are organized around both system fundamentals and future-oriented transition capacity. It calls for a shared long-term vision for "Germany 2030 and 2050"; a public-private laboratory for innovation-policy experimentation; broader use of agile policy tools for SMEs; better data infrastructure and industrial data access; stronger cross-disciplinary and cross-sectoral knowledge transfer; financial markets that can scale breakthrough innovations; public procurement as a driver of innovation; broader civil-society involvement in transition-oriented STI policy; digitalized and strategically used quality infrastructure; and a stronger German role in shaping EU and global innovation policy.

For the LLM wiki, this source is best treated as a high-level OECD diagnostic and policy-design reference. It is not a causal evaluation of a specific German funding programme and it does not directly classify Foerderkatalog projects by technology readiness. Its value is in showing which system-level bottlenecks OECD regarded as binding for Germany: slow firm-level and public-sector digitalisation, underused industrial data, insufficient risk capital for scaling, constrained knowledge transfer from research to industry, administrative barriers for SMEs and start-ups, limited policy experimentation, and the need to use procurement, standards, and quality infrastructure more strategically.

## Research question

How should Germany adapt its innovation policy system so that a historically strong STI and manufacturing base can remain competitive, resilient, and socially legitimate through the digital and green transitions?

## Core argument or contribution

The review's core contribution is a system-level diagnosis of German innovation policy. Germany's innovation system is strong but too dependent on structures that delivered past manufacturing success. Future competitiveness requires policy agility, experimentation, risk-tolerant finance, data infrastructure, knowledge transfer, mission-oriented governance, demand-side instruments, and international/EU leadership. The report reframes German innovation-policy performance around transition readiness, not just aggregate R&D inputs or historical export success.

## Methodology

The source is an OECD country innovation-policy review, not an econometric paper. Its evidence base combines:

- a background report prepared by ZEW and Fraunhofer ISI contributors;
- OECD benchmarking and indicator analysis on R&D, patents, publications, investment, trade, regional and sectoral structure, and innovation outputs;
- policy and programme analysis of German STI strategies and instruments;
- two rounds of fact-finding interviews in April/May 2021 and November 2021 with more than 100 stakeholders;
- discussion and peer review in the OECD Working Party on Innovation and Technology Policy;
- feedback from German ministries, OECD directorates, international experts, and stakeholders.

## Datasets/materials used

The review draws on OECD indicators, German and international innovation statistics, patent/publication/trade indicators, policy documents, programme descriptions, stakeholder interviews, and the commissioned background report. It discusses German policy instruments such as ZIM, SPRIND, tax incentives, knowledge-transfer programmes, public procurement, quality infrastructure, and regulatory experimentation, but it does not provide a project-level dataset for this vault to treat as a canonical empirical dataset.

## Key findings

- Germany has an advanced STI system and a powerful manufacturing base, but the COVID-19 pandemic and the Ukraine war exposed vulnerabilities in energy supply, fossil-fuel dependence, supply chains, digitalisation, and resilience.
- Digital and green transitions change the capabilities Germany needs. Mechanical-engineering and incremental manufacturing strengths remain important, but future value creation increasingly depends on data, software, advanced ICT, AI, microelectronics, new business models, sustainability technologies, and fast diffusion.
- Germany lags in important aspects of digitalisation, including broadband and digital infrastructure, public-sector digitalisation, firm-level adoption of frontier digital tools, and the use of industrial data for innovation.
- The report sees a need for more agile and experimental STI policy. It recommends a public-private laboratory for policy experimentation and broader use of agile tools such as regulatory sandboxes, administrative simplification, and digital government-to-business services.
- SME and Mittelstand innovation participation is central. The review highlights administrative barriers, digital absorption capacity, skills, access to knowledge-transfer channels, and the need for policy instruments that help SMEs contribute to digital and green transitions.
- Knowledge transfer needs to become more cross-disciplinary and cross-sectoral. The review emphasizes university-industry engagement, spin-off support, entrepreneurship training, Fraunhofer and public-research linkages, and better incentives for transfer.
- Financing conditions are a bottleneck for breakthrough and scale-up innovation. The review argues that Germany needs more institutional investment, higher risk tolerance, and fewer regulatory barriers in start-up and growth finance.
- Public procurement, pre-commercial procurement, standards, norms, and quality infrastructure should be used more strategically to create markets for future innovations and shape international technological rules.
- STI governance should be more inclusive and more directional. The report recommends a shared "Germany 2030 and 2050" vision, broader civil-society engagement, and a stronger German role in EU and global innovation-policy governance.

## Limitations

- This is a broad policy review rather than a causal evaluation; it should not be cited for treatment effects of particular funding instruments.
- Many recommendations are system-level and require implementation design before they can be translated into budget lines, programme eligibility rules, or portfolio classifications.
- The evidence base reflects the policy context and data available around 2021-2022. Later changes in German innovation policy, energy policy, digital policy, or EU state-aid rules require separate verification.
- The review does not directly measure technology readiness levels, Foerderkatalog topic quality, or the project-level additionality of German grants.
- Because the report synthesizes many indicators and stakeholder views, exact quantitative claims should be checked in the source chapter before citation.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt/TRL project, the OECD review is a guardrail against interpreting public R&D funding purely as input volume. A retrieval workflow should pair project-level funding records with concepts such as SME participation, data access, knowledge transfer, procurement, risk finance, and mission-oriented governance. The report implies that an A-tier LLM wiki should capture whether funding portfolios support transition capabilities and market creation, not only whether they fund research activity.

For LLM-assisted classification, the review suggests useful coding dimensions: SME/start-up orientation, digital and green transition relevance, data-infrastructure relevance, knowledge-transfer mechanism, procurement or demand-side component, risk-finance or scale-up component, quality-infrastructure/standards relevance, and governance/experimentation relevance. Those are project interpretations derived from the review's recommendations; they should be validated against the concrete project corpus before being used as labels.

## Relevance to radical vs. incremental innovation and middle-tech trap (added 2026-05-11)

The TRL research briefing (`explorations/trl_research_briefing.md`, Section 2.2) uses OECD (2022) as empirical support for two specific claims about Germany's structural innovation bias:

- **Germany ranks high on incremental innovation outputs** (productivity, export quality) but **relatively low on disruptive and radical innovation metrics**
- **The Mittelstand model is identified as both a competitive advantage and a structural barrier to radical innovation**: Mittelstand firms optimize existing product lines; the academic research ecosystem is insufficiently connected to commercialization pathways that would generate radical innovation

These two OECD findings constitute the system-level backdrop for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt TRL hypothesis. If the Förderprojekte portfolio is concentrated at High TRL (near-commercial, incremental improvement) rather than Low TRL (pre-competitive, radical potential), this is consistent with the structural pattern the OECD review diagnoses at the system level.

The briefing pairs OECD (2022) with KfW (2024) — [[20_summaries/kfw2024-sme-innovation.md]] — for the incremental bias claim: OECD provides the system-level structural diagnosis; KfW provides the firm-level statistics (82% of SMEs innovate without own R&D; only ~7% generate new-to-market products).

## Detailed summary

*Boulevard of Broken Dreams* is Josh Lerner's comprehensive critique of government attempts to catalyze venture capital and entrepreneurship. The book's theme: "if at first you don't succeed, try, try again" — characterizing governments' persistent pursuit of public venture capital despite repeated failure.

**History of failure:** Opening chapters survey failed government VC initiatives worldwide. Canonical failure modes: faddism (chasing technology trends), regulatory capture by incumbent interests, cronyism in recipient selection, bailouts of failing companies, excessive scale, moral hazard, and politically-motivated investment misallocation. Lerner likens public venture capital to a "massive casino" (p. 5) where reckless political decisions far outnumber smart market-driven ones.

**The few successes:** Silicon Valley, Tel Aviv (Yozma), Singapore, Dubai. The US success stories evolved from Department of Defense and CIA funding — which operated with different accountability constraints than civilian democratic programs.

**Prescriptive advice:** Government should (a) set the right "climate" — tax incentives, rule of law, IP protection — then (b) act like a private VC firm when making direct investments: let the market drive decisions, be flexible, think small, plan an exit strategy, maintain commercial discipline.

**Democratic tension:** Transparency requirements conflict with the secrecy and discretion effective public VC needs. Lerner's success cases (Singapore, DoD/CIA) involve authoritarian or military-industrial contexts. This creates an uncomfortable implicit recommendation that democratic programs are structurally disadvantaged.

**Structure:** History of failure → prescriptive advice → return to failure theme (including a chapter on sovereign wealth fund problems). A reviewer notes this structure is confusing; the prescriptive section would read better between the two historical sections.

## Research question

Why have public programs to boost entrepreneurship and venture capital so consistently failed, and what governance principles distinguish the few successes from the many failures?

## Core argument or contribution

Government involvement in venture capital is inevitable. The question is not whether to engage but how: emulate private VC governance, keep programs small, maintain commercial discipline, set the macro "climate" rather than replacing markets.

## Key findings

1. **Canonical failure modes:** faddism, regulatory capture, cronyism, bailouts, excessive scale, moral hazard, political misallocation.
2. **Few successes with special features:** Silicon Valley (DoD/DARPA lineage), Tel Aviv (Yozma's sunset/privatization design), Singapore (authoritarian governance), Dubai (sovereign wealth).
3. **Prescriptive principles:** emulate private VC; market-driven selection; flexibility; exit planning; small scale.
4. **Democratic paradox:** transparency requirements undermine the secrecy and discretion effective public VC needs; authoritarian and military contexts consistently outperform democratic ones.

## Implications for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project

1. **Governance preconditions:** Lerner's framework implies the question for German public R&D is not only how much but how it is governed — commercial discipline, exit mechanisms, and insulation from political misallocation are the critical variables alongside TRL distribution.
2. **Incumbent capture as failure mode:** Lerner's "cronyism" and "regulatory capture" match the German pattern (large, established firms receiving disproportionate grants). The Förderkatalog analysis can test whether Germany has fallen into Lerner's failure taxonomy.

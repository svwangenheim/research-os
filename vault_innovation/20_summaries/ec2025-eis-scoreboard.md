## Detailed summary

The European Innovation Scoreboard 2025 is the European Commission's reference report for benchmarking innovation performance. It tracks innovation performance across the EU, its Member States, neighbouring countries, and global competitors using a composite indicator framework. The report is designed for evidence-based policymaking: it identifies relative strengths and weaknesses, tracks changes over time, and helps policymakers interpret national performance in relation to structural context.

The 2025 report marks 25 years of the EIS. It uses a revised framework relative to the 2021-2024 editions. Five indicators were updated to reflect new data and EU research and innovation policy priorities, including two digitalisation indicators and indicators related to external high-tech dependence, production-based CO2 productivity, and labour productivity. Performance scores are indexed relative to EU performance in 2018 and 2025, which allows tracking of progress but also requires care when comparing across framework revisions.

The report finds that EU innovation performance has improved by 12.6 percentage points since 2018. However, performance declined marginally by 0.4 percentage points from 2024 to 2025, continuing a period of relative stability. Every EU Member State improved relative to 2018, but the scale of improvement differs substantially across countries. The report also finds a moderate reduction in disparities across EU Member States since 2018, while divergence persists or grows within some performance groups.

The 2025 country groups are Innovation Leaders, Strong Innovators, Moderate Innovators, and Emerging Innovators. Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands, and Finland are Innovation Leaders. Ireland, Belgium, Luxembourg, Austria, Germany, France, and Estonia are Strong Innovators. Germany therefore remains above the EU average but is not in the Innovation Leader group.

The report also introduces and emphasizes industrial resilience. It links innovation measurement to competitiveness, sustainability, economic security, supply-chain dependencies, high-tech imports, and deep-tech capacity. This makes the 2025 EIS particularly relevant for projects concerned with strategic technologies and EU resilience, but it remains a benchmarking instrument rather than a direct evaluation of specific funding programs.

For the wiki, EIS 2025 should be used as a country and EU innovation-performance benchmarking source. It should not be used as evidence that a specific policy caused a performance change, and it does not provide project-level TRL or Foerderkatalog classifications.

## Research question

How do EU Member States, neighbouring countries, and global competitors compare in innovation performance, and how has performance changed over time across the EIS indicator framework?

## Core argument or contribution

The report contributes a standardized and audited composite-indicator framework for comparing innovation systems. Its main value is not a causal claim but a structured benchmark of relative innovation strengths, weaknesses, and trends.

## Methodology

- Composite innovation scoreboard using 32 indicators in the 2025 framework.
- Performance indexed relative to EU reference years.
- Country grouping into Innovation Leaders, Strong Innovators, Moderate Innovators, and Emerging Innovators.
- Indicator dimensions include framework conditions, investments, innovation activities, impacts, and resilience-related measures.
- Data are drawn from multiple official and expert sources and audited with support from the European Commission's Competence Centre on Composite Indicators and Scoreboards at the Joint Research Centre.
- Contextual indicators are used to help interpret structural differences between countries.

## Key findings

- EU innovation performance increased by 12.6 percentage points from 2018 to 2025.
- EU innovation performance declined marginally by 0.4 percentage points from 2024 to 2025.
- All EU Member States improved relative to 2018, but improvement sizes vary widely.
- Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands, and Finland are Innovation Leaders.
- Ireland, Belgium, Luxembourg, Austria, Germany, France, and Estonia are Strong Innovators.
- Germany is above the EU average but below the Innovation Leader group.
- Croatia moved from Emerging Innovator to Moderate Innovator; Cyprus and Hungary dropped performance groups.
- The report finds slight convergence across EU Member States overall but continued divergence within some groups.
- The 2025 framework adds more attention to digitalisation, high-tech dependency, CO2 productivity, labour productivity, and resilience.

## Limitations

- The EIS is a composite benchmarking framework, not a causal evaluation.
- Indicator selection, normalization, weighting, missing-data treatment, and framework revisions affect rankings and trend interpretation.
- Country-level scores can hide sectoral, regional, firm-size, and technology-specific differences.
- The report does not evaluate the impact of individual funding programs.
- It does not classify projects by TRL, mission orientation, radicalness, or public value.
- Cross-country comparison should be interpreted together with structural indicators and national context.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt/TRL project, EIS 2025 is useful as an external benchmark for Germany's innovation-system position. It helps situate Germany as a Strong Innovator rather than an Innovation Leader and supports retrieval for questions about international comparison, innovation indicators, resilience, high-tech dependency, and digitalisation. It does not answer whether German federal project funding is allocated to low or high TRL, nor whether specific programs cause innovation outcomes.

## Detailed summary

Stehnken et al. produce the most recent comprehensive evaluation of ZIM, Germany's flagship SME innovation program. The evaluation covers the ZIM-3 period (January 2020 to April 2024) under the 2020 Foerderrichtlinie and was commissioned by BMWK. It examines who ZIM reaches, whether it produces additional R&D activity and innovation outcomes, and how the current cohort compares to earlier periods.

The report combines quantitative analysis of ZIM administrative data (29,299 applications between 2018 and July 2023), a large-scale online survey of funded and non-funded applicants, and qualitative interviews. Econometric additionality analysis uses a difference-in-differences design comparing funded and rejected applicants, drawing on Mannheimer Innovationspanel firm data.

**Recipient structure and repeat-recipient dynamics.** The evaluation documents that 44.8% of ZIM-3 R&D recipients had no prior ZIM funding since 2010 — they are "Foerderneulinge." The remaining 55.2% had prior ZIM experience: 16.8% had continuous participation across ZIM pre-2018 and ZIM-2; 22.9% had prior ZIM experience before 2018 but not under ZIM-2 (lapsed participants); and 11.1% had funding only under ZIM-2. Together, 67.7% of ZIM-3 recipients either had no prior ZIM experience since 2010 or had not been funded for more than a decade. This indicates the program does renew its target group over time, but a core group of firms with continuous ZIM experience persists.

Foerderneulinge differ structurally from experienced ZIM recipients: they are more often service-sector firms (44% vs. 28% of experienced recipients), more often micro-enterprises below 20 employees (30% vs. 16%), more often young firms under 10 years old (33% vs. 16%), and more often located in western German states (Bavaria, Hesse, northern Germany).

The approval rate fell from 68.5% under ZIM-2 to 60.3% under ZIM-3.

**Additionality.** The counterfactual survey finds that 65% of funded projects would not have been conducted without ZIM support, and another 28% would have been conducted in reduced form. For rejected applicants, 70% did not implement the rejected project at all; only 3% proceeded unchanged without ZIM funding. These additionality rates are consistent with the Struss et al. (2016) findings for earlier ZIM cohorts.

Input additionality is confirmed econometrically: funded firms show significantly higher R&D expenditure and R&D employment than matched non-funded comparators. Feasibility study recipients report on average EUR 30k additional R&D expenditure and 0.3 additional R&D full-time equivalents, with a leverage ratio of approximately 0.9.

**Output additionality.** 79% of projects at an advanced stage achieved their technical objectives fully or largely. A substantial share of projects led to market-ready results. Employment effects average 1.2 new jobs per project and 5.3 jobs secured per project. Revenue growth of funded firms over the reference period was +24.6%.

**Cooperation effects.** The share of cooperation projects among ZIM-funded R&D activities rose from 73% (2012) to 84% in recent cohorts, reflecting program incentives for firm-university and firm-firm collaboration.

**Limitations identified in the evaluation.** The evaluation explicitly identifies that ZIM primarily deepens R&D activity among already R&D-active SMEs. FuE entry — the induction of firms with no prior R&D activity — is observed in only a small share of recipients. The typical recipient profile (founded ~1993, ~52 employees, ~EUR 8-9M revenue) is a well-established SME, not a young entrant.

**Policy recommendations from the evaluation.** ZIM should be positioned more clearly as an entry instrument for firms without R&D experience, using feasibility studies and lower-threshold formats. Technology-openness should be preserved. The evaluation recommends improving accessibility for Kleinstunternehmen and start-ups, including more flexible credit-worthiness assessment and de-minimis formats for feasibility studies. First-time applicants face a learning-curve hurdle that experienced ZIM firms do not face; the evaluation recommends reducing administrative burden for first-time applicants.

**Note on the ZIM 2025 Richtlinie reform.** A 3-year exclusion period introduced in the ZIM 2025 Richtlinie reform is a policy response to the concern about repeat-recipient concentration identified in this and earlier evaluations. This reform is not documented in the Stehnken et al. (2024) evaluation itself — it is a subsequent policy decision.

**TRL gap.** The evaluation does not classify funded projects by TRL. This is the critical gap for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt TRL project: ZIM additionality is documented, but the stage, type, and novelty of the innovation being funded cannot be inferred from this evaluation.

## Research question

Does Germany's flagship SME innovation program ZIM produce measurable additionality in R&D inputs, innovation outputs, and employment, and what are the structural characteristics and repeat-recipient dynamics of the firms it reaches? The evaluation covers January 2020 to April 2024 (ZIM-3 Richtlinie), commissioned by BMWK.

## Core argument or contribution

ZIM produces strong project-level additionality and works more in depth than in breadth: it substantially increases R&D intensity and output among already R&D-active SMEs, but induces FuE entry in only a small share of recipients. Recipient dynamics show that 55.2% of ZIM-3 firms had prior ZIM experience (55.2%), but 44.8% were genuinely new to ZIM since 2010 and are structurally younger and smaller. The evaluation recommends clearer ZIM positioning as an entry instrument and preserving technology-openness while improving accessibility for Kleinstunternehmen and start-ups.

## Methodology

- Administrative data analysis of 29,299 ZIM applications (January 2018 to July 2023), covering project form, firm category, approval rates, funding amounts, project duration, and prior ZIM participation history.
- Online survey of funded firms and rejected applicants for counterfactual additionality assessment.
- Qualitative interviews with funded firms, research partners, and program administrators.
- Difference-in-differences econometric model for input and output additionality, using Mannheimer Innovationspanel (MIP) firm data matched to ZIM administrative records.
- Controls: pre-application R&D activity, firm size, sector, region, subsidy supply by industry and year.
- Evaluation period: January 2020 to April 2024 (ZIM-3); comparative reference to ZIM-2 (2018-2020) cohorts.

## Datasets/materials used

- ZIM administrative project database (Foerderantragsdaten), application period 2018-2023
- Online survey of funded and non-funded applicants (Prognos/ZEW, 2023)
- Mannheimer Innovationspanel (MIP) / German Community Innovation Survey for firm-level controls

## Key findings

- 44.8% of ZIM-3 recipients are Foerderneulinge (no prior ZIM funding since 2010); 55.2% had prior ZIM experience.
- Among ZIM-3 recipients with prior experience: 16.8% continuous (ZIM pre-2018 and ZIM-2); 22.9% lapsed (pre-2018 only); 11.1% ZIM-2 only.
- Foerderneulinge are structurally different: more service-oriented (44% vs. 28%), smaller under 20 employees (30% vs. 16%), younger under 10 years (33% vs. 16%).
- Approval rate fell from 68.5% (ZIM-2) to 60.3% (ZIM-3).
- Strong additionality: 65% of projects would not have been conducted without ZIM; 70% of rejected projects were not implemented.
- High technical success: 79% of advanced projects achieved technical objectives fully or largely.
- Employment effects: average 1.2 new jobs per project; 5.3 jobs secured.
- Revenue growth of funded firms: +24.6% over reference period.
- Input additionality confirmed econometrically: higher R&D expenditure and employment than matched comparators.
- Cooperation share rose from 73% (2012) to 84% in recent cohorts.
- ZIM works more in depth than in breadth: FuE entry (induction of non-R&D firms) is rare.
- Typical recipient profile: founded ~1993, ~52 employees, ~EUR 8-9M revenue.
- No TRL classification of funded projects is available from this evaluation.
- Evaluation recommends ZIM position as entry instrument; reduce administrative burden for first-time applicants; preserve technology-openness; improve start-up accessibility.

## Limitations

- No TRL classification of funded projects; additionality is documented but not the stage or novelty of innovation.
- Covers only ZIM-3 (2020-2024); the ZIM 2025 Richtlinie reform (including 3-year exclusion period) is not assessed.
- Additionality design is not a sharp regression discontinuity; uses matched survey respondents rather than a fully randomized design.
- Typical-recipient profile is an average concealing within-cohort heterogeneity.
- No comparison of funded project quality vs. rejected project quality at application stage.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

Primary evaluation source for ZIM as a component of the broader federal R&D funding landscape. Key limitation: no TRL classification — this is exactly the gap the Foerderkatalog TRL classification fills. The 55.2% prior-recipient share, combined with the structural differences of Foerderneulinge (younger, smaller, more service-oriented), provides evidence that ZIM's core is established SMEs, while new entrants are a meaningful but structurally different minority.

Precision note for the policy paper: the "60% Wiederholungsempfaenger" figure used in the outline and international-benchmarks synthesis is a rounded approximation. The exact source figure is 55.2% with any prior ZIM experience since 2010, or 16.8% with continuous recent participation across ZIM-2 and ZIM-3. The 3-year exclusion period is a ZIM 2025 Richtlinie reform, not a finding of this evaluation.

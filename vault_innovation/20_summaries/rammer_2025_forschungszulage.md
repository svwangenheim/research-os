## Detailed summary

Rammer provides a current analysis of the Forschungszulage (German R&D tax credit) five years after its January 2020 introduction. The policy brief documents the instrument's rapid evolution through three rounds of scope expansion (€2M → €4M → €10M → €12M caps; rate increases from 25% to 35% for SMEs), current adoption scale, and international positioning.

**Key Strengths for SME Support:**

1. **Designed SME Bias:** The €12M annual cap ensures SMEs can deduct most eligible R&D while large firms with annual R&D of hundreds of millions hit the cap immediately. This creates favorable cost-reduction incentives for smaller firms.

2. **Generous SME Rate:** 35% tax credit for SMEs (vs. 25% for large firms) at the high end of international practice. SMEs can reliably refinance 35% of eligible R&D expenditure.

3. **Largest Program by Participation:** ~19,000 applications through June 2025; ~15,000 with approved projects (2020–2022). Exceeds ZIM (5,000/year) and federal R&D programs (10,000/year). More participants than Horizon Europe (<2,000 German firms/year).

4. **Observed Increase in Research-Active SMEs:** Continuous R&D engagement among SMEs rose from 9.9% (2019) to 12.6% (2023). Timing aligns with program; Rammer notes this is "auffallend" (striking) though causality remains unconfirmed pending ongoing BMF evaluation.

5. **SME-Focused Distribution (with Erosion):** Initially 61% of potential support to SMEs; now 48% after cap increases. The erosion reflects policy evolution to stimulate large-firm R&D, not changes to SME treatment itself.

**Fundamental Limitations for Addressing Innovation Paradox and Middle-Technology Trap:**

1. **Blunt Instrument Cannot Distinguish Innovation Type**
   - Forschungszulage does NOT differentiate between incremental and disruptive innovation
   - Equally subsidizes incumbent product improvements and startup radical innovations
   - Cannot redirect dominant firms' R&D toward breakthrough vs. defensive ends
   - **Direct contradiction with Akcigit mechanism:** Tax credit reduces all R&D costs equally; cannot prevent incumbent capture for innovation-stifling defensive purposes

2. **SME Share Declining with Policy Expansion**
   - SME share fell from 61% → 48% as caps increased
   - Increasing caps to stimulate large-firm R&D necessarily dilutes SME's relative allocation
   - Mirrors the incumbent-capture problem: universal cost-reduction subsidies tend to favor dominant firms absolutely

3. **Modest International Scope**
   - €4 billion annually ≈ 4.7% of German corporate R&D (as estimated under 2026 rules)
   - Comparison: France 19.6%, Ireland 18.5%, Spain 17.2%, UK 15.4% (all >15%)
   - Germany equivalent to US 5.0%, Japan 4.8%, Korea 4.1%
   - Conclusion: Even at full maturity, Forschungszulage is a modest lever relative to total corporate R&D

4. **High Administrative Burden and Selection Bias**
   - Application process "aufwändig" (demanding); firms describe FuE activities for BSFZ certification
   - ~25% of claimed eligible R&D rejected for not meeting strict Frascati criteria
   - Small firms with many small R&D projects avoid application due to effort cost
   - Dual-choice constraint: firms cannot combine tax credit with direct subsidies (ZIM, federal programs)
   - **Consequence:** Deadweight loss among potential users; subsidy underutilized

5. **Long Lag to Financial Impact**
   - Up to 4 years for firm claim; additional years for tax authority processing
   - Actual fiscal costs lag potential: €19M (2021), €155M (2022), ~€500M (2023) vs. €4B potential
   - Discourages firms with short time horizons or limited tax-planning sophistication

6. **No Causal Evidence of Additionality (Yet)**
   - Rammer: "Zur Wirkung der Forschungszulage liegen bislang noch keine Befunde vor"
   - BMF evaluation ongoing; no impact results published
   - Observed increase in research-active SMEs (9.9% → 12.6%) is temporally coincident but causally unconfirmed
   - Risk: Subsidy may simply refinance existing R&D rather than increase total spending

7. **Structural Mismatch with Innovation-Paradox Mechanism**
   - **Akcigit (2024) identifies:** Innovation resources flowing to large incumbents; incentives for defensive R&D (wage premia, political hiring, market defense)
   - **Forschungszulage approach:** Blanket cost reduction (all R&D gets subsidy); no:
     - Preferential support for entrants or young firms
     - Incentive-alignment toward disruptive vs. incremental ends
     - Countering of incumbent market power or network effects
     - Distinction by firm age, growth trajectory, or innovation radicality

## Research question

How effective is the German R&D tax credit for promoting SME research engagement, and what are its limitations for addressing structural innovation-allocation problems (incumbent dominance, R&D-type misallocation)?

## Core argument or contribution

Rammer argues Forschungszulage has successfully become Germany's largest R&D support program by firm participation (19,000 applicants) with design elements favorable to SMEs (35% rate, capped eligible R&D, observed increase in research-active SMEs from 9.9% to 12.6%). However, he acknowledges three structural challenges: (1) SME share eroding as caps increase, (2) modest scope (4.7% of corporate R&D vs. >15% in France/Ireland/Spain), and (3) **unproven additionality.** The brief does not address whether the blunt-instrument tax-credit approach can combat Akcigit-type incumbent dominance or the innovation paradox's allocation problem.

## Methodology

- **Data sources:** BSFZ application/certification statistics (through June 2025); ZEW Mannheimer Innovationspanel; OECD R&D Tax Incentives Database 2025; German federal budget (BMFTR)
- **Design:** Descriptive policy analysis + international benchmarking + longitudinal SME participation trend (2008–2023)
- **Metrics:** SME share of support, application/approval rates, program participation vs. alternatives (ZIM, federal R&D, Horizon), research-active SME incidence
- **Evaluation status:** Formal impact evaluation (additionality, crowding-out) ongoing at BMF; Rammer reports zero causal findings to date

- **OECD R&D Tax Incentives Database (2025):** International tax-credit comparison
- **German federal budget (BMFTR):** Official R&D spending classifications

## Key findings

1. **Scale and Adoption**
   - ~19,000 companies applied; ~15,000 with approved projects (2020–2022 vintage)
   - Estimated annual support at maturity: €4 billion (124% expansion from 2020–2026)
   - **Largest R&D support program in Germany by firm count**
   - Exceeds ZIM (5,000), federal R&D programs (10,000), Horizon Europe (<2,000)

2. **SME Focus with Erosion**
   - Initial (2020): 61% of potential support to SMEs; now (2026): 48%
   - Decline reflects cap increases (€2M → €12M) that allow larger firms to claim more
   - SMEs can claim ~35% of eligible R&D; large firms effectively much lower rates due to cap

3. **International Position**
   - Germany: 4.7% of corporate R&D (estimated, 2026 rules)
   - High-spending countries: France 19.6%, Ireland 18.5%, Spain 17.2%, UK 15.4%
   - Peer group: US 5.0%, Japan 4.8%, Korea 4.1%
   - **Interpretation:** Germany's tax-credit approach is broad but modest in fiscal scope

4. **Observed but Unproven Effect on SME Research Participation**
   - Continuous R&D engagement among SMEs: 9.9% (2019) → 12.6% (2023), +2.7pp
   - Temporal alignment with program introduction and expansion is "auffallend" (striking)
   - Causality unconfirmed; BMF evaluation still underway

5. **Rejection and Implementation Friction**
   - ~25% of claimed eligible R&D rejected by BSFZ for Frascati non-compliance
   - High application burden deters small firms with many small R&D projects
   - Dual-choice constraint prevents combining tax credit with direct subsidies (creates inefficiency)

6. **Long Lag and Cost Timing**
   - Claim window: up to 4 years; tax authority processing: years more
   - Actual costs lag potential: €500M (2023) vs. €4B potential estimate
   - Discourages time-constrained or unsophisticated users

## Limitations

- **No causal evidence on additionality:** Evaluation ongoing; the increase in research-active SMEs cannot yet be attributed to Forschungszulage
- **Cannot distinguish innovation type:** Policy treats incremental and disruptive R&D identically; cannot redirect incumbent resources toward breakthrough innovation
- **Modest international scope:** 4.7% of corporate R&D is lower than France, Ireland, Spain, UK, limiting macro-level impact
- **SME share erosion:** Policy expansion necessarily dilutes relative SME allocation; larger firms benefit proportionally more from cap increases
- **Limited to cost reduction:** Tax credit has no mechanism to address allocation across firm types, innovation strategies, competitive dynamics, or firm age/growth
- **Fungibility risk:** Subsidy can be used equally for defensive (incumbent) and disruptive (entrant) R&D; design cannot prevent capture for defensive purposes

## Methods discussed

- Tax-credit design: reduces user cost of R&D via tax deduction
- Capping mechanism: creates favorable incentive structure for firms below cap (mainly SMEs)
- Frascati Manual-based eligibility: operationalizes "R&D" but broader than basic research, narrower than all innovation

## Implications for the Bundesinnovationshaushalt project

1. **Forschungszulage as Policy Context**
   - €4B annual support is now Germany's single largest R&D policy tool by firm participation
   - Cannot analyze German innovation policy without understanding Forschungszulage's scope and design
   - But tax-credit approach does NOT address innovation-paradox mechanisms: incumbent dominance, allocation distortions, defensive R&D

2. **SME Data Point**
   - The 2.7pp increase in research-active SMEs (9.9% → 12.6%, 2019–2023) is suggestive but unattributed
   - Use the same Mannheimer Innovationspanel to examine: (a) whether SME research-activity increase is concentrated in specific sectors or TRL levels, (b) whether Forschungszulage uptake correlates with TRL choice, (c) whether new research-active SMEs pursue incremental vs. disruptive ends

3. **Policy Design: Blunt vs. Targeted**
   - Forschungszulage = blunt-instrument approach (reduce cost, allow firms to choose)
   - Förderkatalog = targeted approach (select projects by strategic criteria)
   - Akcigit's work suggests **complementary targeted policy** is needed: blunt cost reduction alone may be captured by incumbents for defensive purposes
   - This creates a case for **stronger TRL-level and firm-type screening** in direct subsidy programs

4. **Additionality Warning**
   - Until BMF evaluation publishes results, all claims about Forschungszulage increasing total R&D are preliminary
   - Rammer reports zero impact findings to date; even observed SME-participation increase is temporally coincident, not causal
   - For Förderkatalog analysis: similarly emphasize need for rigorous counterfactual evaluation to measure true program additionality

5. **Innovation-Type Blindness**
   - Forschungszulage's lack of innovation-type distinction is a design feature, not a bug
   - But for addressing the innovation paradox, this is a fundamental limitation
   - **Implication for Förderkatalog:** If portfolio is dominated by incremental/incumbent projects (high TRL, established firms), direct policy can at least try to balance toward disruptive/entrant ends. Forschungszulage cannot.

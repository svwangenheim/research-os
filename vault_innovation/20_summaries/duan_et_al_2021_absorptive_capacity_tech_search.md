# The Moderating Effect of Absorptive Capacity on the Technology Search and Innovation Quality Relationship in High-Tech Manufacturing Firms

## Detailed summary

This paper examines how absorptive capacity (AC) moderates the relationship between technology search strategy and innovation quality in Chinese high-tech manufacturing firms. Technology search is conceptualized along three dimensions: search *breadth* (scope of knowledge sources), search *depth* (reuse depth of existing knowledge), and *balanced search* (equilibrium between breadth and depth).

Using panel data from China's high-tech manufacturing sector (2009–2017), the authors find non-linear (inverted U-shaped) relationships between both search breadth and search depth and innovation quality — there is an optimal search intensity, beyond which quality declines. Balanced search has a consistently positive linear effect.

Absorptive capacity has a more nuanced moderating role than commonly assumed. It does not uniformly amplify search effects: at high search levels (beyond the optimal), higher AC *mitigates* the negative effect of over-search; at low search levels, higher AC *accelerates* the positive relationship. The net moderating effect depends on where a firm sits on its search intensity curve.

The paper speaks to China's "quantity over quality" innovation problem — high patent counts but low commercialization rates — and argues that the solution requires smarter search combined with absorptive capacity investment, not simply more search.

## Research question

Does absorptive capacity moderate the technology search → innovation quality relationship, and how does this moderation vary across search intensity levels?

## Core argument or contribution

1. Search breadth and depth each have inverted U-shaped relationships with innovation quality — optimal search levels exist.
2. Balanced search (breadth-depth equilibrium) positively affects innovation quality.
3. AC is a heterogeneous moderator — it mitigates over-search and accelerates under-search effects, but does not uniformly amplify.

## Methodology

- Panel data: China's high-tech manufacturing industry, 2009–2017
- Dependent variable: Innovation quality (patent quality index; commercialization rate)
- Search breadth/depth/balance constructed from patent and R&D data
- AC proxy: R&D intensity
- Quadratic and interaction terms in fixed-effects panel model

## Datasets/materials used

- China's high-tech manufacturing industry panel data, 2009–2017 (national statistics)
- Patent data for innovation quality measurement

## Key findings

1. Search breadth and depth each have inverted U-shaped relationships with innovation quality.
2. Balanced search has a positive linear relationship with innovation quality.
3. AC has heterogeneous moderating effects: mitigates over-search penalty; accelerates under-search gains.

## Limitations

- China-specific context; generalizability uncertain
- R&D intensity is a noisy AC proxy
- Causality from panel structure is limited
- Does not distinguish basic from applied research

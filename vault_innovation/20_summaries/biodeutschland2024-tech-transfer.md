## Detailed summary

This position paper sets out BIO Deutschland's demands for improving the translation of scientific discoveries into biotech innovation in Germany. It frames biotechnology as highly innovative, entrepreneur-driven, and capital-intensive, and argues that technology-transfer offices at universities and research institutions are indispensable for commercializing research results. The paper addresses three actor groups: government at federal and state level, academia, and industry.

The first demand is cultural. BIO Deutschland argues that universities and research institutions need stronger entrepreneurial and innovation cultures. In natural-science faculties, publication remains the dominant career metric, while patenting, commercialization, and entrepreneurial pathways receive too little attention. The paper calls for entrepreneurial education in university curricula, more risk-taking among academic researchers, dual career paths between academia and industry, simplified secondary-employment permissions, and easier leaves of absence for commercial activity.

The second demand is institutional and financial: technology transfer should be established and strengthened as a "third mission" alongside research and teaching. The paper argues that many German universities and non-university research institutions treat commercial exploitation as secondary and that technology-transfer offices are often underfunded and understaffed. BIO Deutschland recommends earmarking an additional 5 percent of basic/project funding for technology-transfer infrastructure and another 5 percent for validation studies. It also recommends exceptions to the Besserstellungsverbot so technology-transfer offices can hire and retain experienced industry professionals.

The paper further calls for technology-transfer indicators as quality measures for universities and research institutions. Suggested indicators include industry partnerships, patents granted and pending, licenses, spin-outs, and incubators. The aim is to make translation visible in institutional evaluation, rather than relying almost entirely on publication metrics.

The third demand concerns financing and growth conditions for spin-outs. BIO Deutschland argues that German biotech start-ups need stable support schemes and follow-on finance because biotechnology R&D is costly, risky, and long-horizon, making debt financing unsuitable for many start-ups. The paper calls for maintaining and expanding EXIST, GO-Bio, KMU-innovativ bioeconomy/biomedicine lines, SPRIND, and other support structures where they work, while being cautious about creating new agencies or structures. It also stresses venture-capital and growth-finance reforms, including tax and legal conditions for venture funds and private investors, enabling pension funds and health insurance companies to invest more in biotech, and continuing the WIN initiative.

## Research question

What reforms does BIO Deutschland argue are needed to improve the translation of scientific findings into biotech commercialization and spin-out growth in Germany?

## Core argument or contribution

The position paper's contribution is a policy-demand package for Germany's biotech transfer and translation system. It argues that better translation requires simultaneous changes in university culture, professional transfer infrastructure, validation funding, institutional metrics, start-up support, and growth-finance conditions.

## Key findings

- BIO Deutschland sees weak entrepreneurial culture at universities and research institutions as a barrier to biotech commercialization.
- Technology transfer should be strengthened as a funded third mission alongside research and teaching.
- Transfer offices need professional staffing, better budgets, and exceptions from public-sector pay restrictions where necessary.
- Validation studies need earmarked funding because inventions often require proof-of-concept work before licensing or spin-out.
- Universities and research institutions should be assessed partly with transfer indicators such as industry partnerships, patents, licenses, spin-outs, and incubators.
- Start-up schemes such as EXIST, GO-Bio, and KMU-innovativ should be stable and reliable because biotech start-ups depend on continuity in early support.
- Growth finance is a major bottleneck for biotech spin-outs; the paper supports better venture-capital conditions and broader institutional-investor participation.

## Limitations

- This is a position paper by an industry association, not an independent empirical study.
- The document does not quantify causal effects of specific transfer policies.
- Recommendations reflect the perspective and priorities of the German biotechnology industry.
- The paper focuses on biotechnology and translation, so it should not be generalized to all technologies without additional evidence.
- It does not analyze Foerderkatalog project data, TRL distributions, or funding-recipient concentration.

## Detailed summary

This paper provides the most direct empirical test of the mechanism connecting corporate basic research to product innovation output. The authors propose and test a model in which absorptive capacity acts as a *mediator* between basic research and product innovation: basic research doesn't primarily produce innovation directly; it builds the firm's capacity to identify, assimilate, and exploit external knowledge, which in turn generates product innovation.

Using panel data on 8,861 Spanish firms from the PITEC survey, they estimate a structural mediation model with three hypotheses:

- **H1**: Basic research → absorptive capacity (via knowledge stock and knowledge flow accumulation)
- **H2**: Absorptive capacity → product innovation
- **H3**: Appropriability *positively* moderates H1 in the weak-appropriability direction — weaker IP protection *amplifies* the basic research → AC link

Key findings:
1. Basic research in firms increases knowledge stock and flows, improving absorptive capacity. This effect persists for **up to 3 years**.
2. Absorptive capacity translates into better product innovation performance.
3. Weak appropriability regimes positively moderate the basic research → AC link — when firms cannot fully appropriate the returns on their basic research, the external knowledge pool is enriched by spillovers, making AC more valuable and easier to build.

The paper challenges managerial "disdain" for basic research as unprofitable, and broadens the sample beyond knowledge-intensive sectors (pharmacie, biotech) to all industries.

## Research question

Does basic research in firms improve product innovation? Through what mechanism — and does appropriability condition this relationship?

## Core argument or contribution

1. The mechanism is: basic research → absorptive capacity → product innovation (full mediation).
2. The basic research effect on AC persists for up to 3 years.
3. Weak appropriability paradoxically strengthens the basic research → AC link (spillovers enrich the external knowledge pool).
4. The mechanism generalizes across industries (not just high-tech/pharma).

## Methodology

- **Sample**: 8,861 Spanish firms from PITEC (Panel de Innovación Tecnológica), multi-wave panel
- **Model**: Structural mediation model; lagged specifications to test temporal persistence
- **AC measure**: Multi-item index (knowledge stock, knowledge flows, exploitation capacity) — not just R&D intensity
- **Lagged analysis**: Tests whether basic research in year t affects AC in t, t+1, t+2, t+3

## Datasets/materials used

- **PITEC** (Panel de Innovación Tecnológica): Spanish innovation panel; N = 8,861 firms, cross-industry

## Key findings

1. Basic research → absorptive capacity: confirmed; effect lasts up to 3 years.
2. Absorptive capacity → product innovation: confirmed.
3. Weak appropriability positively moderates H1 (basic research → AC).
4. Full mediation: basic research improves product innovation *through* AC, not independently.

## Limitations

- Working paper (not peer-reviewed)
- Spanish firms; PITEC covers mostly mid-to-large firms
- Appropriability measure is rough (industry-level index)
- Causal identification limited despite panel structure
- 3-year persistence may reflect cumulative R&D rather than basic research specifically

## Implications for the project domain

- **Core mechanism for TRL policy logic**: basic research → AC → product innovation is the pathway explaining why funding TRL 1–3 has downstream product effects. This supports the case for early-stage public funding in firms, not just universities.
- **Temporal persistence**: the 3-year lag means that cuts to basic research have delayed costs — firms don't immediately lose innovation capacity.
- **Cross-sector generalizability**: the finding covers all industries (not just pharma/biotech), strengthening the case for basic research support across the Förderkatalog's diverse sectors.
- **Germany relevance**: the appropriability finding is less directly applicable in Germany's strong-IPR environment, but the core H1→H2 mediation chain likely still holds.

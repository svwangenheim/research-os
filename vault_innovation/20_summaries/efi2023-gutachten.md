## Detailed summary

The 2023 EFI report frames German research and innovation policy as needing a "Zeitenwende" of its own. The report starts from the premise that the major transformations facing Germany, including the energy transition, mobility transition, and digitalization of the economy and society, require long-term research and innovation policy rather than short-term crisis management alone. It argues that Germany's policy capacity is constrained by overlapping crises, fiscal pressure, fragmented governance, and long-standing bottlenecks in digital administration, key technologies, skilled labor, transfer, and innovation-friendly regulation. The central governance argument is that Germany cannot manage transformative innovation through siloed ministry programs. EFI recommends a government committee for innovation and transformation anchored in the Federal Chancellery, cross-ministerial mission teams, involvement of state-secretary level actors, mission-oriented roadmaps, and a reform of the project-agency system so funding instruments can be bundled and steered toward results. The report treats the federal "Zukunftsstrategie Forschung und Innovation" as a potential umbrella strategy, but criticizes the strategy for remaining too broad and insufficiently operational unless concrete, measurable missions and implementation structures are added. The report also comments on institutional instruments. It notes that DATI was still missing a viable concept at the time of the report and that SPRIND still needed the operational freedoms required for breakthrough innovation. EFI links these institutional issues to the broader transfer problem: Germany has strong research capacity, but turning knowledge into new firms, applications, and markets requires more agile institutions, better incentives, and less administrative friction. Several policy bottlenecks are emphasized. First, Germany and Europe risk technological lag in key technologies and must build competences, infrastructure, regulatory capacity, and strategic autonomy. EFI specifically connects this to digitalization governance, dependencies on China, and the strict separation between military and civilian research, which the report argues should be reconsidered where useful spillovers and dual-use synergies exist. Second, firms face uncertainty and may reduce longer-term F&I activity during overlapping crises; therefore innovation policy must not only supply money but also improve framework conditions such as data access, procurement, and regulatory experimentation. Third, demographic change and skill shortages threaten the long-run innovation base, requiring better use of existing labor, attraction of foreign skilled workers, and more predictable academic careers. The 2023 report contains several thematic chapters that matter for the vault. On mission orientation, it argues that broad high-level missions must be translated into actionable mission goals, measurable transformation targets, and roadmaps across ministries. On data and digital governance, it stresses that unclear data rules, weak public-sector digitalization, and fragmented governance limit digital innovation. On technology markets, the report discusses how markets for technological knowledge and IP transfer can improve the use of research results, including patent databases, deal databases, and technology platforms. On transfer, it emphasizes that research commercialization depends on institutional incentives and information infrastructures, not just research output. The report also discusses space policy and technological sovereignty, where start-ups, SMEs, research organizations, and data infrastructures are part of national capability formation. For the LLM/wiki project, EFI 2023 is important because it supplies the policy-system layer around the more paper-specific evidence in the vault. Papers on incumbent lock-in, SME innovation, AI adoption, technology transfer, and strategic industrial policy identify mechanisms. EFI 2023 connects those mechanisms to German governance reforms: missions, roadmaps, transfer agencies, digital/data rules, public procurement, dual-use policy, project-agency reform, and evaluation-oriented steering.

## Research question

The report asks how Germany's research and innovation policy should respond to large transformations, geopolitical shocks, digitalization, demographic change, technological dependencies, and persistent innovation bottlenecks. More concretely, it asks what governance structures, institutional reforms, and framework conditions are needed so F&I policy can support transformative change rather than remain fragmented across ministries and instruments.

## Core argument or contribution

The core contribution is a governance diagnosis: Germany needs a mission-oriented F&I-policy shift that combines strategic steering, cross-ministerial coordination, operational roadmaps, agile institutions, and better framework conditions. The report does not treat innovation policy as only funding volume. It argues that Germany must improve how goals are selected, how ministries coordinate, how transfer institutions work, how data and digital infrastructure enable innovation, and how regulatory and procurement systems reduce or increase innovation barriers. For the vault, this strengthens the synthesis that Germany's innovation paradox is partly an institutional and allocation problem. High R&D capacity can still underperform if policy missions are vague, transfer institutions are slow, digital governance is weak, data access is uncertain, and project funding is not linked to measurable transformation outcomes.

## Methodology

This is an expert commission annual report, not a single econometric paper. Its method combines policy review, synthesis of commissioned studies on the German innovation system, descriptive indicators, institutional comparison, and expert judgment. The converted source explicitly references "Studien zum deutschen Innovationssystem" and data tables that document Germany's research and innovation performance. Several chapters draw on administrative, survey, patent, and international comparison data, including data from innovation surveys and patent databases. Because the report is a policy synthesis, its recommendations should be read as expert-commission conclusions rather than causal estimates from one identification design. Where it discusses causal claims or measured effects, downstream use should verify the underlying commissioned study or cited literature.

## Key findings

- Germany needs a long-term F&I-policy "Zeitenwende" because crisis management alone will not solve the energy, mobility, digital, and demographic transformations.
- Siloed ministry structures are a major governance bottleneck; EFI recommends a Chancellery-level innovation and transformation committee, mission teams, mission roadmaps, and project-agency reform.
- The federal Zukunftsstrategie contains elements of mission orientation but needs more concrete governance, measurable missions, and implementation structures.
- DATI and SPRIND are important transfer/breakthrough institutions, but in 2023 EFI saw unresolved design and autonomy problems.
- Germany and Europe risk falling behind in key technologies and should build competences, infrastructure, regulation, and technological sovereignty.
- Digital governance and data-economy rules are central innovation bottlenecks, not side issues.
- Innovation barriers include regulation, long administrative procedures, weak procurement incentives, data-access uncertainty, skill shortages, and fragmented transfer systems.
- Reallabore, data-economy rules, and better public procurement can complement direct funding by improving the conditions under which innovation happens.

## Limitations

The report is broad and strategic. It does not provide a single uniform causal identification strategy for all recommendations. Some claims depend on commissioned studies or secondary evidence that should be checked before citation-level use. The converted Markdown is large and includes front matter, tables, footnotes, glossary material, and references; specific numeric claims should therefore be verified against the relevant chapter or original table. The report's value for the wiki is high as a synthesis and policy-governance source, but it should not replace close reading of specialist empirical papers when the question is about causal effects.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt/TRL project, EFI 2023 implies that project-level classification should not only ask what technology is funded and at what maturity stage. It should also ask whether the portfolio maps onto missions, transformation goals, strategic technologies, transfer bottlenecks, data/digital infrastructure, and commercialization pathways. A TRL distribution can show maturity, but EFI 2023 warns that governance, coordination, data access, and transfer institutions determine whether mature or early-stage projects contribute to transformation. For wiki-pull, the summary should be retrieved for queries about German mission-oriented innovation policy, DATI/SPRIND, project-agency reform, digital/data governance, technology transfer, and why Germany's innovation problem is partly institutional rather than only financial.

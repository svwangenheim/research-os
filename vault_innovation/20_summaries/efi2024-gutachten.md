## Detailed summary

The 2024 EFI report continues the diagnosis that German research and innovation policy must support long-term transformations while resisting the temptation to retreat into short-term crisis management. The report frames the energy transition, mobility transition, digitalization of the economy and society, and sustainable agriculture as transformations that need both technological and social innovation. EFI's opening recommendation is that transformative policy should respect five principles: short-term measures should still serve long-term structural goals; social compensation should be considered from the start; structural change should not be reduced to financial compensation; the search for solutions should leave room for business experimentation and social participation; and human capital must be secured over the long run. The report then comments on the current F&I-policy agenda. It supports mission teams as a suitable format for implementing tasks bundled under the federal missions, but emphasizes that mission teams need state-secretary involvement, strategic accompaniment, and their own budgets. It warns that mission success is unlikely within one legislative period because missions are long-term institutional projects. This extends EFI 2023's argument: mission orientation is not merely strategy language; it requires durable governance structures, budgets, and patience. EFI 2024 also highlights regulatory experimentation. It supports a Reallabore law because experimental spaces can reduce technological and economic uncertainty, test regulatory conditions, involve potential users early, and shorten the path to market. The report argues that experiment clauses should be broad enough to remain usable as technologies evolve. The report reviews several German innovation institutions. It sees the SPRIND Freedom Act as overdue but incomplete because political influence and budget-law constraints remain. It sees DATI as finally taking shape and views the openness of the DATIpilot formats as a positive signal, especially compared with earlier, narrower concepts. For the Forschungszulage, the report notes rising application numbers but low awareness and use among smaller firms. It stresses that existing studies cannot yet answer whether the tax credit has increased R&D spending or activated firms that previously did not conduct R&D, making this an evaluation priority. Transfer is a major theme. EFI 2024 argues that Germany struggles with research-based spin-outs partly because IP transfer from universities and public research organizations is difficult. It discusses IP-Transfer 3.0 and virtual participation models, then recommends shifting incentives in public research institutions away from patent or licensing revenue and toward the sustainable success of supported spin-outs. This links directly to the vault's commercialization-gap and technology-transfer concepts. The report also asks Germany to reconsider strict civil-military separation in R&D. It distinguishes spillovers and dual use, cites examples such as DARPA and Israel's Unit 8200, and argues that military R&D can have positive effects on civilian R&D, productivity, and employment. EFI does not argue for indiscriminate militarization of research; the claim is that Germany should reassess options for useful synergies under changed security conditions. Data is another major theme. EFI states that data are central for the F&I system and that progress in data provision and use is required for digital transformation. It welcomes health-data legislation but says further steps are needed, including agricultural data spaces and a research data law. It also argues that legal fragmentation and uneven interpretation of data rules across federal states limit data access and data use. The most methodologically important chapter for the vault is the 2024 section on causal analysis of policy effects. EFI argues that many federally commissioned evaluations of F&I policy measures cannot establish whether observed developments were caused by the measures under study. It attributes this to evaluations that often fail to meet causal-analysis requirements or lack the necessary data conditions. The report therefore calls for causal analyses to be systematically integrated into F&I policy, for evaluation tenders to require causal analysis where appropriate, for better data access for evaluators, and for all commissioned evaluations to be published. The thematic chapters broaden the report. Sustainable agriculture is treated as a field where digital, smart, and green genetic technologies could raise productivity, sustainability, and resilience, but adoption is limited by cost, infrastructure, interoperability, regulation, acceptance, and information deficits. International mobility in the science and innovation system is treated as a human-capital and knowledge-flow issue. Social innovation is treated as complementary to technological innovation. The AI chapter treats artificial intelligence as a key technology with transformative potential and technological-sovereignty implications. EFI calls for stronger AI basic research, better data and compute infrastructure, support for open-source AI, skills development, and legal clarity for firms using AI.

## Research question

The report asks how Germany should strengthen transformative F&I policy and the innovation system in 2024, with special attention to mission implementation, regulatory experimentation, institutional transfer mechanisms, R&D tax incentives, IP transfer, data access, causal policy evaluation, sustainable agriculture, research mobility, social innovation, and AI.

## Core argument or contribution

The core contribution is that Germany's innovation policy must become more implementation-capable and more evidence-capable. Mission teams need budgets and strategic support. Experimental regulation should reduce uncertainty. SPRIND and DATI need enough openness and autonomy to operate as innovation institutions. Transfer incentives should favor successful spin-outs over institutional licensing revenue. The Forschungszulage needs evaluation of activation and additionality. Data access and public data infrastructures are prerequisites for digital innovation and for evaluating policy effects. AI policy needs research, compute, data, skills, open-source support, and legal clarity. For the wiki, EFI 2024 adds a crucial evaluation layer. It does not merely say "more innovation policy"; it says innovation policy must be designed so its effects can be measured, learned from, and revised.

## Methodology

Like the 2023 report, EFI 2024 is an expert commission annual report that synthesizes policy developments, commissioned studies, indicators, legal/institutional analysis, and thematic evidence. It includes descriptive assessments and recommendations, and it explicitly reviews the methodological quality of F&I-policy evaluations. The causal-evaluation chapter distinguishes observed developments from causally attributable policy effects and emphasizes the requirements for appropriate evaluation design and data access.

## Key findings

- Transformative policy should connect short-term measures to long-term structural goals and include social compensation, experimentation, participation, and human-capital strategy.
- Mission teams are useful but need strategic accompaniment, state-secretary involvement, and their own budgets.
- Reallabore can reduce uncertainty and accelerate commercialization if experiment clauses are broad and future-proof.
- SPRIND's freedom was improved but remains incomplete; policy influence and budget constraints still matter.
- DATI's open pilot formats received strong participation and should remain open in the final concept.
- The Forschungszulage is increasingly used but still not well known among smaller firms; its activation and additionality effects remain evaluation questions.
- IP transfer from research organizations to spin-outs remains difficult; incentives should reward sustainable spin-out success rather than only patent or licensing revenue.
- Germany should reconsider strict civil-military R&D separation where spillovers and dual-use synergies can support innovation.
- Data access and data infrastructure are essential for innovation and evaluation.
- Many commissioned F&I evaluations do not support causal conclusions; in the source's reviewed corpus, only a small subset of the 81 examined evaluation studies supports robust causal statements, so evaluation design, data availability, publication, and policy learning need reform.
- AI is a key technology where Germany/Europe need stronger research, compute, data infrastructure, open-source ecosystems, skills, and legal clarity.

## Limitations

The report is an expert synthesis, not a single empirical paper. Its evaluation chapter critiques evaluation practice but is itself a policy-synthesis chapter; users should inspect the underlying EFI evidence and cited evaluation studies before making narrow empirical claims. The converted Markdown contains tables, references, glossary entries, and line-break artifacts, so numeric claims should be verified in the original PDF. Some thematic sections, such as AI and sustainable agriculture, are broad and should be linked to specialist sources before citation-level use.

## Implications for LLM research or the project domain

For the Bundesinnovationshaushalt/TRL project, EFI 2024 adds two concrete implications. First, the project should distinguish descriptive portfolio mapping from causal evaluation. A TRL classifier can describe what the funding portfolio appears to support, but it cannot by itself estimate the causal effect of a funding instrument. Second, data access and publication rules matter: if administrative data, project descriptions, outcome data, and evaluation studies remain fragmented or unpublished, policy learning will be weak even if classification methods improve. For wiki-pull, this summary should be retrieved for questions about policy evaluation, causal evidence, Forschungszulage, IP transfer, DATI, SPRIND, Reallabore, data access, AI policy, and how to connect project-level funding data to evidence-based innovation policy.

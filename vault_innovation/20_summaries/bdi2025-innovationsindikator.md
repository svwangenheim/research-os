## Detailed summary

The Innovationsindikator 2024 is a benchmarking report on national innovation systems. It compares 35 economies and presents Germany's position across three separate composite perspectives: general innovation capacity, key technologies, and sustainability. The report is not a causal evaluation of a specific funding instrument. Its value for the vault is as a high-level diagnostic of Germany's innovation-system position and policy agenda.

The report's headline is that Germany is stagnating while China remains dynamic. Germany's position in the innovation-capacity ranking has eroded further: the report states that Germany loses two places and now ranks 12th out of 35 economies. Switzerland is the leading country in the 2024 innovation-capacity ranking, with Singapore and Denmark also at the top. Denmark is highlighted as a particularly dynamic country that improved its innovation performance and ranking strongly.

Germany performs better outside the main innovation-capacity ranking. In key technologies, Germany remains in 7th place. In sustainability, Germany remains in 3rd place. The report therefore does not present Germany as uniformly weak; rather, it distinguishes a broader innovation-capacity problem from comparatively stronger positions in selected future-oriented technology and sustainability dimensions.

The policy diagnosis is broad and reform-oriented. The editorial and summary sections argue that Germany needs to reopen and strengthen its innovation system internationally, focus additional public R&D resources on areas that matter for long-term innovation capacity, help more medium-sized firms orient business models toward innovation, new technologies, and global commercialization, promote excellent science rather than broad undifferentiated funding, improve transfer, implement missions more purposefully, remove contradictions in funding policy, and provide resources. The report also stresses technological sovereignty, key technologies, dual-use research, and the role of scientific excellence and international talent.

The methodology section describes the Innovationsindikator as a composite-indicator system rather than a single raw statistic. Since the redesign described in the report, it covers three functional dimensions of innovation systems through separately calculated composite indicators. Indicator selection, normalization, and aggregation are explicit methodological steps. The report uses a benchmark/reference group of countries for normalization and notes that not all indicators are available up to the current edge; some values use estimates or nowcasting. For key technologies, Taiwan's missing data mean that the analysis is performed for 34 rather than 35 countries.

## Research question

How does Germany's innovation system compare internationally in innovation capacity, key technologies, and sustainability, and what policy tasks follow from Germany's current ranking and trend?

## Core argument or contribution

The report contributes a composite-indicator benchmarking diagnosis. Its core argument is that Germany's innovation capacity is slipping relative to leading and dynamic economies, even though Germany retains stronger positions in key technologies and sustainability. The policy implication is that Germany needs a more focused, excellent, transfer-oriented, and strategically coherent research and innovation policy.

## Methodology

- International benchmarking of 35 economies.
- Composite indicators for three separate dimensions: innovation capacity, key technologies, and sustainability.
- Indicator selection based on innovation-system functions and empirical-methodological considerations.
- Normalization of country values relative to a benchmark/reference group.
- Aggregation of individual indicators into dimension-specific composite indicators.
- Use of estimates/nowcasting where current-edge statistical data are incomplete.
- Separate key-technology analysis for 34 countries where Taiwan data are insufficient.

## Key findings

- Germany falls two places in innovation capacity and ranks 12th of 35 economies.
- Switzerland ranks first in innovation capacity; Singapore and Denmark are also leading countries.
- Denmark is highlighted as one of the most dynamic and innovative countries and ranks strongly in some key technologies.
- Germany remains 7th in the key-technology indicator.
- Germany remains 3rd in the sustainability indicator.
- China is described as the only large economy with very dynamic innovation-capacity development, though it ranks 25th overall in the innovation ranking.
- The report frames Germany's policy tasks around openness, focused R&D resources, SME innovation, scientific excellence, transfer, missions, removal of funding contradictions, technological sovereignty, and reliable resources.

## Limitations

- The report is a benchmarking and policy-diagnosis document, not a causal study.
- Composite indicators depend on indicator selection, normalization, aggregation, missing-data handling, and the chosen reference group.
- The report does not analyze individual German funding projects, recipients, TRL levels, or Foerderkatalog text.
- Rankings should be interpreted as comparative indicators, not as direct measures of the effectiveness of a specific policy program.
- The converted source text contains OCR artifacts; exact wording and figure values should be checked against the original PDF or website before quotation.
